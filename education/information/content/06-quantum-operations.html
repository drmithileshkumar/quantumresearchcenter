6.1 Physical Constructions

Let a qubit control an initially zero auxiliary via CNOT, then ignore the auxiliary. The joint evolution is reversible, yet the qubit loses its visible interference. Irreversibility can arise from limiting which systems we retain.

6.1.1 Ancillas, interaction, and discarding

For \(\ket\psi=a\ket0+b\ket1\), the interaction produces \(a\ket{00}+b\ket{11}\). Tracing the auxiliary out gives diag(|a|²,|b|²). The off-diagonal terms have vanished locally because the auxiliary keeps a distinguishing record. If we retain both systems and reverse CNOT, the initial pure qubit returns. Losing access to the auxiliary changes the available recovery.

6.1.2 Reversible and irreversible descriptions

The construction is: initialize an auxiliary state, apply a joint unitary, and discard some output systems. Resetting a qubit is an extreme example: swap it with a zero auxiliary and discard the old state. This is a valid operation on the retained system, but not an invertible unitary there. The information went into another system rather than being reversibly merged into one state.

Problem 6.1. At p=1/2, what does phase-flip noise do to plus?

Worked solution

Its off-diagonal entries vanish, producing I/2. At p=1 it instead produces the pure minus state; a known deterministic phase flip is reversible.

6.2 Quantum Channels

6.2.1 Operator-sum representations

For a pure zero auxiliary, define \(K_j=\bra j_EU\ket0_E\). Discarding the auxiliary gives the operator-sum rule \(\mathcal E(\rho)=\sum_jK_j\rho K_j^\dagger\). For complete dephasing, K0=|0〉〈0| and K1=|1〉〈1|. These operators describe an unobserved environmental branch; they are not necessarily outcomes available to the user.

6.2.2 Trace preservation and complete positivity

Trace preservation requires \(\sum_jK_j^\dagger K_j=I\). Positivity must remain true even when the input is entangled with an untouched reference; this is complete positivity. The auxiliary-unitary construction guarantees it. Ordinary matrix transposition preserves positivity of a single matrix but fails this stronger requirement: partial transposition of a Bell density matrix has an eigenvalue −1/2. It cannot be a physical channel on one half of every entangled input.

Problem 6.2. Verify trace preservation for amplitude damping.

Worked solution

\(K_0^\dagger K_0=\mathrm{diag}(1,1-\gamma)\) and \(K_1^\dagger K_1=\mathrm{diag}(0,\gamma)\), whose sum is I.

6.3 Noise and Composition

6.3.1 Simple qubit channels

A phase-flip channel is \((1-p)\rho+pZ\rho Z\); its off-diagonal entries are multiplied by 1−2p. An amplitude-damping channel has \(K_0=\ket0\bra0+\sqrt{1-\gamma}\ket1\bra1\), \(K_1=\sqrt\gamma\ket0\bra1\). On \(\rho=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\c^*&b\end{pmatrix}\), its output is \(\begin{pmatrix}a+\gamma b&\sqrt{1-\gamma}c\\\sqrt{1-\gamma}c^*&(1-\gamma)b\end{pmatrix}\). This explicitly moves excited population to zero while reducing coherence.

6.3.2 Channel composition and accessible information

Composing channels multiplies their branch operators: if the first has Kj and the second Ll, the combined operators are LlKj. A receiver cannot gain optimal distinguishability by applying the same channel to both candidate inputs: any later decision procedure could also be performed after that channel as part of an original-input measurement. This operational data-processing argument concerns optimized accessible decisions, not every arbitrarily chosen measurement statistic.

Problem 6.3. Does reversing the retained qubit alone undo complete dephasing?

Worked solution

No universal qubit-only inverse exists. Plus and minus both become I/2, so their distinction has been lost from the retained subsystem.

A measurement is an operation whose branch record is retained. We now describe both the outcome probabilities and the conditional state changes.
