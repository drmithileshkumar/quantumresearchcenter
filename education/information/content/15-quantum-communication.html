15.1 Communication Tasks

A channel sometimes transmits a qubit perfectly and sometimes replaces it with an orthogonal erasure flag. The receiver knows which uses were erased. This simple channel separates classical transmission, quantum transmission, and assisted transmission.

15.1.1 Classical and quantum messages

Classical transmission asks the receiver to identify a message label. Quantum transmission asks an unknown state, potentially entangled with a reference, to survive the encoding, channel, and decoding. A device that measures and reprepares basis labels may be a good classical channel while destroying quantum coherence. The reliability criterion must match the requested kind of information.

15.1.2 Assistance and resource models

Specify available assistance: pre-shared entanglement, one-way or two-way classical communication, and any other side channels. Teleportation shows that an ebit plus two classical bits can transmit a qubit, while dense coding changes classical capacity with entanglement assistance. These are different resource models, so their rates cannot be compared without their resource accounts.

Problem 15.1. Why does a known erasure differ from an unflagged bit flip?

Worked solution

The receiver knows the failed positions and can adapt decoding to that side information. The channel output alphabet includes a flag distinct from the qubit subspace.

15.2 Coding and Rates

15.2.1 Achievability and converse statements

A rate R is achievable if a sequence of n-use codes transmits about nR bits or qubits with error tending to zero. A converse excludes rates above the capacity under the same criterion. A finite demonstration with a small error does not establish either asymptotic claim. We assume finite-dimensional memoryless channels, meaning independent uses of the same channel map, unless stated otherwise.

15.2.2 Classical and entanglement-assisted capacities

For unassisted classical communication, define \(\chi^*(\mathcal N)\) as the maximum output Holevo information over input ensembles. General classical capacity is the regularized quantity \(C(\mathcal N)=\sup_n\chi^*(\mathcal N^{\otimes n})/n\), allowing entangled encodings and collective decoding. With unlimited prior entanglement, the classical capacity is the single-use maximum of \(I(R:B)\) after sending one half of a purification through the channel. These coding theorems are stated here, not derived by the examples; single-use simplification without assistance requires additional structure.

Problem 15.2. What happens at e=1/2?

Worked solution

Classical capacity is 1/2 bit per use, entanglement-assisted classical capacity is one bit per use, and unassisted quantum capacity is zero. The tasks and resources are different.

15.3 Quantum Transmission

15.3.1 Coherent information and quantum capacity

For input ρ purified by RA and output state ωRB, coherent information is \(I_c(\rho,\mathcal N)=S(B)_\omega-S(RB)_\omega\). Unassisted quantum capacity is \(Q(\mathcal N)=\sup_n n^{-1}\max_{\rho_n} I_c(\rho_n,\mathcal N^{\otimes n})\). The optimization includes correlations across inputs; one use need not attain the capacity. This is an asymptotic coding theorem. A large positive value for a particular input is useful evidence of an achievable rate, but a general converse needs the full theorem.

15.3.2 Regularization and finite-resource limitations

For the qubit erasure channel with erasure probability e, the exact capacities are C=1−e classical bits, entanglement-assisted classical capacity 2(1−e) bits, and unassisted Q=max(0,1−2e) qubits per use. These are established channel-specific theorems. With a maximally entangled reference, \(S(B)=h_2(e)+(1-e)\) and \(S(RB)=h_2(e)+e\), so coherent information is 1−2e. At e=1/4, these three capacities are 3/4,3/2,1/2. Two-way classical assistance changes the quantum rate; do not silently use it in the unassisted claim. Finite block length, decoding effort, and experimental noise remain practical constraints.

Problem 15.3. Why is a single-use coherent information not generally “the quantum capacity”?

Worked solution

Block encodings can use correlations across channel inputs and improve the per-use value. The regularized maximization is essential without a theorem proving additivity for the channel.

Communication rates describe reliability. The final chapter asks when transmitted data can also be secret from an adversary.
