4.1 Joint States

Alice and Bob each hold a two-state system. Four configuration labels are needed for the pair: 00,01,10,11, with Alice’s bit first. Joint amplitudes contain more possibilities than independently assigning each party a state.

4.1.1 Product configurations and amplitudes

If Alice has a|0〉+b|1〉 and Bob has c|0〉+d|1〉, their independently prepared joint amplitudes are ac,ad,bc,bd. This multiplication rule defines the tensor product, written \(\ket\psi_A\otimes\ket\phi_B\). A general joint state instead has four coefficients \(\alpha_{00},\alpha_{01},\alpha_{10},\alpha_{11}\) with squared magnitudes summing to one. Tensor notation abbreviates the paired configurations; it does not add a new physical system.

4.1.2 Tensor products and local operations

An operation acting only on Alice transforms her label in every term and leaves Bob’s label untouched. We write it U⊗I. For example H⊗I maps 00 to (00+10)/√2. CNOT is a joint operation: it maps |a,b〉 to |a,b XOR a〉. A circuit with H on Alice followed by CNOT from Alice to Bob prepares a Bell pair from 00.

Problem 4.1. Is (|00〉+|01〉)/√2 entangled?

Worked solution

No. It factors as |0〉⊗|+〉. Two nonzero joint amplitudes are not enough to establish entanglement.

4.2 Entanglement

4.2.1 Bell-state construction

The resulting state is \(\ket{\Phi^+}=(\ket{00}+\ket{11})/\sqrt2\). It has equal-amplitude equal-bit configurations and zero unequal-bit amplitudes. This is a coherent superposition: applying H to both systems leaves the same Bell state. Consequently both computational-basis and plus/minus-basis measurements give equal results.

4.2.2 Failure of product descriptions

Could it be a product? The coefficients would require ac=bd=1/√2 but ad=bc=0. Nonzero ac and bd force all four factors nonzero, contradicting the latter equations. More generally a two-qubit pure state is a product exactly when \(\alpha_{00}\alpha_{11}-\alpha_{01}\alpha_{10}=0\). Entanglement means a pure joint state fails this product description; mixed-state entanglement will require a broader definition.

Problem 4.2. What does Z on Alice do to the Bell pair?

Worked solution

It changes the sign of the 11 amplitude, producing (00−11)/√2. The computational-basis counts stay the same, but plus/minus correlations change.

4.3 Joint Measurement

4.3.1 Marginal and conditional probabilities

For the Bell pair, each local outcome is fair. Summing joint probabilities over Bob’s bit gives Alice’s marginal probability one half for either bit. Conditional on Alice observing zero, Bob’s computational-basis result is zero with certainty. Conditional predictions differ from unconditioned remote predictions; Bob needs Alice’s record to sort his trials into those groups.

4.3.2 Correlations and classical comparison

A classical mixture of 00 and 11 also gives perfectly correlated computational-basis results. In the plus/minus basis it gives all four pairs equally often, unlike the Bell pair. Thus correlations in one basis do not establish entanglement. The different joint phases and coherent amplitudes matter. We next need a common notation for the mixture and for a subsystem of the entangled state.

Problem 4.3. Can Bob tell from his local counts whether Alice measured her Bell half?

Worked solution

Not without a record selecting her outcome. His unconditioned local distribution remains fair. A general no-signalling argument follows in Chapter 9.

Density operators will combine uncertain preparations and reduced subsystem descriptions without confusing them with pure superpositions.
