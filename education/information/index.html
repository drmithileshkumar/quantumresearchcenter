Quantum Information

From distinguishable messages to quantum communication

What can we learn, preserve, transform, and communicate when information is carried by quantum systems? Develop states, measurements, entanglement, entropy, and communication through concrete tasks, returning to nonorthogonal states, a Bell pair, and a noisy channel.

Begin reading →Continue where you left off →

Prerequisites and approach

No prior quantum mechanics, information theory, or linear algebra course is assumed. Probability and mathematics are introduced in context. Quantum Computing is a complementary course, not a prerequisite.

16 of 16 chapters contain draft lessons with worked examples and solved problems. These are teaching manuscripts in development; advanced results are stated with their assumptions and sources where a full proof lies beyond the course.

Read in order on your first pass. Pause at each prediction, reproduce the worked calculations, and try the Problems before opening their solutions. Interactive figures compare phase, noise, and measurement choices; every figure also has a static explanation.

The recurring examples are two nonorthogonal states, a Bell pair, and a noisy qubit. Their purpose changes as the course develops: first predict an observation, then distinguish, preserve, transmit, or protect a state.

Landscape of quantum information

Quantum information branches into seven connected domains. Follow the branches from the central idea to a domain and then to its topics. Select any topic to open its explanation in the course.

Topic links by domain

  1. Information and states

    Describe an encoding and calculate what a receiver can observe.

    • Physical messages and distinguishability
    • Probability, classical entropy, and coding
    • Amplitudes, interference, and quantum states

    Supplies the language for every other domain.

  2. Composite systems and correlations

    Separate what belongs to a whole system from what is accessible to one part.

    • Tensor products and joint states
    • Entanglement and Bell pairs
    • Ensembles, density operators, and reduced states
    • Purification and preparation records

    Connects local predictions to shared resources.

  3. Dynamics and observation

    Model an interaction, the loss of a system, or the retention of a measurement record.

    • Ancillas, interaction, and discarding
    • Quantum channels and complete positivity
    • Noise and channel composition
    • Measurement effects, instruments, and disturbance

    Provides the allowed operations for every protocol.

  4. Learning and physical limits

    Choose a measurement for a decision, then establish what no device can accomplish.

    • Minimum-error and unambiguous discrimination
    • Accessible classical information
    • No-cloning and no-broadcasting
    • No-signalling and conditional predictions

    Sets the limits that communication and security must respect.

  5. Entanglement as a resource

    Account for shared pairs, transmitted systems, classical records, and conversion failures.

    • Teleportation, dense coding, and swapping
    • Local operations, Schmidt coefficients, and majorization
    • Concentration and dilution
    • Mixed-state entanglement and irreversibility

    Uses entropy to turn shared states into quantitative resource accounts.

  6. Entropy and compression

    Quantify uncertainty and correlation, then determine how much quantum memory a source needs.

    • Spectra and von Neumann entropy
    • Mutual information and conditional entropy
    • Relative entropy, data processing, and the Holevo bound
    • Fidelity, typical subspaces, and Schumacher compression

    Links the structure of a state to asymptotic storage and communication rates.

  7. Reliable and secure communication

    Preserve an unknown state through noise, compare transmission rates, and define a secrecy claim.

    • Quantum codes, syndromes, and recovery
    • Classical and entanglement-assisted capacities
    • Coherent information and quantum capacity
    • BB84, parameter estimation, and privacy amplification
    • Authentication and composable security

    Brings states, channels, entanglement, and entropy together under explicit resource and error criteria.

Connections across the landscape

Distinguishability → information bounds
The zero-versus-plus decision becomes a comparison between a measurement’s information and the Holevo bound.
Bell pairs → resource rates
The same pair used in teleportation becomes a unit for entanglement conversion and a resource in assisted communication.
Environmental records → protection
Noise can move information into an inaccessible system. Error correction protects the logical state; cryptography asks how much an adversary could learn.
