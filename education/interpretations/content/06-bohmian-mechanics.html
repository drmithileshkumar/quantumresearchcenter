What changes when particles have positions in addition to a wavefunction?

6.1 State and guiding law

6.1.1 Derive a velocity field from probability current

Bohmian mechanics supplements the wavefunction with an actual configuration Q(t). For N nonrelativistic spinless particles without vector potentials, the wavefunction obeys the usual Schrödinger equation on configuration space. The positions obey a guiding equation. Writing the law explicitly prevents the vague image of tiny classical balls following Newtonian trajectories.

\[\dot Q_k=\left.\frac{\hbar}{m_k}\operatorname{Im}\frac{\nabla_k\Psi}{\Psi}\right|_{q=Q},\qquad j_k=\frac{\hbar}{m_k}\operatorname{Im}(\Psi^*\nabla_k\Psi).\]

Away from nodes, the velocity is jₖ/|Ψ|². For a local polar form Ψ=R exp(iS/ℏ), it is ∇ₖS/mₖ. The actual configuration and the wavefunction jointly determine the motion. Spin and electromagnetic potentials require the appropriate generalized current; the displayed formula is deliberately restricted.

For a plane wave on a periodic line, Ψ∝exp(ikx), the velocity is ℏk/m. For a nondegenerate stationary state with a real spatial wavefunction, the spatial phase gradient is zero and the velocity vanishes away from nodes. This may surprise classical intuition, but it follows directly from the stated theory. One should assess the model by its full measurement predictions rather than replacing its law with a classical guess.

Worked check 6.1. For Ψ=R(x)e^{ikx} with positive real R, find v.

Reasoned solution

The logarithmic derivative is R′/R+ik. Only k contributes to its imaginary part, giving v=ℏk/m.

6.2 Quantum equilibrium

6.2.1 Why the Born density is preserved

Schrödinger evolution implies the continuity equation

\[\partial_t|\Psi|^2+\sum_k\nabla_k\cdot j_k=0.\]

An ensemble of configurations transported by the guiding velocity satisfies \(\partial_t\rho+\sum_k\nabla_k\cdot(\rho v_k)=0\). Because jₖ=|Ψ|²vₖ, the density |Ψ|² obeys the same transport equation. Subject to the usual regularity and boundary conditions, if ρ=|Ψ|² initially, this equality is preserved. This property is called equivariance.

Equivariance does not say that every arbitrary initial distribution magically becomes |Ψ|². Quantum equilibrium is an additional statistical condition; typicality accounts explain its role by considering suitable measures on configurations, while relaxation accounts investigate particular nonequilibrium dynamics. These arguments should not be conflated with the continuity-equation identity.

In equilibrium, ensembles reproduce the familiar position probabilities and, with a suitable analysis of measurement devices, the usual quantum statistics. The Born density has a precise dynamical role here: it is preserved by the combined wave and configuration dynamics. Its interpretation and justification are still distinct questions from preservation.

Worked check 6.2. Does equivariance show that ρ=2|Ψ|² is a distinct equilibrium density?

Reasoned solution

No. It is not normalized when Ψ is normalized. Equivariance concerns evolution of the normalized |Ψ|² density; arbitrary alternatives require their own initial conditions.

6.3 Measurement and nonlocality

6.3.1 Follow the actual configuration into a record

Let an apparatus interaction produce two macroscopically disjoint packets in configuration space, Ψ=αΨ₀+βΨ₁. The actual configuration lies in one packet's support. When the other packet has negligible support near that configuration, the guiding equation is effectively governed by the occupied packet. This is effective collapse. It is not a literal deletion of the other wave component.

If the packets later overlap coherently, the neglected component can affect motion again. The effective wavefunction is therefore justified by dynamical separation, not by an irrevocable declaration that unused terms are nonexistent. This will matter for hypothetical measurements on entire laboratories.

For entangled Ψ, the velocity of one particle generally depends on the whole configuration, including distant coordinates. Bohmian mechanics is nonlocal in this sense. In equilibrium it nevertheless reproduces operational no-signalling. A relativistic extension is a further task; the nonrelativistic equation here is not already a Lorentz-covariant completion.

Finally, an operator measurement can depend on the full experimental arrangement. The theory does not assign every Hermitian operator a context-independent intrinsic value. Definite positions therefore do not evade the contextuality theorem by pretending its impossible value table exists.

Worked check 6.3. Why can an empty packet matter after recombination?

Reasoned solution

The velocity depends on the full wavefunction and its gradient at the actual configuration. Overlapping packets contribute interference terms there even if only one was occupied while they were separated.
