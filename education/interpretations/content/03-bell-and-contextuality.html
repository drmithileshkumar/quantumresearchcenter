Which assumptions about reality are actually excluded by quantum correlations?

3.1 A precise locality condition

3.1.1 Derive the CHSH bound

Alice chooses x∈{0,1}, Bob chooses y∈{0,1}, and each records ±1. Introduce a candidate complete variable λ. Bell-local factorization says \(p(a,b|x,y,\lambda)=p(a|x,\lambda)p(b|y,\lambda)\). Measurement independence says the distribution μ(λ) does not depend on x or y. Neither assumption should disappear behind the word “realism.”

Let Aₓ(λ) and Bᵧ(λ) be the conditional mean outcomes, each between −1 and 1. For a fixed λ the CHSH expression is \(A_0(B_0+B_1)+A_1(B_0-B_1)\). Its magnitude is at most \(|B_0+B_1|+|B_0-B_1|\le2\). Averaging over a setting-independent μ preserves the bound:

\[|S|=|E_{00}+E_{01}+E_{10}-E_{11}|\le2.\]

Notice that we did not assume deterministic outcomes. They appear only as extreme cases of conditional means. The result constrains a combination of probabilistic and causal assumptions. Rejecting factorization is one response; rejecting measurement independence is another, with its own explanatory responsibilities. An inequality alone does not decide which account to adopt.

Worked check 3.1. For deterministic B₀=B₁=−1, what is the fixed-λ CHSH value?

Reasoned solution

It is −2A₀, with magnitude two; the A₁ contribution vanishes.

3.2 Quantum correlations without messages

3.2.1 Separate factorization from no-signalling

Now use \(|\Phi^+\rangle\), Alice's A₀=Z, A₁=X, and Bob's \(B_0=(Z+X)/\sqrt2\), \(B_1=(Z-X)/\sqrt2\). The nonzero correlations XX and ZZ both equal one; XZ and ZX vanish. Substituting gives E₀₀=E₀₁=E₁₀=1/√2 and E₁₁=−1/√2, hence S=2√2.

Does Bob send a message by choosing his setting? No. For a complete local POVM \(\{F_b^y\}\),

\[\sum_b p(a,b|x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\rho(E_a^x\otimes\sum_bF_b^y)\right]=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho(E_a^x\otimes I)].\]

Alice's marginal is independent of y. This is operational no-signalling. It does not imply the stronger factorization conditional on λ. Confusing the two makes it impossible to describe theories that reproduce Bell correlations without controllable faster-than-light communication.

The calculation is ideal. Experimental inference also needs appropriate treatment of detection, setting choice, timing, and statistical uncertainty. Here we derive the theoretical benchmark rather than claiming a particular experiment closes every loophole. Later chapters will use this benchmark to ask what a proposed ontology must reproduce.

Worked check 3.2. For white-noise visibility v, ρ=v|Φ+⟩⟨Φ+|+(1−v)I/4, when is CHSH violated with these settings?

Reasoned solution

All correlations scale by v, so S=2√2v. A violation requires v>1/√2.

3.3 Contextual values

3.3.1 An algebraic obstruction to a global value table

Contextuality asks a different question: could every observable have a fixed value independent of which compatible observables accompany it? For sharp measurements consider the array

\[\begin{pmatrix}X\otimes I&I\otimes X&X\otimes X\\I\otimes Z&Z\otimes I&Z\otimes Z\\X\otimes Z&Z\otimes X&Y\otimes Y\end{pmatrix}.\]

Each row and column consists of commuting operators. The three row products and first two column products are +I; the final column product is −I. Suppose each entry has a context-independent value ±1 and the value of a commuting product equals the product of values. Multiplying all six required constraints gives +1 on the left, because every entry occurs twice, but −1 on the right. No such assignment exists.

This is a state-independent contradiction for that value model in dimension four. It does not forbid all descriptions containing additional variables: those descriptions can be contextual. It also is not the complete modern definition of contextuality. Generalized noncontextuality concerns operationally equivalent preparations, transformations, and measurements and does not simply assume predetermined values for every effect.

The lesson is methodological. Identify the compatibility relations and the value rule before invoking a no-go theorem. An interpretation earns its place by explaining how it handles the assumptions, not by declaring the theorem irrelevant.

Worked check 3.3. Verify the last column product.

Reasoned solution

(X⊗X)(Z⊗Z)=−Y⊗Y because XZ=−iY. Multiplying by Y⊗Y gives −I.
