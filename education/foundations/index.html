Quantum Foundations

Quantum theory derived from physical principles

Quantum mechanics is usually taught from postulates: Hilbert space, the Born rule, unitary evolution, and the tensor product, each stated and then used. This course derives them. It begins with the least a testable theory must say, adds a few general principles one at a time, and proves that quantum theory is the only theory satisfying them. Each principle is paired with a foil, a consistent theory that violates it, so you can see what the principle rules out.

Prerequisites and approach

Comfort with vectors, matrices, and probability at the level of a first university course. No prior quantum mechanics is assumed; complex numbers, density matrices, and Hilbert space are constructed when the argument needs them. Quantum Information and Quantum Computing are complementary courses.

17 of 17 chapters contain draft lessons with worked examples and solved problems. These are teaching manuscripts in development; advanced results are stated with their assumptions and sources where a full proof lies beyond the course.

The course works in finite dimensions, where every step can be stated precisely. Chapter 16 explains what changes for position and momentum, which need infinite-dimensional spaces. Chapters 1–4 set up the space of possible theories, Chapters 5–9 introduce the principles and derive the formalism, Chapters 10–15 develop its consequences, and Chapters 16–17 treat infinite dimensions and review what was assumed.

Experiments such as Stern–Gerlach appear as illustrations and tests of principles, never as their justification: a finite table of outcomes is consistent with many theories. Where a derivation rests on a theorem whose full proof lies beyond the course, the text states it, says what it assumes, and cites the source.
