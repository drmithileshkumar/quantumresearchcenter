Quantum Error Correction

How to protect an unknown state and compute when operations can fail

You have stored an unknown qubit. An interaction with its environment may have changed it. How could you discover what went wrong without discovering the state you wanted to preserve? Starting with this question, we construct quantum codes, extract error syndromes, build logical operations, and account for the resources needed to make a computation reliable.

Prerequisites and approach

Complete Quantum Information Theory and the basic Quantum Computing course, or bring equivalent knowledge of states, channels, reference systems, Pauli operators, and elementary circuits. Binary linear algebra and classical codes are developed as needed. Hardware knowledge is helpful but not required.

10 of 10 chapters contain draft lessons with worked examples and solved problems. These are teaching manuscripts in development; advanced results are stated with their assumptions and sources where a full proof lies beyond the course.

The ten chapters form a cumulative argument. Chapters 1–2 establish what recovery must preserve. Chapters 3–5 construct and analyze codes. Chapters 6–7 turn syndromes into decoding decisions and local geometric codes. Chapters 8–10 confront faulty recovery, universal gates, and the cost of a reliable computation.

Work through the small cases before reading the general statement. Each chapter includes three worked checks and ten exercises with hints and reasoned solutions. Deep no-go and threshold theorems are stated with scope and references; numerical models are labeled as models. The course is an initial draft textbook, not a hardware performance forecast.

Conventions: qubits are ordered left to right; Pauli subscripts label physical positions; binary arithmetic is modulo two; stabilizer syndromes use 0 for eigenvalue +1 and 1 for −1. Code parameters are [[n,k,d]]. Gate conjugations use UPU†, and fidelity means squared fidelity. Unless specified otherwise, syndrome measurements and recovery are ideal through Chapter 7.

Reference reading: Daniel Gottesman, An Introduction to Quantum Error Correction and Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computation, and Barbara Terhal, Quantum Error Correction for Quantum Memories. The final chapter links a reproducible numerical verifier and proposes a resource-estimation project.
