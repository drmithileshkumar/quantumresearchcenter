Examination Philosophy

Examinations in this course probe vision and conceptual understanding rather than mechanical algebraic manipulation. While problem sets develop computational fluency, an oral or conceptual examination tests whether you understand why the formalism exists: why classical information erasure dissipates heat, why amplitudes cancel where probabilities cannot, why non-orthogonal states resist cloning, and how entanglement sets the boundary between classical and quantum algorithms.

The problems collected here are designed for a 30-minute viva-voce or oral defense. They require clear, concise explanations derived directly from first principles, testing physical intuition, geometric visualization on the state plane, and architectural insight.

Suggested 30-Minute Oral Exam Structure

For an examiner conducting a 30-minute oral evaluation on Chapters 1–6, select one or two core questions from each of the following thematic blocks:

Time Topic Focus Target Concepts
00–05 min Classical Computation Colliding trajectories, Landauer dissipation, cycle topologies, and uncomputation
05–10 min Probabilities & Interference \(\ell_1\) vs \(\ell_2\) conservation, path cancellation, and which-path detection
10–15 min State Geometry & Measurement Inner products as transition amplitudes, isometries, and incompatible bases
15–20 min Single-Qubit Mechanics Pauli operators, trace decompositions, Hadamard involution, and relative phase
20–25 min Composite Systems & Bounds Local vs entangling operations, Bell states, no-cloning, and no-signalling
25–30 min Parallelism & Readout Superposition evaluation, measurement collapse, and preimage superpositions
