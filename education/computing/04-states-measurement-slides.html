4.1

Vectors and Matrices

Our path calculations already have the shape of linear algebra. What do we gain by using it?

Read the chapter2 / 21

A state is a vector

List amplitudes in a fixed order: \(\ket0=(1,0)^T,\ \ket1=(0,1)^T\).

The bookkeeping rules are vector addition and scaling. Amplitudes \(3/5,4/5\) give probabilities \(9/25,16/25\).

§4.1.1 in the book3 / 21

What is the amplitude from one state to another?

  1. To a configuration: \(\braket j\psi=\psi_j\).
  2. Through every configuration: \(\braket\phi\psi=\sum_j\braket\phi j\,\braket j\psi\), the sum rule.
  3. Choose \(\braket\phi j=\phi_j^*\), so that \(\braket\psi\psi=\sum_j|\psi_j|^2=1\).
  4. \[\braket\phi\psi=\sum_j\phi_j^*\psi_j\]

The inner product is the transition amplitude.

Predict

Why the conjugate?

Without the conjugate, what would \((\ket0+i\ket1)/\sqrt2\) give as its amplitude to itself?

Answer

\(\tfrac12+\tfrac{i^2}2=0\), as if its total probability were zero. With the conjugate, \(\tfrac12+\tfrac12=1\).

§4.1.2 in the book5 / 21

How large can a transition amplitude be?

Split \(\ket\psi\) into a part along \(\ket\phi\) and a part perpendicular to it.

\(1=|\braket\phi\psi|^2+\|\psi_\perp\|^2\), so \(|\braket\phi\psi|^2\le1\). For real unit vectors, \(\braket\phi\psi=\cos\theta\).

§4.1.3 in the book6 / 21

A step is a matrix

Entry \(U_{ji}=\bra jU\ket i\): column \(i\) lists where \(i\) goes.

Multiplying the matrix by the column performs the path sums.

§4.1.4 in the book7 / 21

Which matrices keep total probability one?

  1. Each column has unit length (outgoing normalization).
  2. Different columns are orthogonal (the cross-sum condition).
  3. Together: \(\sum_jU_{ji}^*U_{jk}=\delta_{ik}\). Such a \(U\) is unitary.
  4. Two steps: \((VU)_{ki}=\sum_jV_{kj}U_{ji}\). The rightmost acts first.
§4.1.4 in the book8 / 21

Predict

How do we undo the square root of NOT?

Reversing the arrows of \(\sqrt X\) gives \(\sqrt X\) again, and \(\sqrt X\sqrt X=X\). What else must change?

Answer

Also conjugate the weights. The return paths give \(|a|^2+|b|^2=1\), and the crossing paths cancel.

§4.1.5 in the book9 / 21

The adjoint undoes every unitary step

Adjoint

\(\bra iU^\dagger\ket j=\bigl(\bra jU\ket i\bigr)^*\): reverse every arrow and conjugate its weight. For a unitary step, \(U^\dagger U=I\).

Unitary steps keep lengths and angles, \(\braket{U\chi}{U\psi}=\braket\chi\psi\). A shear also has an adjoint, but it does not undo the shear.

4.2

Postulates

Which parts of this calculation describe nature?

Read the chapter11 / 21

What is a state?

State postulate

A state of a finite quantum system is a unit vector of complex amplitudes. Vectors that differ only by a global phase represent the same state.

A qubit: \(\ket\psi=\cos\frac\theta2\ket0+e^{i\phi}\sin\frac\theta2\ket1\).

§4.2.1 in the book12 / 21

How does a closed system change?

Evolution postulate

The evolution of a closed system is unitary: \(\ket\psi\mapsto U\ket\psi\), with \(U^\dagger U=I\).

The mathematics forced unitarity for consistency; the postulate says nature behaves this way.

§4.2.1 in the book13 / 21

What does a measurement report?

Measurement postulate

Measuring \(\ket\psi=\sum_jv_j\ket j\) in the computational basis gives \(j\) with probability \(|v_j|^2\). The state becomes \(\ket j\).

With the projector \(P_j=\ketbra jj\): \(p(j)=\|P_j\ket\psi\|^2\).

§4.2.2 in the book14 / 21

Measurement keeps one component

Keep one perpendicular component, with probability equal to its squared length, and rescale it.

§4.2.2 in the book15 / 21

Predict

Must every measurement of |+⟩ be uncertain?

Measuring \(\ket+=(\ket0+\ket1)/\sqrt2\) in the computational basis gives two equally likely results. Is that true of every measurement?

Answer

No. A measurement in a basis containing \(\ket+\), the X measurement, gives \(+\) with certainty. The measurement chooses the question.

§4.2.2 in the book16 / 21

Looking between two Hadamard steps

Uninterrupted

  • \(\ket0\to\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\ket0+\ket1)\to\ket0\)
  • Certain return to 0.

Measured in the middle

  • The measurement leaves \(\ket0\) or \(\ket1\).
  • The second H gives each result half the time.
  • The update cannot be undone.

Unitary evolution carries every amplitude forward; measurement keeps one branch.

§4.2.2 in the book17 / 21

What does a pair of qubits need?

  1. Four complete configurations: \(c_{00}\ket{00}+c_{01}\ket{01}+c_{10}\ket{10}+c_{11}\ket{11}\).
  2. Independent preparations: the amplitude of \(01\) is \(ad\), by the product rule.
  3. Tensor product: \((a,b)^T\otimes(c,d)^T=(ac,ad,bc,bd)^T\).
  4. Composite-state postulate: every unit vector of this tensor-product space is allowed.
§4.2.3 in the book18 / 21

Predict

Is every joint state a product?

Can \((\ket{00}+\ket{11})/\sqrt2\) be written as \((a\ket0+b\ket1)\otimes(c\ket0+d\ket1)\)?

Answer

No. Nonzero \(ac\) and \(bd\) force \(ad\) and \(bc\) to be nonzero, but the state has no \(01\) or \(10\) term. It is entangled.

§4.2.3 in the book19 / 21

Four joint configurations, four amplitudes

Dimensions multiply: n qubits need one amplitude per bit string, 2ⁿ in all.

What we found

  1. States are vectors; steps are matrices; the inner product is the transition amplitude.
  2. Steps that keep total probability are unitary, and their adjoint undoes them.
  3. Postulates: unit vectors, unitary evolution, measurement by projection, tensor products.
  4. Measurement keeps one branch and cannot be undone.
  5. Joint states need not be products: entanglement.
