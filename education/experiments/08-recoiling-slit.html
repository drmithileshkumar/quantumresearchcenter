II · Paths, information, and time · Chapter 08

Einstein’s Recoiling Slit

Can a movable slit record a path without changing interference?

In this chapter
  1. The question and the apparatus
  2. Build a prediction
  3. What the evidence can establish
  4. Interactive experiment
  5. Problems and solutions

8.1 The question and the apparatus

Imagine mounting part of a double-slit apparatus on a support sensitive enough to register momentum recoil. The recoil might distinguish which path a particle took. Could one retain the usual interference pattern while also reading a perfect path record? This thought experiment asks whether the measuring apparatus can be excluded from the quantum description.

Treat the movable support as a quantum system. The two path alternatives leave it in states |dL⟩ and |dR⟩. If these states are identical, the support stores no path information. If they are orthogonal, a suitable measurement can distinguish them perfectly. The support's mass alone does not decide this; the distinguishability of its possible states does.

The visual draws two Gaussian pointer amplitudes separated in a dimensionless readout coordinate. Their overlap is the important variable. The coordinate is a toy model for an apparatus record, not a literal mechanical trajectory of Einstein's original slit arrangement.

8.2 Build a prediction

After the interaction, the joint state has the form (|L⟩|dL⟩+|R⟩|dR⟩)/√2. Ignoring the detector means tracing it out. The off-diagonal path term is multiplied by γ=⟨dR|dL⟩. For equal path weights and otherwise ideal alignment, the visibility is V=|γ|.

For real Gaussian amplitudes of equal width σ whose probability distributions have variance σ² and whose centres are separated by Δ, the overlap is \(\gamma=\exp[-\Delta^2/(8\sigma^2)]\). Our control is Δ/σ. Moving the pointer distributions apart reduces the cross term smoothly; there is no special threshold at which a human decides to look.

For two equally likely pure detector states, optimal distinguishability is \(D=\sqrt{1-|\gamma|^2}\). In this restricted model D²+V²=1. More general imperfections give inequalities and require careful definitions. Do not apply this equality indiscriminately to every noisy interferometer.

8.3 What the evidence can establish

The calculation locates complementarity in correlations with the apparatus. A recorded recoil can remove local interference even before anyone reads the record. An uncontrolled random phase can also reduce visibility, so observing low contrast alone does not prove that an accessible record exists.

One can try to measure the detector in a different basis and sort the particle events conditionally. That leads to quantum erasure in Chapter 10. It changes which joint correlations are displayed; it does not restore an unconditional pattern merely by refusing to read a classical record.

As a thought experiment, the setup is most useful when its assumptions are explicit: coherent preparation, a controlled apparatus interaction, known detector states, and an ideal recombiner. A real implementation needs an independently characterized coupling and detector response. The two-Gaussian visualization demonstrates the mathematics of distinguishability, not a historical reconstruction.

Explore the experiment

Separate the two pointer distributions. Compare overlap, visibility, and optimal distinguishability. Find a separation where neither the path information nor the interference is perfect.

A worked example

At Δ=2σ, calculate the visibility and distinguishability.

V=e⁻¹ᐟ²≈0.607. Then D=√(1−e⁻¹)≈0.795. Their squared values sum to one within rounding. Neither perfect path knowledge nor perfect visibility is available.

Try it yourself

Problem 8.1

What is the visibility when the two detector states coincide?

Hint

Their inner product is one.

Worked solution

V=1 and D=0 in the ideal balanced pure-state model.

Problem 8.2

Does leaving a perfect path detector unread restore fringes?

Hint

Trace over its orthogonal states.

Worked solution

No. Orthogonal path records make the local cross term zero whether or not a person reads them.

Problem 8.3

Why is D²+V²=1 not a universal laboratory calibration?

Hint

List the assumptions of the derivation.

Worked solution

It assumes balanced paths, pure detector states, optimal discrimination, and no other loss of coherence. Mixed states and technical imperfections require a more general treatment.

What to carry forward

Include the measuring apparatus in the state. The overlap of possible records controls interference in the reduced system.
