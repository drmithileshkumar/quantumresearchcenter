I · Observations and models · Chapter 04

Stern–Gerlach and Sequential Measurements

Why does inserting a different measurement change later outcomes?

In this chapter
  1. The question and the apparatus
  2. Build a prediction
  3. What the evidence can establish
  4. Interactive experiment
  5. Problems and solutions

4.1 The question and the apparatus

An inhomogeneous magnetic field couples a particle's magnetic moment to its motion. For a suitable spin-one-half beam, the outgoing wave packets separate into two channels. A slit can select the upper channel, preparing a definite result for a subsequent analyzer along the same axis. Repeating an ideal measurement along that axis then reproduces the selected result.

Rotate the second analyzer. A beam prepared as +z splits between the outcomes of an x-oriented analyzer. If we retain the +x output and then measure z, both signs reappear. A classical picture of a fixed little arrow revealed without disturbance cannot explain this sequence without adding a new account of measurement.

The historical silver-atom experiment involves atomic structure and translational motion. Our spin model isolates a two-dimensional degree of freedom. The branches in the animation represent separated output channels; their vertical spacing is schematic and is not a calculation of the magnetic force or flight time.

4.2 Build a prediction

Represent +z and −z by the column vectors (1,0) and (0,1). A spin-up state along an axis at polar angle θ in the xz plane is \(|+_\theta\rangle=\cos(\theta/2)|+z\rangle+\sin(\theta/2)|-z\rangle\). The probability of +θ after preparing +z is the squared overlap: cos²(θ/2).

The half-angle matters. At θ=90°, both outcomes have probability 1/2; at 180°, the +θ output is the −z state. Spin axes are physical directions in space, while the amplitudes inhabit a state space. They should not be identified with the orientation of an ordinary spinning ball.

The state after selecting +x is (|+z⟩+|−z⟩)/√2. A final z measurement therefore gives each sign with probability 1/2. Starting with a +z beam, selecting +x transmits half the trials, and then selecting +z transmits half of those. The net transmission is 1/4. Conditional and unconditional probabilities answer different questions.

4.3 What the evidence can establish

Sequential experiments test both outcome probabilities and the state changes associated with measurement. A detector that reports +x and then re-prepares +z would have the same immediate reporting statistics as an ideal x measurement but different later statistics. The observable alone does not specify the entire measurement procedure.

In a physical apparatus, imperfect separation, lost particles, and unwanted spin rotations affect the ideal sequence. Normalize counts to the relevant input population. If you retain one output, label it as postselection rather than implying that every input particle survives. Selection changes which trials reach the next stage.

The simulation starts with +z and rotates a single analyzer, displaying its two probabilities. Use the worked sequence to extend it to a chain. It illustrates the spin-one-half rule but does not claim that the measured particle had a definite value for every unperformed measurement.

Explore the experiment

Rotate the analyzer from 0° through 90° to 180°. Predict the two branch weights before moving the slider. Relate the changing weights to cos²(θ/2), not cos²θ.

A worked example

From 800 prepared +z particles, how many ideally survive a +x filter followed by a +z filter?

The first selection retains 800×1/2=400 on average. The second retains 400×1/2=200. Without the intermediate x filter, the ideal +z filter retains all 800.

Try it yourself

Problem 4.1

What is P(+θ) at θ=60° for +z input?

Hint

Use the half-angle.

Worked solution

cos²30°=3/4.

Problem 4.2

After selecting −x, what is P(+z)?

Hint

Write the −x state in the z basis.

Worked solution

The state is (|+z⟩−|−z⟩)/√2, so P(+z)=1/2. The minus sign affects x interference, not these z probabilities.

Problem 4.3

Why must the survival fraction be reported with the final outcome ratio?

Hint

Compare the original beam with the selected beam.

Worked solution

The selected ensemble may have a simple outcome ratio while most input particles were discarded. Without the survival fraction, the protocol cannot be reconstructed.

What to carry forward

A measurement is an operation with outcomes and a conditional state change. Its position in a sequence affects what happens next.
