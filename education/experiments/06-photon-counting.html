I · Observations and models · Chapter 06

Single Photons and Coincidences

What makes a source of light nonclassical?

In this chapter
  1. The question and the apparatus
  2. Build a prediction
  3. What the evidence can establish
  4. Interactive experiment
  5. Problems and solutions

6.1 The question and the apparatus

Direct a light source onto a balanced beam splitter and place a detector at each output. Record both the single-detector counts and pairs of detections separated by a delay τ. A coincidence histogram asks how likely another detection is near a detection already recorded. It measures correlations, not just average brightness.

A single-photon input ideally cannot trigger both outputs in the same trial. A weak laser is different: attenuation reduces its average photon number but leaves a nonzero multiphoton probability. A dim source is therefore not automatically a single-photon source. In heralded experiments, a partner detection identifies the trials in which a photon is expected.

The animation compares idealized antibunched, coherent, and bunched light. It shows a normalized correlation curve rather than laboratory time tags. Detector dead time, timing jitter, dark counts, and accidental coincidences are omitted from the curve but must be included when analysing an instrument.

6.2 Build a prediction

The normalized second-order correlation can be written \(g^{(2)}(0)=\langle n(n-1)\rangle/\langle n\rangle^2\) for a single mode with nonzero mean. For a one-photon number state, n(n−1)=0 and g⁽²⁾(0)=0. For ideal coherent light the value is one. A single-mode thermal field gives two.

The browser uses representative delay profiles: 1−exp(−|τ|/τc) for antibunching, one for coherent light, and 1+exp(−|τ|/τc) for bunching. These expose the dip or peak but are not universal source models. Real resonance fluorescence can have oscillations, and multimode thermal light need not reach two.

Why is a value below one interesting? A classical fluctuating nonnegative intensity satisfies ⟨I²⟩≥⟨I⟩². Under the corresponding photodetection model this implies g⁽²⁾(0)≥1. Sub-Poissonian or antibunched statistics violate that classical intensity description. The assumptions about detection and normalization remain part of the inference.

6.3 What the evidence can establish

Use two detectors because one detector's dead time can itself produce an apparent absence of nearby clicks. Estimate accidentals from appropriate displaced windows, report the coincidence-bin width, and characterize background. A very narrow uncorrected dip is not persuasive if the instrument suppresses close events by construction.

Antibunching is evidence for nonclassical light; it does not guarantee purity of polarization, perfect indistinguishability, or high collection efficiency. Those are different properties requiring different measurements. Chapter 16 uses two-photon interference to examine mode overlap.

The display supplies the curves by definition, so it cannot certify a source. Its purpose is to connect different questions—brightness, photon number, and timing correlations—to different measurements. A published source characterization should supply raw or suitably processed coincidence data and explain the corrections.

Explore the experiment

Switch among the three source models. Compare the zero-delay value with the long-delay baseline. Explain why lowering brightness alone does not change a coherent source into an antibunched one.

A worked example

A distribution contains one photon with probability 0.2 and vacuum otherwise. Calculate g⁽²⁾(0).

The mean photon number is 0.2. Since n is always zero or one, ⟨n(n−1)⟩=0 and g⁽²⁾(0)=0. Low delivery probability can coexist with ideal antibunching; this statistic does not measure efficiency.

Try it yourself

Problem 6.1

What is g⁽²⁾(0) for exactly two photons?

Hint

Compute the numerator and denominator separately.

Worked solution

2×1/2²=1/2. A value below one need not mean exactly one photon.

Problem 6.2

Why use a second detector?

Hint

Consider the response immediately after one detector fires.

Worked solution

A single detector may be unable to fire again during its dead time. Two independent outputs help distinguish source correlations from that artifact.

Problem 6.3

Can perfect antibunching establish polarization purity?

Hint

Identify which degree of freedom was measured.

Worked solution

No. Photon-number statistics do not determine polarization. Polarization tomography is a separate measurement.

What to carry forward

Brightness and nonclassicality are different measurements. Correlation claims need an explicit detection and normalization model.
