IV · Correlations and constraints · Chapter 16

Two-Photon Interference and Entanglement

What changes when two indistinguishable photons meet?

In this chapter
  1. The question and the apparatus
  2. Build a prediction
  3. What the evidence can establish
  4. Interactive experiment
  5. Problems and solutions

16.1 The question and the apparatus

Send one photon into each input of a balanced beam splitter and count coincidences between its outputs. If the photons overlap in their relevant modes, coincidence counts are suppressed: the Hong–Ou–Mandel effect. Delaying one photon makes them distinguishable and restores the baseline.

Overlap includes timing, spectrum, polarization, and spatial mode. Equal colour labels alone do not guarantee indistinguishability. A delay scan is useful because it changes one of those variables while leaving much of the apparatus fixed. Loss, multiphoton emissions, and detector response still affect the measured dip.

The visual shows two incoming wave-packet envelopes, their relative delay, and a coincidence curve. It depicts the cancellation of two-photon alternatives, not an attraction between photons. In the ideal output state, either both photons leave one port or both leave the other.

16.2 Build a prediction

With a consistent balanced splitter convention, input creation operators transform as a†→(c†+d†)/√2 and b†→(c†−d†)/√2. Their product becomes [(c†)²−(d†)²]/2. The c†d† terms cancel. Properly normalized, the output is (|2,0⟩−|0,2⟩)/√2.

For pure single-photon modes f and g, the coincidence probability is \(P_c=(1-|\langle f|g\rangle|^2)/2\). The teaching model chooses squared overlap exp(−δ²), where δ is dimensionless delay. This gives Pc=0 at perfect overlap and approaches 1/2 at large separation.

Entanglement is a related but distinct property. A polarization pair such as (|HH⟩+|VV⟩)/√2 has correlations that cannot be represented as a product state. Comparing several polarization bases distinguishes it from an ordinary mixture of HH and VV. A Hong–Ou–Mandel dip alone does not certify every desired polarization-entanglement property.

16.3 What the evidence can establish

Measure the large-delay baseline and the near-zero-delay coincidence rate using the same normalization. Report the source model and how multiphoton terms were handled. Visibility can be affected by postselection, so the protocol must say which trials enter the denominator.

The two-photon experiment tests indistinguishability through interference. Entanglement certification requires an appropriate witness, tomography, or a Bell test with its assumptions. Different optical experiments reveal different resources; a single impressive plot is not a complete characterization.

In this course the dip is an ideal prediction. The next chapters use entangled states to examine local models and contextual value assignments. Their conclusions require more than mode overlap: measurement settings, correlations, and assumptions about which values can be jointly assigned become central.

Explore the experiment

Slide one packet through the other. Compare the temporal overlap with the coincidence dip. Explain why the large-delay coincidence probability is 1/2 rather than one.

A worked example

If the squared mode overlap is 0.8, what coincidence probability is predicted?

Pc=(1−0.8)/2=0.1. Relative to the distinguishable baseline 0.5, the ideal dip visibility is (0.5−0.1)/0.5=0.8.

Try it yourself

Problem 16.1

Why is the coincidence baseline 1/2 for distinguishable inputs?

Hint

List the independent output alternatives.

Worked solution

Each input has two equally likely outputs. Two of the four alternatives put one photon at each output.

Problem 16.2

Does a pair that always gives matching H/V results have to be entangled?

Hint

Compare a mixture of HH and VV.

Worked solution

No. A 50/50 classical mixture gives the same H/V correlations. Measurements in other bases are needed.

Problem 16.3

What happens if the photons have orthogonal polarizations?

Hint

Consider their mode inner product.

Worked solution

Without erasing that distinguishability, the overlap is zero and the ideal coincidence probability is 1/2.

What to carry forward

Two-photon interference probes indistinguishability. Entanglement and nonlocal correlations require their own measurements and criteria.
