24.1 The question and the apparatus

Your task is to audit a phase scan from a two-output interferometer. The course provides a downloadable synthetic dataset generated by a seeded binomial model. It is explicitly not measured laboratory data. Its purpose is to make the analysis reproducible and to let you discover how a fitted parameter depends on sampling and assumptions.

The dataset contains sixteen equally spaced phase settings, 200 trials per setting, and the two output counts. A visibility control changes the generating model. Resetting returns to the initial model and seed. Export the CSV with its metadata before analysing it, so the exact input is preserved.

For an experimental extension, replace the synthetic file with a published dataset whose provenance and reuse conditions permit analysis. Record its source, apparatus, calibration, selection rules, and missing-event treatment. Do not quietly relabel the course file as an experiment or use its agreement with the generator as evidence for quantum theory.

24.2 Build a prediction

Fit P0(φ)=[1+V cosφ]/2 for this known zero-phase-offset toy model. With evenly spaced settings over a full turn, a simple moment estimate is \(\hat V=(2/M)\sum_{j=1}^M (2k_j/N-1)\cos\phi_j\). This follows from the average of cos²φ being 1/2 and the average of cosφ being zero.

For independent binomial counts with a common N, the variance of this estimate is \(16/(M^2N)\sum_j p_j(1-p_j)\cos^2\phi_j\). The display uses fitted probabilities for a plug-in standard error. It is an approximation, especially near boundaries; a likelihood analysis or parametric bootstrap is a useful extension.

A more realistic fit would include a phase offset, imbalance, and calibrated background. Each added parameter needs enough data to constrain it. Do not force an invalid estimate into [0,1] without reporting that the unconstrained model failed its physical bounds.

24.3 What the evidence can establish

Write a report with a question, apparatus/model diagram, data provenance, method, result with uncertainty, residual plot, alternative explanations, and limitations. Include the CSV and enough code or equations to reproduce the result. State whether the analysis was chosen before looking at the data.

Your central conclusion should concern an estimated interference contrast under an explicit binomial model. It should not claim nonclassical light, a Bell violation, or a test of observers: those require different preparations and measurements. The ability to say what a dataset does not establish is an experimental skill.

Completion criteria: reproduce the plotted counts; derive or implement the estimator; assess whether residuals show a phase-dependent pattern; compare the estimate with the generating value without hiding discrepancies; and explain how your inference would change with loss, phase drift, or correlated trials. For a real dataset, replace knowledge of the generator with independent calibration and a documented model comparison.

Explore the experiment

Generate a scan, export it, and reproduce the visibility estimate. Change the generating visibility and see how the estimated uncertainty changes. Compare the synthetic samples with the fitted curve rather than assuming exact agreement.

Try it yourself

Problem 24.1

Why include both output counts instead of only their difference?

Hint

Consider normalization and possible missing trials.

Worked solution

Both counts preserve the number of detected trials and allow the denominator to be checked. An experimental file should additionally report emitted or heralded trials and losses when available.

Problem 24.2

What does a sinusoidal residual after fitting suggest?

Hint

Think of a missing parameter.

Worked solution

A phase offset or an additional harmonic may be missing from the model. Calibration drift is another possibility; inspect the collection order and apparatus records.

Problem 24.3

What should change when replacing the synthetic scan with a real one?

Hint

List information the generator gave you for free.

Worked solution

Document provenance, calibration, timing, efficiency, loss, background, and selection. Check independence and model adequacy instead of assuming the binomial generator describes the apparatus.

What to carry forward

A reproducible result includes its data, model, uncertainty, and limits. The capstone completes the course by making those commitments inspectable.
