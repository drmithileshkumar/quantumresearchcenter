9.1 Selecting path readout or recombination after entry

9.1.1 Inserting or omitting the second beam splitter on the fly

In Wheeler’s delayed-choice thought experiment, a single-photon wave packet enters a Mach–Zehnder interferometer through the first beam splitter \(\mathrm{BS}_1\). Only after the pulse has passed \(\mathrm{BS}_1\) and entered the two separated arms does a fast electro-optic switch decide whether to leave the second beam splitter \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) in place (the closed configuration) or remove it (the open configuration). When \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) is present, the detector counts vary sinusoidally with the relative arm phase \(\phi\); when \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) is absent, each detector \(D_0\) and \(D_1\) monitors a single arm directly and clicks with probability \(1/2\) independent of \(\phi\).

9.1.2 Causal ordering of preparation, propagation, and measurement choice

Why did Wheeler propose delaying the choice until after \(\mathrm{BS}_1\)? A naive classical picture suggests that at \(\mathrm{BS}_1\) each photon must already adopt either a localized single-arm trajectory (to explain why only one detector clicks in the open configuration) or a two-arm wave state (to explain phase-dependent interference in the closed configuration). Delaying the choice between open and closed configurations until after \(\mathrm{BS}_1\) refutes that classical either-or pre-commitment—without requiring any backward-in-time influence from \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) to \(\mathrm{BS}_1\).

9.2 Incompatible observables on a single two-path state

9.2.1 Evaluating open-port and closed-port operators on the same state

In standard quantum mechanics, the state inside the interferometer after \(\mathrm{BS}_1\) and the phase shifter is the same pure superposition in every run, regardless of what the switch at \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) will do later:

\[\lvert\psi(\phi)\rangle = \frac{\lvert 0\rangle + e^{i\phi}\lvert 1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}.\]

The late switch simply chooses which unitary operator precedes the detectors: the identity \(I\) in the open configuration, or the Hadamard recombination \(H\) in the closed configuration. Under \(I\), the outcome probabilities are \(P_0=|\langle 0\vert\psi(\phi)\rangle|^2=1/2\) and \(P_1=|\langle 1\vert\psi(\phi)\rangle|^2=1/2\); under \(H\), they are \(P_0(\phi)=(1+\cos\phi)/2\) and \(P_1(\phi)=(1-\cos\phi)/2\).

9.2.2 Wave-like and particle-like statistics as basis choices

Notice that the open configuration measures the Pauli-\(Z\) observable (\(\lvert 0\rangle,\lvert 1\rangle\) arm basis) on \(\lvert\psi(\phi)\rangle\), whereas the closed configuration measures the Pauli-\(X\) observable (\(\lvert\pm\rangle=(\lvert 0\rangle\pm\lvert 1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}\) superposition basis). A single quantum state \(\lvert\psi(\phi)\rangle\) naturally yields \(50/50\) random outcomes in the \(Z\) basis and phase-dependent deterministic outcomes (\(\phi=0\) or \(\pi\)) in the \(X\) basis; no retroactive change to the state at \(\mathrm{BS}_1\) ever occurs.

9.2.3 Phase sensitivity without rewriting past records

Because no measurement record is formed at \(\mathrm{BS}_1\), switching \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) in or out never overwrites an existing classical record. Instead, it selects which of two noncommuting observables (\(Z\) or \(X\)) is finally registered at \(D_0\) and \(D_1\).

9.3 Spacelike switching and experimental timing audits

9.3.1 Relativistic separation in laboratory delayed-choice tests

In laboratory realizations such as Jacques et al. (\(2007\)), single photons from a nitrogen-vacancy color center traverse a \(48\text{-m}\) polarization interferometer while a fast electro-optic modulator driven by a physical random-number generator selects the open or closed measurement at spacelike separation from the photon’s entry at \(\mathrm{BS}_1\). Spacelike separation ensures that no subluminal or luminal signal from the switch could inform the photon at \(\mathrm{BS}_1\) which configuration will be chosen.

9.3.2 Separating empirical probabilities from interpretive histories

Different interpretations narrate the interior of the interferometer differently—Copenhagen refuses to assign a trajectory prior to detection, whereas Bohmian mechanics assigns a trajectory guided by a two-arm pilot wave that recombines at \(\mathrm{BS}_2\)—yet all valid interpretations predict the exact same detector probabilities \(P_0(\phi)\) and \(P_1(\phi)\).

9.3.3 Comparing full phase scans across open and closed modes

Comparing the open and closed configurations requires scanning the phase \(\phi\) across a full turn rather than inspecting a single phase point where the two curves happen to cross.

Explore the experiment

Set \(\phi=0^\circ\) and toggle between the closed configuration (\(\mathrm{BS}_2\) present, giving \(P_0=1,P_1=0\)) and the open configuration (\(\mathrm{BS}_2\) absent, giving \(P_0=1/2,P_1=1/2\)). Then sweep \(\phi\) across \([0^\circ,360^\circ]\) in both modes.

A worked example

At phase \(\phi=90^\circ\) (\(\pi/2\)), both the open configuration (\(\mathrm{BS}_2\) absent) and the closed configuration (\(\mathrm{BS}_2\) present) predict \(P_0=1/2\) and \(P_1=1/2\). Does this mean the two configurations perform the same measurement?

No. At \(\phi=90^\circ\), the internal state is \(\lvert +i\rangle=(\lvert 0\rangle+i\lvert 1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}\), which happens to lie on the equator at equal angle from both the \(Z\)-basis states \(\{\lvert 0\rangle,\lvert 1\rangle\}\) (measured in the open configuration) and the \(X\)-basis states \(\{\lvert +\rangle,\lvert -\rangle\}\) (measured in the closed configuration). Shifting the phase to \(\phi=0^\circ\) immediately distinguishes the two measurements: the open configuration still gives \(P_0=P_1=1/2\), whereas the closed configuration gives \(P_0=1\) and \(P_1=0\).

Try it yourself

Problem 9.1

In the two-dimensional arm basis \(\{\lvert 0\rangle,\lvert 1\rangle\}\), what \(2\times 2\) unitary matrix replaces the Hadamard matrix \(H\) at the recombination stage when \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) is removed?

Hint

Removing \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) routes arm \(0\) directly to \(D_0\) and arm \(1\) directly to \(D_1\).

Worked solution

The \(2\times 2\) identity matrix \(I=\mathrm{diag}(1,1)\) acts on \(\lvert\psi(\phi)\rangle\), leaving the path amplitudes \(1/\sqrt{2}\) and \(e^{i\phi}/\sqrt{2}\) unmixed so that \(P_0=P_1=1/2\).

Problem 9.2

When \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) is present and detector \(D_0\) clicks at \(\phi=0^\circ\), does that click reveal which arm (\(\lvert 0\rangle\) or \(\lvert 1\rangle\)) the photon took inside the interferometer?

Hint

Identify which basis state \(D_0\) projects onto after \(H\).

Worked solution

No. With \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) present, a click at \(D_0\) projects onto the symmetric superposition \(\lvert +\rangle=H\lvert 0\rangle=(\lvert 0\rangle+\lvert 1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}\), which contains equal contributions from both arms.

Problem 9.3

What experimental timing condition is required to ensure that the choice to insert or remove \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) is relativistically separated from the photon’s entry at \(\mathrm{BS}_1\)?

Hint

Compare the spatial separation \(\Delta L\) between \(\mathrm{BS}_1\) and the switch with the light-travel time \(c\,\Delta t\).

Worked solution

The random switching event at distance \(\Delta L\) from \(\mathrm{BS}_1\) must occur within a time window \(\Delta t\) around the photon’s passage through \(\mathrm{BS}_1\) satisfying \(|\Delta t|<\Delta L/c\), together with verified single-photon \(g^{(2)}(0)\ll 1\) and physical random-bit generation.

What to carry forward

Wheeler’s delayed-choice experiment chooses at the last moment whether to measure the path observable \(Z\) or the interference observable \(X\) on the same two-arm superposition \(\lvert\psi(\phi)\rangle\). No retrocausal rewriting of the past is needed or implied.
