17.1 The question and the apparatus

The EPR argument asks whether quantum mechanics completely describes physical reality. Perfect correlations appear to let one predict a distant quantity without acting on the distant system. Bohm's spin version makes this accessible with two spin-one-half particles in a singlet. Equal-axis measurements always give opposite results, while either local record alone is random.

Perfect anticorrelation is not sufficient to rule out a classical account: a source could send opposite prewritten instructions. Bell's insight is to compare several independently chosen measurement settings and constrain the entire family of correlations predicted by a local model. Each station records its setting and a result ±1.

The visual uses singlet spins measured along axes in one plane. Alice's axes are 0° and 90°. Bob's axes are θ and −θ. This is a spin-angle convention; photon polarization correlations involve doubled physical analyzer angles. Keeping the convention explicit avoids a common factor-of-two error.

17.2 Build a prediction

A local model with shared variable λ factorizes the conditional probabilities as P(a,b|x,y,λ)=P(a|x,λ)P(b|y,λ). Assume the setting distribution is independent of λ. Deterministic assignments suffice for deriving the bound because stochastic assignments are mixtures of them.

For predetermined ±1 values A0,A1,B0,B1, the expression C=A0(B0+B1)+A1(B0−B1) is always ±2: one bracket vanishes and the other is ±2. Averaging yields \(|S|=|E_{00}+E_{01}+E_{10}-E_{11}|\le2\). This is the CHSH inequality.

Quantum singlet correlations obey E(a,b)=−cos(a−b). With the axes in the visual, S=−2(cosθ+sinθ), reaching magnitude 2√2 at θ=45°. The local marginals remain 1/2, so stronger correlations do not enable a controllable message from one station to the other.

17.3 What the evidence can establish

A physical Bell test must address detection and locality loopholes and account for the setting process and statistical analysis. Throwing away missing detections can invalidate the simple local bound unless justified by an additional sampling assumption. A finite estimate slightly above two needs a statistical analysis; the curve in the simulator is an exact model expectation.

A violation rules out the stated package of local factorization, setting independence, and the experimental assumptions. It is not a proof that communication can exceed light speed, that every notion of reality is impossible, or that one interpretation is uniquely correct. Different interpretations locate the failed assumptions differently.

The lesson separates EPR's motivation, the Bell inequality, and its experimental test. The thought argument alone is not a measured violation. The browser implements the quantum correlation function, so seeing it cross the bound checks the model and geometry, not nature.

Explore the experiment

Sweep Bob’s angle and find the largest |S|. Inspect the four correlation terms separately. Check that the local outcome probabilities stay balanced throughout.

A worked example

At θ=45°, calculate the four correlations and S.

E00=E01=E10=−1/√2 and E11=+1/√2. Thus S=−4/√2=−2√2, whose magnitude exceeds the local bound two.

Try it yourself

Problem 17.1

Why can perfect anticorrelation at one setting have a local explanation?

Hint

Construct opposite instructions.

Worked solution

The source can send A=λ and B=−λ for a random λ=±1. Additional settings are needed to constrain such models.

Problem 17.2

What is |S| at θ=0°?

Hint

Substitute into the derived expression.

Worked solution

|S|=2, at the local bound. Entanglement alone does not make every setting choice violate CHSH.

Problem 17.3

Can Alice choose her individual singlet outcome to signal?

Hint

Distinguish a setting choice from a random result.

Worked solution

No. Her outcome is uncontrollable and Bob’s marginal remains balanced. Correlations are revealed by comparing records.

What to carry forward

Bell tests constrain a precise assumption package using correlations across settings. Keep the local bound, quantum prediction, and statistical estimate distinct.
