23.1 The question and the apparatus

Prepare a superposition, let an unknown frequency offset act for time t, and convert the accumulated phase into a population. Repeat and infer the offset. This is the basic logic behind Ramsey-based frequency sensing. Atomic clocks add a feedback loop that adjusts an oscillator using the atomic response.

Sensitivity depends on the slope of the response and the measurement noise. A high-contrast signal at its maximum can be locally insensitive because the slope there is zero. A phase bias moves the operating point to the steep part of a fringe. The apparatus must also resolve which fringe is being measured.

The explorer varies the interrogation time in units of a chosen coherence time. It displays the decreasing contrast and the predicted frequency uncertainty for a fixed number of shots. Longer interrogation is beneficial only until the loss of contrast outweighs the additional phase accumulation.

23.2 Build a prediction

Use P0=[1+V cos(δωt+φ)]/2 and choose the quadrature point where the cosine is zero. There |∂P0/∂δω|=Vt/2. For N independent binary outcomes, the standard error of the measured probability is 1/(2√N). Linear error propagation gives \(\delta\omega\simeq1/(Vt\sqrt N)\).

For V=e−t/T₂ and fixed N, minimizing the predicted uncertainty gives t=T2. If instead the total free-evolution time is fixed and overhead is negligible, N≈Ttotal/t and the optimum becomes t=T2/2. The resource constraint changes the answer. Real overhead, correlated noise, and phase ambiguity modify both estimates.

Entanglement can change sensitivity scaling in ideal models, but noise and preparation cost must be counted. A claim of quantum advantage requires comparison at matched resources, not merely comparing an entangled sensor with an arbitrarily weak baseline. The present display uses independent probes and makes no entanglement advantage claim.

23.3 What the evidence can establish

Calibrate the relation between field and frequency, characterize dead time and drift, and report how uncertainty was estimated. Repeated measurements in a noisy laboratory may be correlated, invalidating a naïve √N improvement. Clock stability over time and the uncertainty of an absolute frequency are related but distinct quantities.

The displayed optimum is conditional on exponential contrast loss and a fixed-shot budget. It is not an equipment recommendation or a universal optimal sensing time. Use the alternate resource calculation as an example of how experimental design changes when costs are accounted for.

A useful extension is to compare the model with a supplied measured Ramsey scan or clock dataset, keeping calibration and uncertainty metadata. Until such measurements are provided, generated curves and CSV files in this course remain labelled synthetic.

Explore the experiment

Sweep t/T2 and locate the minimum fixed-shot uncertainty. Compare it with the decreasing contrast and the increasing phase-accumulation time. Explain why t=0 is uninformative.

A worked example

At t=T2 with N=100, what is δω in units of 1/T2?

V=e⁻¹ and √N=10, so δωT2=e/10≈0.272. This is a local error-propagation estimate near quadrature, not a global confidence interval over many phase wraps.

Try it yourself

Problem 23.1

How does quadrupling N change the uncertainty under independent noise?

Hint

Use the square root dependence.

Worked solution

It halves the uncertainty.

Problem 23.2

Why is the top of a fringe a poor local operating point?

Hint

Differentiate the probability.

Worked solution

The first derivative with respect to frequency vanishes there, so a small offset produces only a second-order change.

Problem 23.3

Why do fixed-shot and fixed-total-time optimizations differ?

Hint

Count how many shots fit as t changes.

Worked solution

With fixed total time, longer shots reduce N. That extra resource cost shifts the optimum from T2 to T2/2 in the ideal overhead-free model.

What to carry forward

Sensitivity is a combination of response slope, noise, and resources. Quote an optimum together with the assumptions that make it optimal.
