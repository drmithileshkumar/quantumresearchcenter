19.1 The question and the apparatus

Alice holds an unknown qubit and one half of a Bell pair; Bob holds the other half. Alice performs an entangling operation and measures her two qubits. She sends two classical bits to Bob, who applies a correction. The protocol transfers the input state to Bob's system while consuming the shared entanglement.

The transferred object is a state, not matter. Alice does not learn its arbitrary amplitudes from one copy and does not retain an independent copy. The two-bit message is necessary: without it, Bob cannot know which correction to apply and cannot recover a controllable signal.

The visual lets you choose a real input state and one of the four measurement branches. It displays Bob's state before and after the correction. A branch selector examines conditional predictions; it is not a way for Alice to choose her random measurement outcomes.

19.2 Build a prediction

Write |ψ⟩=α|0⟩+β|1⟩, with |α|²+|β|²=1. After Alice's CNOT and Hadamard, the joint state can be grouped as \(\tfrac12\sum_{a,b=0}^1|ab\rangle X^bZ^a|\psi\rangle\). Each branch has probability 1/4, independent of the input. Bob applies ZᵃXᵇ, the inverse up to an irrelevant global phase.

Before the message arrives, Bob averages over the four branches: ρB=¼ΣabXᵇZᵃρZᵃXᵇ=I/2. This explains the absence of faster-than-light transmission within the protocol. The dependence on |ψ⟩ appears in correlations with Alice's classical record.

Entanglement swapping replaces the unknown input with one member of another Bell pair. A Bell measurement on the two middle systems prepares a conditional Bell state of the outer systems. Its label depends on the middle result. This does not mean the outer observers can identify that conditional state from their unsorted local records.

19.3 What the evidence can establish

A laboratory claim must specify the ensemble of input states, success probability, heralding, and the definition of fidelity. The familiar average fidelity benchmark 2/3 applies to deterministic measure-and-prepare strategies for a uniformly distributed unknown pure qubit. It is not a universal benchmark for arbitrary selected inputs or postselected protocols.

Linear-optical Bell measurements can have limited success probabilities, so a high conditional fidelity must be reported with the successful-event rate. Check that the state was not simply known in advance to the receiving apparatus. Input selection and verification measurements are part of the experiment.

The explorer is an exact ideal circuit identity with real amplitudes. General complex inputs obey the same identity. Its branch-by-branch agreement verifies the algebra but does not measure transmission fidelity, loss tolerance, or the quality of an actual entanglement source.

Explore the experiment

Choose an input angle, cycle through all four messages, and apply the correction. Compare the corrected state with the input. Then average the uncorrected branches conceptually to recover I/2.

A worked example

If Alice records a=1,b=0, what state does Bob have and how does he correct it?

Bob has Z|ψ⟩=α|0⟩−β|1⟩. Applying Z again restores |ψ⟩ because Z²=I. The message identifies the correction without revealing α or β.

Try it yourself

Problem 19.1

Why are the two classical bits insufficient to describe an arbitrary qubit by themselves?

Hint

Compare the continuous input parameters with four messages.

Worked solution

The bits label a correction to a pre-existing entangled resource. They are not a classical specification of the continuous amplitudes.

Problem 19.2

What state does Bob assign without the message?

Hint

Average all four Pauli branches.

Worked solution

I/2 for every input, so no input information can be recovered locally before receiving the message.

Problem 19.3

Does postselected fidelity alone characterize a useful experiment?

Hint

Consider an almost never successful device.

Worked solution

No. Report success probability, input ensemble, loss, and the applicable classical comparison alongside conditional fidelity.

What to carry forward

Teleportation combines entanglement, a local measurement, and classical communication. Conditional state transfer does not bypass communication or clone the input.
