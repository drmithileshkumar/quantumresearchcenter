3.1 The question and the apparatus

Send electrons through two narrow apertures towards a position-sensitive screen. Lower the source rate so simultaneous particles are rare. Each recorded event is localized, yet the accumulated distribution can contain alternating maxima and minima. The central observation concerns the relation between individual events and the distribution of many identically prepared trials.

A slit plate sets the geometry; propagation maps transverse momentum to screen position; the detector bins arrivals. Opening only one slit changes the preparation and therefore the expected pattern. Adding a device that records the path changes the joint system. These are different experiments, not merely different stories about an unchanged dataset.

The display builds a screen histogram from a model distribution. Moving dots before the screen are symbols for trials, not measured electron trajectories. We draw a broad diffraction envelope and a controllable interference contrast. The model uses a Gaussian envelope for clarity rather than a literal rectangular-aperture diffraction profile.

3.2 Build a prediction

When two alternatives lead to the same final outcome without a distinguishing record, add their probability amplitudes before squaring: \(P(x)=|\psi_L(x)+\psi_R(x)|^2\). Expanding gives the two individual intensities plus \(2\operatorname{Re}(\psi_L^*\psi_R)\), the interference term. Adding probabilities alone omits that term.

For equal amplitudes with a phase difference δ, the intensity is proportional to 1+cosδ. Opposite phases cancel; equal phases reinforce. In a far-field two-slit geometry, δ is approximately 2πdx/(λL), where d is slit separation, L is distance to the screen, and x is transverse position. Adjacent fringes are separated by Δx≈λL/d.

For matter waves, λ=h/p. Increasing particle momentum therefore narrows the fringe spacing at fixed geometry. The simulation holds the spacing fixed and varies visibility V in a normalized model proportional to an envelope times [1+V cosδ]. V=0 removes interference but does not remove diffraction.

3.3 What the evidence can establish

Single events rule out interpreting the screen solely as a continuous deposited intensity. Their distribution also rules out the particular model that assigns independent slit probabilities and then adds them without interference. It does not rule out every possible trajectory-based theory: such theories can include additional dynamics that reproduce interference.

Compare both-slit and single-slit runs under stable source conditions. Account for detector nonuniformity, finite slit widths, coherence length, and vibration. If a path detector is added, check whether the contrast loss is explained by entanglement, random phase shifts, or a mixture of mechanisms. A washed-out pattern alone does not identify the mechanism.

The bin counts fluctuate even when the underlying distribution is fixed. The smooth curve is the model expectation and the dots are synthetic samples. Their agreement illustrates sampling, not a new experimental confirmation. Later chapters examine how a path record controls the interference term without requiring a conscious observer.

Explore the experiment

Keep the visibility at one and watch individual arrivals accumulate. Reset at V=0 and compare the smooth envelope. Explain why a single detection never displays a fringe.

A worked example

Electrons with wavelength 0.05 nm pass slits 1 μm apart, with a screen 1 m away. Estimate the fringe spacing.

Δx=λL/d=(5×10⁻¹¹ m)(1 m)/(10⁻⁶ m)=5×10⁻⁵ m=50 μm. This estimate assumes far-field geometry and sufficient transverse coherence.

Try it yourself

Problem 3.1

Two amplitudes are 1/2 and −1/2. What is their combined probability at this outcome?

Hint

Add amplitudes first.

Worked solution

The sum is zero, so the probability is zero. Adding the individual probabilities would incorrectly give 1/2.

Problem 3.2

What happens to fringe spacing if momentum doubles?

Hint

Use λ=h/p.

Worked solution

The wavelength halves, so Δx halves at fixed slit spacing and screen distance.

Problem 3.3

Does losing visible fringes prove a path was observed?

Hint

Think of other ways to average the phase.

Worked solution

No. Uncontrolled phase drift can also wash out fringes. Additional measurements are needed to identify the physical mechanism.

What to carry forward

Interference is a statement about outcome probabilities under specified alternatives. A diagram of alternatives should not be mistaken for a measured path.
