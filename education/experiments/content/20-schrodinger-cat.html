20.1 Amplifying a microscopic superposition into pointer records

20.1.1 Coupling a two-level trigger to a macroscopic apparatus pointer

In his 1935 essay, Erwin Schrödinger posed a sharp question about how the linear unitary dynamics of quantum mechanics connects to definite macroscopic records: if a microscopic system \(S\) prepared in a superposition \(\alpha|0\rangle_S + \beta|1\rangle_S\) interacts with a macroscopic detector or pointer \(M\) initialized in a ready state \(|R_0\rangle_M\), linearity of the time-evolution operator \(U_{SM}\) forces the composite state into an entangled superposition of distinct pointer configurations:

\[U_{SM}\Big(\big(\alpha|0\rangle_S + \beta|1\rangle_S\big)\otimes|R_0\rangle_M\Big) = \alpha|0\rangle_S|R_0\rangle_M + \beta|1\rangle_S|R_1\rangle_M, \qquad \langle R_0|R_1\rangle = 0.\]

20.1.2 Von Neumann premeasurement and linearity of pointer entanglement

In John von Neumann’s measurement chain, this unitary stage \(U_{SM}\) is called a premeasurement: it correlates the system eigenbasis \(\{|0\rangle_S, |1\rangle_S\}\) with orthogonal pointer states \(\{|R_0\rangle_M, |R_1\rangle_M\}\) without breaking unitarity. In modern laboratory realizations—such as a Rydberg atom coupled dispersively to a microwave cavity field or a superconducting qubit coupled to a resonator or mechanical mode—the pointer states \(|R_0\rangle_M\) and \(|R_1\rangle_M\) are coherent states \(|\pm\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}\rangle\) separated in phase space by distance \(D = 2|\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}|\).

20.1.3 Pointer branch weights versus coherent off-diagonal terms

Written in the joint two-branch subspace spanned by \(|\Phi_0\rangle = |0, R_0\rangle\) and \(|\Phi_1\rangle = |1, R_1\rangle\), the pure premeasurement state has density matrix

\[\rho_{\mathrm{pure}} = \begin{pmatrix} |\alpha|^2 & \alpha\beta^* \\ \alpha^*\beta & |\beta|^2 \end{pmatrix}.\]

The diagonal entries \(|\alpha|^2\) and \(|\beta|^2\) give the probabilities of finding the pointer in \(|R_0\rangle_M\) or \(|R_1\rangle_M\), while the off-diagonal terms \(\alpha\beta^*\) encode phase coherence between the two pointer branches.

20.2 Reduced density matrices and environmental decoherence

20.2.1 Comparing a coherent pointer superposition with an incoherent mixture

An incoherent classical mixture of the two pointer branches—where the pointer is already definitely in \(|0, R_0\rangle\) with probability \(|\alpha|^2\) or definitely in \(|1, R_1\rangle\) with probability \(|\beta|^2\)—is described by the diagonal density matrix

\[\rho_{\mathrm{mix}} = \begin{pmatrix} |\alpha|^2 & 0 \\ 0 & |\beta|^2 \end{pmatrix}.\]

For any observable that is diagonal in the pointer basis \(\{|\Phi_0\rangle, |\Phi_1\rangle\}\), \(\rho_{\mathrm{pure}}\) and \(\rho_{\mathrm{mix}}\) predict the exact same outcome probabilities \(|\alpha|^2\) and \(|\beta|^2\).

20.2.2 Probing off-diagonal coherence in the joint pointer subspace

Distinguishing \(\rho_{\mathrm{pure}}\) from \(\rho_{\mathrm{mix}}\) requires measuring an interference observable that flips both the microscopic trigger \(S\) and the pointer \(M\) simultaneously—such as the joint parity/coherence operator \(\hat{X}_{\mathrm{joint}}(\phi) = e^{-i\phi}|\Phi_0\rangle\langle\Phi_1| + e^{i\phi}|\Phi_1\rangle\langle\Phi_0|\). For a state with coherence factor \(W \in [0, 1]\),

\[\rho(W) = \begin{pmatrix} |\alpha|^2 & W\alpha\beta^* \\ W\alpha^*\beta & |\beta|^2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad P_+(\phi) = \frac{1}{2} + W|\alpha\beta|\cos(\phi - \arg(\alpha\beta^*)).\]

Varying \(\phi\) produces interference fringes of visibility \(V = 2W|\alpha\beta|\).

20.2.3 Suppression of local coherence by orthogonal environment records

A macroscopic pointer cannot be isolated from its surrounding environment \(E\) (scattered photons, phonons, residual gas molecules). Even a tiny interaction leaks which-branch information into \(E\):

\[\big(\alpha|\Phi_0\rangle + \beta|\Phi_1\rangle\big)\otimes|E_0\rangle \;\longrightarrow\; |\Psi_{SME}\rangle = \alpha|\Phi_0\rangle|E_0\rangle + \beta|\Phi_1\rangle|E_1\rangle.\]

Tracing over the unobserved environment degrees of freedom yields the reduced system–pointer state

\[\rho_{SM} = \mathrm{Tr}_E\big(|\Psi_{SME}\rangle\langle\Psi_{SME}|\big) = \begin{pmatrix} |\alpha|^2 & \alpha\beta^*\langle E_1|E_0\rangle \\ \alpha^*\beta\langle E_0|E_1\rangle & |\beta|^2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad W = |\langle E_0|E_1\rangle|.\]

For a photonic or oscillator cat state of size \(|\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}|^2 = \bar{n}\) coupled to an environment with energy loss rate \(\kappa\), the overlap decays as \(W(t) = \exp(-2\kappa\bar{n}\,t)\): the decoherence rate \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{dec}} = 2\kappa\bar{n}\) scales linearly with the macroscopic separation \(D^2 = 4\bar{n}\) between the two pointer branches.

20.3 Decoherence versus the definite-outcome problem

20.3.1 Why environment-induced decoherence does not by itself select one branch

Environment-induced decoherence quantitatively explains why \(W = |\langle E_0|E_1\rangle|\) vanishes on sub-femtosecond timescales for macroscopic pointers and why preferred pointer bases (\(|R_0\rangle, |R_1\rangle\)) are dynamically selected (einselection) by local environmental monitoring. However, decoherence is derived entirely within linear unitary quantum mechanics applied to \(S + M + E\): the global state \(|\Psi_{SME}\rangle\) remains an entangled superposition of all branches, and tracing out \(E\) yields an improper mixture \(\rho_{SM}\). Decoherence explains why local interference vanishes, while the interpretive question of how or whether a single outcome is selected belongs to interpretations of quantum mechanics (Copenhagen/operational, Everett/many-worlds, Bohmian mechanics, or objective-collapse models).

20.3.2 Evaluating mesoscopic cat-state experiments in cavities and traps

Experiments in cavity QED (Haroche’s group), trapped ions (Wineland’s group), superconducting circuit QED, and optomechanical resonators directly verify the \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{dec}} \propto D^2\) scaling law by preparing cat states \(|\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}\rangle + e^{i\phi}|-\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}\rangle\) of tunable phase-space separation \(D = 2|\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}|\) and measuring Wigner-function negativity or parity oscillations as a function of hold time \(t\) and cat size \(\bar{n}\).

20.3.3 Keeping formal density-matrix predictions distinct from interpretations

In an experimental audit, the testable physical claims are the reduced density matrix \(\rho_{SM}(t)\), the Wigner fringe visibility \(V(\bar{n}, t) = \exp(-2\kappa\bar{n}\,t)\), and the purity \(\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{SM}^2)\). Metaphysical claims that do not modify \(\rho_{SM}(t)\) cannot be decided by any measurement restricted to \(S + M\); by contrast, objective-collapse theories (such as GRW or CSL) predict an extra environment-independent damping term that can be bounded experimentally by ultracold mechanical and matter-wave interferometers.

Explore the Apparatus

Adjust the initial branch weight \(|\alpha|^2\), the pointer separation \(\bar{n} = |\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}|^2\), and the environmental coupling \(\kappa t\) to watch the off-diagonal coherence \(W = |\langle E_0|E_1\rangle| = \exp(-2\kappa\bar{n}\,t)\) and the joint interference visibility \(V = 2W|\alpha\beta|\) decay while the diagonal branch probabilities remain constant.

Worked Check

Problem: A superconducting microwave cavity with photon energy relaxation rate \(\kappa = 2.0\times 10^4\text{ s}^{-1}\) (\(T_1^{\mathrm{cav}} = 50\,\mu\text{s}\)) is prepared in an equal cat superposition \((|\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}\rangle + |-\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}\rangle)/\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}\) with mean photon number \(\bar{n} = |\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}|^2 = 5.0\). Calculate the cat decoherence rate \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{dec}}\), the coherence time \(T_{\mathrm{cat}} = 1/\Gamma_{\mathrm{dec}}\), and the remaining off-diagonal coherence \(W(t)\) and reduced purity \(\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{SM}^2)\) after \(t = 2.5\,\mu\text{s}\).

Solution:

1. The cat decoherence rate scales as \(2\bar{n}\) times the single-photon loss rate \(\kappa\):

\[\Gamma_{\mathrm{dec}} = 2\kappa\bar{n} = 2(2.0\times 10^4\text{ s}^{-1})(5.0) = 2.0\times 10^5\text{ s}^{-1}, \qquad T_{\mathrm{cat}} = \frac{1}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{dec}}} = 5.0\,\mu\text{s}.\]

2. At \(t = 2.5\,\mu\text{s}\), the coherence factor is

\[W(2.5\,\mu\text{s}) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{2.5\,\mu\text{s}}{5.0\,\mu\text{s}}\right) = e^{-0.50} \approx 0.6065.\]

3. For equal branch weights \(|\alpha|^2 = |\beta|^2 = 1/2\), the reduced two-branch purity is

\[\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\rho_{SM}^2\right) = |\alpha|^4 + |\beta|^4 + 2W^2|\alpha|^2|\beta|^2 = \frac{1 + W^2}{2} = \frac{1 + e^{-1.0}}{2} \approx 0.6839.\]

Practice Problems

Problem 20.1: Scattering of \(N\) environmental photons

Suppose each scattered thermal photon has state overlap \(|\langle e_0|e_1\rangle| = 0.90\) between the two pointer positions. After \(N = 30\) independent photons scatter off the pointer, what is the collective environment overlap \(W = |\langle E_0|E_1\rangle| = |\langle e_0|e_1\rangle|^N\)?

Hint

For a product of \(N\) independent environmental modes, the inner product multiplies: \(\langle E_0|E_1\rangle = \prod_{k=1}^N \langle e_0^{(k)}|e_1^{(k)}\rangle\).

Solution

Raising the single-photon overlap to the \(30\)th power gives \(W = 0.90^{30} \approx 0.0424\). Even when each individual environmental particle carries very weak which-branch distinguishability, tens of scattered particles suppress macroscopic coherence exponentially.

Problem 20.2: Comparing single-branch vs. joint observables

For the reduced density matrix \(\rho(W) = \frac{1}{2}|\Phi_0\rangle\langle\Phi_0| + \frac{1}{2}|\Phi_1\rangle\langle\Phi_1| + \frac{W}{2}(|\Phi_0\rangle\langle\Phi_1| + |\Phi_1\rangle\langle\Phi_0|)\), show that any observable of the form \(\hat{O}_S \otimes I_M\) has an expectation value completely independent of \(W\) whenever \(\langle R_0|R_1\rangle = 0\).

Hint

Evaluate \(\langle\Phi_0|\hat{O}_S\otimes I_M|\Phi_1\rangle = \langle 0|\hat{O}_S|1\rangle\langle R_0|R_1\rangle\).

Solution

In the cross term, \(\langle\Phi_0|\hat{O}_S\otimes I_M|\Phi_1\rangle = \langle 0|\hat{O}_S|1\rangle\langle R_0|R_1\rangle = 0\) because the pointer states are orthogonal (\(\langle R_0|R_1\rangle = 0\)). Thus \(\mathrm{Tr}[(\hat{O}_S\otimes I_M)\rho(W)] = \frac{1}{2}\langle 0|\hat{O}_S|0\rangle + \frac{1}{2}\langle 1|\hat{O}_S|1\rangle\), which is independent of \(W\). Observing the coherence \(W\) requires an operator that simultaneously connects \(|0\rangle \leftrightarrow |1\rangle\) and \(|R_0\rangle \leftrightarrow |R_1\rangle\).

Problem 20.3: Cat size scaling of decoherence

In a cavity QED cat-state experiment, doubling the phase-space displacement amplitude from \(|\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}| = 1.5\) to \(|\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}| = 3.0\) changes the cat coherence time \(T_{\mathrm{cat}}\) by what factor?

Hint

Recall that \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{dec}} = 2\kappa|\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}|^2\).

Solution

Doubling \(|\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}|\) quadruples the mean photon number \(\bar{n} = |\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}|^2\) from \(2.25\) to \(9.0\), which quadruples the decoherence rate \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{dec}}\) and shortens the cat coherence time \(T_{\mathrm{cat}}\) by a factor of \(4\).

Key Takeaways

  • Unitary premeasurement couples a microscopic superposition \(\alpha|0\rangle_S + \beta|1\rangle_S\) to orthogonal pointer states \(|R_0\rangle_M, |R_1\rangle_M\), forming the entangled state \(\alpha|0,R_0\rangle + \beta|1,R_1\rangle\).
  • Tracing out an environment that records branch states \(|E_0\rangle, |E_1\rangle\) damps the off-diagonal pointer coherence by \(W = |\langle E_0|E_1\rangle|\).
  • For oscillator cat states of size \(\bar{n} = |\alpha_{\mathrm{cat}}|^2\), the decoherence rate \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{dec}} = 2\kappa\bar{n}\) grows in proportion to the squared phase-space separation between branches.
  • Environment-induced decoherence explains the rapid suppression of local interference in the pointer basis, while the question of single-outcome selection distinguishes interpretations of quantum mechanics.
