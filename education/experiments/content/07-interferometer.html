7.1 Two-arm recombination in a Mach–Zehnder interferometer

7.1.1 Splitting, phase shifting, and recombining two optical modes

A Mach–Zehnder interferometer divides a collimated input beam at a first balanced beam splitter (\(\mathrm{BS}_1\)) into an upper arm and a lower arm, redirects both arms with mirrors toward a second balanced beam splitter (\(\mathrm{BS}_2\)), and monitors the two exit ports with detectors \(D_0\) and \(D_1\). A dispersive phase plate in one arm introduces a controllable relative optical phase \(\phi\). Even though \(\mathrm{BS}_1\) and \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) would each send an isolated beam to \(D_0\) and \(D_1\) with equal probability \(1/2\), recombining both arms at \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) routes \(100\%\) of the output to \(D_0\) at \(\phi=0\) and \(100\%\) to \(D_1\) at \(\phi=\pi\).

7.1.2 Mode matching as the condition for indistinguishable paths

Full interference at \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) requires that the two arms be indistinguishable in every accessory degree of freedom at recombination: spatial wavefront, polarization, frequency spectrum, arrival time within the coherence length, and any coupled environment state. A mismatch in polarization or a path-identifying label reduces the interference contrast without changing the \(50{:}50\) splitting ratio of either beam splitter alone.

7.2 Beam-splitter matrices and fringe visibility

7.2.1 Hadamard convention for balanced two-port splitters

Label the two spatial arm modes by the orthonormal basis \(\lvert 0\rangle=(1,0)^T\) and \(\lvert 1\rangle=(0,1)^T\). Choosing the symmetric Hadamard phase convention for each lossless \(50{:}50\) beam splitter (an optical dielectric slab may instead assign a factor of \(i\) to reflection, which merely shifts the origin of \(\phi\) by \(\pi/2\)), \(\mathrm{BS}_1\) acts via

\[H = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad H\lvert 0\rangle = \frac{\lvert 0\rangle + \lvert 1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}.\]

Inserting the phase shift \(\phi\) on mode \(\lvert 1\rangle\) applies \(\mathrm{diag}(1,e^{i\phi})\), producing the internal two-arm state \(\lvert\psi(\phi)\rangle=(\lvert 0\rangle+e^{i\phi}\lvert 1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}\).

7.2.2 Phase-dependent output probabilities and dark-port cancellation

Recombining the two arms at \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) applies \(H\) a second time, yielding the output amplitudes \((1+e^{i\phi})/2\) at port \(D_0\) and \((1-e^{i\phi})/2\) at port \(D_1\). Taking squared magnitudes gives the complementary detection probabilities

\[P_0(\phi) = \left|\frac{1+e^{i\phi}}{2}\right|^2 = \frac{1+\cos\phi}{2} = \cos^2\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right), \qquad P_1(\phi) = \frac{1-\cos\phi}{2} = \sin^2\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right).\]

At \(\phi=0\), the two paths to \(D_1\) carry opposite signs (\(+1/2\) and \(-1/2\)) and cancel completely (\(P_1=0\), dark port) while reinforcing at \(D_0\) (\(P_0=1\), bright port). At \(\phi=\pi\), the bright and dark ports swap; at \(\phi=\pi/2\) (\(90^\circ\)), both ports register probability \(1/2\).

7.2.3 Visibility reduction from partial coherence

When partial path distinguishability or phase jitter damps the off-diagonal coherence between the two arms by a real factor \(V\in[0,1]\), the output probabilities become \(P_0(\phi)=(1+V\cos\phi)/2\) and \(P_1(\phi)=(1-V\cos\phi)/2\). The operational fringe visibility is

\[V = \frac{P_{\max}-P_{\min}}{P_{\max}+P_{\min}}.\]

Because a single phase setting \(\phi\) cannot separate a phase offset from a reduced visibility \(V<1\), characterizing an interferometer requires scanning \(\phi\) across at least one full period.

7.3 Phase scans, drift controls, and single-photon fringes

7.3.1 Comparing classical wave interference with single-photon counts

Classical coherent electromagnetic waves passing through a Mach–Zehnder interferometer exhibit the exact same normalized port intensities \(\cos^2(\phi/2)\) and \(\sin^2(\phi/2)\). What makes a single-photon Mach–Zehnder experiment distinctively quantum is the combination of full fringe visibility \(V\approx 1\) when \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) is present with photon anticorrelation \(g^{(2)}(0)\ll 1\) between the two arms when \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) is removed.

7.3.2 Calibrating detector imbalance and path-length drift

In a physical optical setup, thermal expansion of mirror mounts shifts \(\phi(t)\) over time, while unequal detector efficiencies \(\eta_0\neq\eta_1\) skew the raw count ratio. Recording blocked-arm baselines, interleaving reference phase points during a scan, and normalizing each port by its calibrated efficiency prevent instrumental drift from masquerading asdecoherence.

7.3.3 Foundation for delayed-choice, eraser, and bomb-tester protocols

This two-mode Mach–Zehnder algebra serves as the backbone for Wheeler’s delayed-choice experiment (Chapter 9), the quantum eraser (Chapter 10), and the Elitzur–Vaidman interaction-free bomb tester (Chapter 11).

Explore the experiment

Sweep the phase \(\phi\) from \(0^\circ\) through \(360^\circ\). Pause at \(\phi=0^\circ\), \(90^\circ\), \(180^\circ\), and \(270^\circ\) to compare \(P_0(\phi)\) and \(P_1(\phi)\), and verify that \(P_0(\phi)+P_1(\phi)=1\) at every phase setting.

A worked example

At which phase angles \(\phi\in[0^\circ,360^\circ)\) does the ideal Mach–Zehnder interferometer yield \(P_0(\phi)=3/4\) at detector \(D_0\)?

Setting \(P_0(\phi)=(1+\cos\phi)/2=3/4\) gives \(\cos\phi=1/2\). Within a single turn \([0^\circ,360^\circ)\), there are two solutions: \(\phi=60^\circ\) (\(\pi/3\)) and \(\phi=300^\circ\) (\(5\pi/3\)). Because \(\cos\phi\) is an even periodic function, measuring \(P_0\) at a single phase setting does not determine \(\phi\) uniquely without scanning adjacent phases.

Try it yourself

Problem 7.1

What are the detector probabilities \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) if the second beam splitter \(\mathrm{BS}_2\) is removed from the Mach–Zehnder interferometer?

Hint

Evaluate the squared magnitudes of the amplitudes in \(\lvert\psi(\phi)\rangle=(\lvert 0\rangle+e^{i\phi}\lvert 1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}\) directly in the path basis.

Worked solution

Without \(\mathrm{BS}_2\), detectors \(D_0\) and \(D_1\) measure the two arm modes directly: \(P_0=|1/\sqrt{2}|^2=1/2\) and \(P_1=|e^{i\phi}/\sqrt{2}|^2=1/2\) for every value of \(\phi\). Without recombination, the relative phase \(\phi\) has no effect on the detector probabilities.

Problem 7.2

In a partially coherent Mach–Zehnder interferometer with visibility \(V=0.60\), what are the maximum and minimum values of \(P_0(\phi)\) across a full phase scan?

Hint

Set \(\cos\phi=+1\) and \(\cos\phi=-1\) in \(P_0(\phi)=(1+V\cos\phi)/2\).

Worked solution

The maximum is \(P_{\max}=(1+0.60)/2=0.80\) at \(\phi=0^\circ\) and the minimum is \(P_{\min}=(1-0.60)/2=0.20\) at \(\phi=180^\circ\). Substituting into \((P_{\max}-P_{\min})/(P_{\max}+P_{\min})=(0.80-0.20)/(0.80+0.20)=0.60\) recovers \(V\).

Problem 7.3

Why must \(P_0(\phi)+P_1(\phi)=1\) hold for every phase \(\phi\) in an ideal lossless Mach–Zehnder interferometer?

Hint

Consider the unitarity of \(H\) and the phase operator \(\mathrm{diag}(1,e^{i\phi})\).

Worked solution

Both \(H\) and \(\mathrm{diag}(1,e^{i\phi})\) are unitary matrices, so their product \(U(\phi)=H\,\mathrm{diag}(1,e^{i\phi})H\) preserves the norm of the state vector: \(P_0(\phi)+P_1(\phi)=\lVert U(\phi)\lvert 0\rangle\rVert^2=1\).

What to carry forward

A relative phase \(\phi\) between two modes becomes an observable probability difference only after a recombining beam splitter mixes the two alternatives. Always specify the beam-splitter phase convention and scan \(\phi\) across a full period to separate phase shifts from visibility loss.
