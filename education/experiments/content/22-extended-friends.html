22.1 Two entangled laboratories and superobserver basis choices

22.1.1 Comparing the Frauchiger–Renner protocol with local-friendliness theorems

A single Wigner’s friend laboratory (Chapter 21) does not by itself force a Bell-type contradiction: one can always assign a joint probability distribution to the friend’s single outcome and Wigner’s single outcome because the two measurements are never performed together. By coupling two isolated Wigner’s-friend laboratories—\(\mathrm{Lab}_1\) containing friend \(F_1\) and \(\mathrm{Lab}_2\) containing friend \(F_2\)—and allowing external superobservers \(W_1\) and \(W_2\) to measure those laboratories in complementary bases, Časlav Brukner (2018), Daniela Frauchiger and Renato Renner (2018), and Bong et al. (2020) proved extended Wigner’s-friend no-go theorems. These theorems show that one cannot simultaneously maintain universal unitary quantum mechanics, locality/no-superdeterminism, and the assumption that every internal measurement produces a single context-independent “absolute” outcome that can be combined into a joint four-party probability table \(P(f_1, f_2, w_1, w_2)\).

22.1.2 Internal record basis states across two coupled laboratories

Let \(|0\rangle_{L_k}\) and \(|1\rangle_{L_k}\) denote the orthonormal composite states of laboratory \(k \in \{1, 2\}\) in which the internal system and the friend’s memory register record outcome \(0\) (e.g., “heads” or \(-1\)) or outcome \(1\) (e.g., “tails” or \(+1\)):

\[|0\rangle_{L_k} = |0\rangle_{S_k}|R_0\rangle_{F_k}, \qquad |1\rangle_{L_k} = |1\rangle_{S_k}|R_1\rangle_{F_k}.\]

Each superobserver \(W_k\) can either open the door to read the friend’s record in the computational basis \(\{|0\rangle_{L_k}, |1\rangle_{L_k}\}\) or perform a coherent reversal measurement in the Hadamard/superobserver basis

\[|\mathrm{ok}\rangle_{L_k} = \frac{|0\rangle_{L_k} - |1\rangle_{L_k}}{\sqrt{2}} = |-\rangle_{L_k}, \qquad |\mathrm{fail}\rangle_{L_k} = \frac{|0\rangle_{L_k} + |1\rangle_{L_k}}{\sqrt{2}} = |+\rangle_{L_k}.\]

22.1.3 Tracking amplitude interference separately from modal agent reasoning

Extended-friend papers often interleave two very different kinds of derivation: (1) standard Hilbert-space inner products on the two-laboratory state \(|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle\), which are completely unambiguous, and (2) modal logic rules describing how one agent reasons about another agent’s certainty across different measurement contexts. Keeping the exact Hilbert-space amplitudes separate from the modal inference rules makes it immediately transparent why the contradiction arises.

22.2 Three-term amplitude cancellation and the one-twelfth probability

22.2.1 Evaluating the non-maximally entangled two-lab state in the outside basis

In the Frauchiger–Renner state preparation, friend \(F_1\) measures a biased coin with \(P(0) = 1/3\) and \(P(1) = 2/3\); conditioned on \(0\), \(F_1\) sends \(|0\rangle_{S_2}\) to \(F_2\), and conditioned on \(1\), \(F_1\) sends \((|0\rangle_{S_2} + |1\rangle_{S_2})/\sqrt{2}\) to \(F_2\). Under universal unitary evolution of both isolated laboratories, the joint state of \(\mathrm{Lab}_1 \otimes \mathrm{Lab}_2\) is the Hardy-type non-maximally entangled state

\[|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\Big(|0,0\rangle + |1,0\rangle + |1,1\rangle\Big),\]

where the fourth computational basis term \(|0,1\rangle\) has amplitude strictly zero: \(\langle 0,1|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = 0\).

Now evaluate the amplitude for both superobservers \(W_1\) and \(W_2\) to obtain \(|\mathrm{ok}\rangle = (|0\rangle - |1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}\) simultaneously. Since

\[|\mathrm{ok}, \mathrm{ok}\rangle = \frac{1}{2}\Big(|0,0\rangle - |0,1\rangle - |1,0\rangle + |1,1\rangle\Big),\]

taking the inner product with \(|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle\) gives

\[\langle\mathrm{ok},\mathrm{ok}|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}\big(1 - 0 - 1 + 1\big) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{ok}_2) = \big|\langle\mathrm{ok},\mathrm{ok}|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle\big|^2 = \frac{1}{12}.\]

22.2.2 Full joint probability table for superobserver outcomes

Projecting \(|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle\) onto all four basis states \(\{|\mathrm{ok}\rangle, |\mathrm{fail}\rangle\}^{\otimes 2}\) yields the normalized superobserver distribution:

\[P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{ok}_2) = \frac{1}{12}, \qquad P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{fail}_2) = \frac{1}{12}, \qquad P(\mathrm{fail}_1, \mathrm{ok}_2) = \frac{1}{12}, \qquad P(\mathrm{fail}_1, \mathrm{fail}_2) = \frac{9}{12} = \frac{3}{4}.\]

Notice that the \(|0,0\rangle\) and \(|1,0\rangle\) amplitudes cancel destructively in \(\langle\mathrm{ok},\mathrm{ok}|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle\) (\(+1 - 1 = 0\)), leaving only the \(|1,1\rangle\) contribution \(1/(2\sqrt{3})\).

22.2.3 Where chained certainty inferences cross incompatible contexts

Why do the chained agent inferences predict \(P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{ok}_2) = 0\) when the quantum calculation gives \(1/12\)? Consider the three certainty conditions encoded in \(|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle\):

  1. Because \(\langle 0,1|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = 0\), if \(F_1\) records \(1_{L_1}\), then \(\mathrm{Lab}_1\) is definitely in \(|1\rangle_{L_1}\)? No: if \(F_2\) records \(1_{L_2}\), the only surviving computational branch is \(|1,1\rangle\), so \(L_1\) is in \(|1\rangle_{L_1}\).
  2. Writing \(|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\,|+\rangle_{L_1}|0\rangle_{L_2} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\,|1\rangle_{L_1}|1\rangle_{L_2}\) shows that \(\langle\mathrm{ok}_1, 0_{L_2}|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = 0\): conditioned on \(W_1\) obtaining \(\mathrm{ok}_1\), \(\mathrm{Lab}_2\) would yield \(1_{L_2}\) with certainty if measured in the \(Z_{L_2}\) basis.
  3. Writing \(|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\,|0\rangle_{L_1}|0\rangle_{L_2} + \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\,|1\rangle_{L_1}|+\rangle_{L_2}\) shows that \(\langle 1_{L_1}, \mathrm{ok}_2|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = 0\): conditioned on \(1_{L_1}\), \(W_2\) would obtain \(\mathrm{fail}_2\) with certainty.

Chaining (2) \(\mathrm{ok}_1 \Rightarrow 1_{L_2}\), (1) \(1_{L_2} \Rightarrow 1_{L_1}\), and (3) \(1_{L_1} \Rightarrow \mathrm{fail}_2\) treats the unperformed intermediate measurements \(Z_{L_2}\) and \(Z_{L_1}\) as possessing definite values inside the run where \(W_1\) and \(W_2\) actually measure \(X_{L_1}\) and \(X_{L_2}\). Just as in Hardy’s paradox and Chapter 18, combining counterfactual certainties from noncommuting contexts eliminates the destructive interference between \(|0,0\rangle\) and \(|1,0\rangle\).

22.3 Dissecting no-go assumption packages

22.3.1 Universal unitary validity, single outcomes, and cross-agent consistency

The Frauchiger–Renner theorem proves that at least one of three assumptions must be dropped: (Q) universal validity of unitary quantum mechanics at the superobserver scale, (S) each measurement has a single outcome, and (C) agents can paste together certainty claims derived in incompatible measurement contexts as if a single global history of internal and external outcomes existed.

22.3.2 Absoluteness of observed events in local-friendliness inequalities

The local-friendliness theorem (Bong et al., 2020) replaces modal agent logic with a strictly operational Bell-like test. In each run, superobserver \(W_k\) chooses either \(x_k = 0\) (open the door and copy friend \(F_k\)’s record \(w_k = f_k\)) or \(x_k = 1\) (measure \(\mathrm{Lab}_k\) in a rotated basis). The assumption of Absoluteness of Observed Events (AOE) states that in every run there exists a joint probability distribution \(P(w_1, w_2, f_1, f_2 \mid x_1, x_2)\) whose marginals recover the observed outcome when \(x_k = 0\). Together with Locality and No-Superdeterminism, AOE implies that the observed correlations \(E(x_1, x_2)\) must satisfy the standard CHSH and local-friendliness polytope bounds (such as \(|S| \le 2\)), whereas unitary quantum mechanics on two entangled laboratories violates those bounds up to \(2\sqrt{2}\).

22.3.3 Why destroying internal records during superobserver readout matters

Measuring \(\mathrm{Lab}_k\) in the \(|\mathrm{ok}\rangle/|\mathrm{fail}\rangle\) basis requires applying the inverse of the friend’s measurement unitary and erasing the internal record \(f_k\). Consequently, in any run where \(W_1\) and \(W_2\) actually measure \(\mathrm{ok}/\mathrm{fail}\), no durable physical record of \((f_1, f_2)\) survives at the end of the experiment.

Explore the Apparatus

Inspect the four computational branch amplitudes of the two-laboratory state \(|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = (|0,0\rangle + |1,0\rangle + |1,1\rangle)/\sqrt{3}\) and adjust the coherence factor \(W \in [0, 1]\) of the superobserver reversal measurements. Compare the unitary quantum probability \(P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{ok}_2) = 1/12\) against the decohered mixture (\(W = 0\), where \(P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{ok}_2) = 1/4\)) and trace how the \(|0,0\rangle\) and \(|1,0\rangle\) amplitudes cancel destructively.

Worked Check

Problem: Suppose the two laboratories are prepared in \(|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = (|0,0\rangle + |1,0\rangle + |1,1\rangle)/\sqrt{3}\), and each laboratory undergoes partial dephasing in the friend’s record basis so that off-diagonal elements between \(|0\rangle_{L_k}\) and \(|1\rangle_{L_k}\) are damped by \(W \in [0,1]\) independently in each lab. Find \(P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{ok}_2)\) as a function of \(W\), and evaluate it at \(W = 1\) (ideal unitary labs) and \(W = 0\) (collapsed labs).

Solution:

1. Since \(|\mathrm{ok}\rangle\langle\mathrm{ok}| = \frac{1}{2}(I - X)\), independent dephasing \(X_{L_k} \mapsto W X_{L_k}\) replaces the projector expectation by

\[P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{ok}_2) = \frac{1}{4}\Big\langle (I - W X_{L_1})\otimes (I - W X_{L_2})\Big\rangle_{\Psi}.\]

2. On \(|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = (|0,0\rangle + |1,0\rangle + |1,1\rangle)/\sqrt{3}\), direct evaluation gives

\[\langle X_{L_1}\otimes I\rangle = \frac{2}{3}, \qquad \langle I\otimes X_{L_2}\rangle = \frac{2}{3}, \qquad \langle X_{L_1}\otimes X_{L_2}\rangle = \frac{2}{3}.\]

3. Substituting these three expectations yields

\[P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{ok}_2) = \frac{1}{4}\left(1 - \frac{4}{3}W + \frac{2}{3}W^2\right) = \frac{3 - 4W + 2W^2}{12}.\]

At \(W = 1\) (coherent unitary labs), \(P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{ok}_2) = (3 - 4 + 2)/12 = 1/12 \approx 0.0833\). At \(W = 0\) (collapsed labs), \(P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{ok}_2) = 3/12 = 1/4 = 0.250\).

Practice Problems

Problem 22.1: Verifying the zero-amplitude Hardy condition

Starting from the state \(|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = (|0,0\rangle + |1,0\rangle + |1,1\rangle)/\sqrt{3}\) and \(|\mathrm{ok}\rangle = (|0\rangle - |1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}\), verify explicitly that \(\langle\mathrm{ok}_1, 0_{L_2}|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = 0\) and \(\langle 1_{L_1}, \mathrm{ok}_2|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = 0\).

Hint

Expand \(\langle\mathrm{ok}_1, 0_{L_2}| = (\langle 0,0| - \langle 1,0|)/\sqrt{2}\) and take the inner product with \(|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle\).

Solution

First, \(\langle\mathrm{ok}_1, 0_{L_2}|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(1 - 1 + 0) = 0\). Second, \(\langle 1_{L_1}, \mathrm{ok}_2| = (\langle 1,0| - \langle 1,1|)/\sqrt{2}\), so \(\langle 1_{L_1}, \mathrm{ok}_2|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(0 + 1 - 1) = 0\).

Problem 22.2: Superobserver marginal probability \(P(\mathrm{ok}_1)\)

Using \(|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\,|\mathrm{fail}\rangle_{L_1}|0\rangle_{L_2} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\,|1\rangle_{L_1}|1\rangle_{L_2}\), find the marginal probability \(P(\mathrm{ok}_1)\) that superobserver \(W_1\) obtains outcome \(\mathrm{ok}_1\) regardless of what \(W_2\) measures.

Hint

Use \(\langle\mathrm{ok}|\mathrm{fail}\rangle = 0\) and \(|\langle\mathrm{ok}|1\rangle|^2 = 1/2\).

Solution

Projecting onto \(|\mathrm{ok}\rangle_{L_1}\) annihilates the first term and leaves \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\langle\mathrm{ok}|1\rangle_{L_1}|1\rangle_{L_2} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}|1\rangle_{L_2}\), whose norm squared is \(P(\mathrm{ok}_1) = 1/6 = 2/12\) (which equals \(P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{ok}_2) + P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{fail}_2) = 1/12 + 1/12\)).

Problem 22.3: Why \(P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{ok}_2) = 1/4\) when both friends’ records collapse

Suppose both friends’ measurements irreversibly collapse the state to the diagonal mixture \(\rho_{\mathrm{mix}} = \frac{1}{3}(|0,0\rangle\langle 0,0| + |1,0\rangle\langle 1,0| + |1,1\rangle\langle 1,1|)\). Show directly that \(\langle\mathrm{ok}, \mathrm{ok}|\rho_{\mathrm{mix}}|\mathrm{ok}, \mathrm{ok}\rangle = 1/4\).

Hint

For every computational basis state \(|a,b\rangle\), \(|\langle\mathrm{ok},\mathrm{ok}|a,b\rangle|^2 = (1/2)(1/2) = 1/4\).

Solution

Because \(|\langle\mathrm{ok},\mathrm{ok}|a,b\rangle|^2 = 1/4\) for every \((a,b) \in \{0,1\}^2\), any diagonal mixture \(\sum_{a,b} p_{ab}|a,b\rangle\langle a,b|\) yields \(P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{ok}_2) = \frac{1}{4}\sum_{a,b} p_{ab} = 1/4\). The reduction from \(1/4\) down to \(1/12\) in the unitary case is a direct signature of destructive quantum interference between \(|0,0\rangle\) and \(|1,0\rangle\).

Key Takeaways

  • For the two-laboratory state \(|\Psi_{L_1 L_2}\rangle = (|0,0\rangle + |1,0\rangle + |1,1\rangle)/\sqrt{3}\), unitary quantum mechanics predicts \(P(\mathrm{ok}_1, \mathrm{ok}_2) = |\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}(1 - 0 - 1 + 1)|^2 = 1/12\).
  • The apparent paradox (\(P = 0\) vs.\ \(P = 1/12\)) arises from chaining counterfactual certainty statements across noncommuting measurement contexts (\(Z_{L_k}\) vs.\ \(X_{L_k}\)), which ignores the destructive interference between the \(|0,0\rangle\) and \(|1,0\rangle\) amplitudes.
  • Local-friendliness theorems show that universal unitarity, locality, and the existence of a single joint probability distribution over both internal and external outcomes cannot all hold simultaneously.
