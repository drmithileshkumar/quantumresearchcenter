21.1 The question and the apparatus

A friend inside an isolated laboratory measures a qubit prepared in |+⟩. The friend records zero or one. Wigner outside treats the qubit and the friend’s record as a combined quantum system. If the interaction is unitary and the whole laboratory remains isolated, Wigner writes a coherent superposition of the two correlated record states.

Two possible later actions must be distinguished. Wigner can open the laboratory and read the record, or attempt a joint interference measurement that probes coherence between the entire record branches. The second action requires extraordinary control, including every degree of freedom that contains which-record information. It is not equivalent to asking the friend what happened.

The visual models the friend as a reversible memory qubit. That makes the algebra explicit and experimentally approachable, but does not model consciousness or prove that a human observer can be coherently reversed. The sealed box is an assumption in the thought experiment, not evidence that macroscopic isolation is achievable.

21.2 Build a prediction

Let |F0⟩ and |F1⟩ be orthogonal memory states. Ideal premeasurement gives |Ψ⟩=(|0,F0⟩+|1,F1⟩)/√2. Reading the record yields equal probabilities. Define |W±⟩=(|0,F0⟩±|1,F1⟩)/√2. Wigner's coherent description predicts P(W+)=1; an actual incoherent 50/50 mixture predicts 1/2.

A convenient circuit writes a record with a CNOT from system to memory. Reversing that CNOT, then measuring the system in X, implements an interference test on this ideal subspace. It also erases the memory's record. One cannot both retain an independent perfectly distinguishing record and recover perfect interference in that same reduced system.

With real residual coherence γ, the explorer gives P(W+)=(1+γ)/2. The friend's record probabilities remain 1/2. A state conditioned on a particular friend's record and Wigner's unconditioned joint state answer different questions. They cannot be substituted into one another without tracking the conditioning and the later measurement.

21.3 What the evidence can establish

Wigner's friend exposes the difficulty of applying quantum theory to observers who themselves use it. The tension depends on what a quantum state represents, how outcome statements are used, and which global operations are allowed. Different interpretations respond differently. The elementary calculation is not a proof that ordinary quantum predictions are inconsistent.

Experiments can implement memory systems and interference operations inspired by the scenario. Their conclusions concern those systems and their measured correlations. Equating such memories with conscious observers is an additional interpretive step, not an observed feature of the apparatus.

The essential practical limitation is leaked information. If an environment holds an unreversed copy of the record, reversing the small memory alone does not restore full coherence. Any claim of a Wigner measurement should identify the controlled subsystem and explain which records remain outside it.

Explore the experiment

Switch Wigner’s measurement between reading the record and testing interference. Vary the surviving coherence. Observe that the two measurement choices reveal different properties of the same joint-state model.

A worked example

A record is copied to an inaccessible environment with orthogonal states. Wigner reverses only the system–memory CNOT. What X statistics remain?

The system is still correlated with orthogonal environment records. Its reduced state is I/2, so both X outcomes have probability 1/2. Reversing the accessible record does not erase the environmental copy.

Try it yourself

Problem 21.1

What does Wigner predict for reading the friend’s record in the coherent model?

Hint

Use the record basis, not the W basis.

Worked solution

Zero and one each have probability 1/2. Coherence is not visible in that basis.

Problem 21.2

What distinguishes a coherent laboratory from a mixture?

Hint

Find a joint observable with different expectations.

Worked solution

The W± measurement gives P(W+)=1 for the ideal coherent state and 1/2 for the mixture.

Problem 21.3

Does a photonic memory experiment show that a person’s experience was reversed?

Hint

Identify the implemented observer model.

Worked solution

No. It tests controlled quantum degrees of freedom used as a record. A claim about conscious experience requires assumptions beyond those measurements.

What to carry forward

Specify the observer, the conditioning, and the later measurement. A reversible memory model illuminates the thought experiment without settling the status of human observers.
