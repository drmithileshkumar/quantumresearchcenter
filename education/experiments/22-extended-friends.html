V · Records and observers · Chapter 22

Extended Friends and Consistency

Which assumptions make different observers’ certainty claims conflict?

In this chapter
  1. The question and the apparatus
  2. Build a prediction
  3. What the evidence can establish
  4. Interactive experiment
  5. Problems and solutions

22.1 The question and the apparatus

Extended Wigner scenarios place more than one observing laboratory inside a larger coherent experiment. Frauchiger and Renner analyse a protocol in which agents reason about other agents' predictions. Bong and colleagues formulate a different family of constraints, called local-friendliness inequalities. These are related questions, but they are not the same theorem.

We first examine a two-laboratory state that captures the probability calculation in the Frauchiger–Renner setup. Each laboratory includes a recorded result. Treat the states |h⟩ and |t⟩ as orthogonal records in the first lab, and |d⟩ and |u⟩ as orthogonal records in the second. An outside experimenter can, ideally, measure superpositions of those record states.

The display separates record-basis probabilities from probabilities in the two outside ok/fail bases. It animates the three amplitude contributions to an ok,ok event. No agents' beliefs are simulated. The inference rules must be examined in words rather than inferred from the animation alone.

22.2 Build a prediction

Consider \(|\Psi\rangle=(|h,d\rangle+|t,d\rangle+|t,u\rangle)/\sqrt3\). Let |ok⟩A=(|h⟩−|t⟩)/√2 and |ok⟩B=(|d⟩−|u⟩)/√2, with plus signs defining fail. The three contributions to the ok,ok amplitude are +1/√12, −1/√12, and +1/√12. Their sum is 1/√12, so the probability is 1/12.

The other joint probabilities are P(ok,fail)=1/12, P(fail,ok)=1/12, and P(fail,fail)=3/4. They sum to one. Ordinary unitary quantum theory therefore makes an unambiguous prediction for this fully specified joint measurement.

The conceptual puzzle adds certainty inferences: conditioned on t, the second lab is in the fail superposition; a recorded u excludes h; and an outside ok in the first lab excludes d in the relevant hybrid context. Chaining those statements across observers can appear to forbid ok,ok. The chain crosses different measurement contexts and uses assumptions about transferable certainty and single outcomes. The nonzero amplitude alone is not a contradiction.

22.3 What the evidence can establish

Frauchiger–Renner's no-go claim concerns a package of universal quantum reasoning, consistency between agents, and single outcomes under its protocol. Responses differ over which inference steps are warranted. This chapter supplies the finite amplitude calculation and locates the extra reasoning assumptions; it does not pretend that a probability table proves a unique interpretation.

Local-friendliness results instead constrain combinations including absoluteness of observed events and local causal assumptions, under idealized control of friend systems. Their experimental implementations use small quantum systems as observers. Extending the conclusion to conscious observers or unrestricted macroscopic laboratories requires further premises.

In both cases, specify what is physically measured, what is counterfactual, and what information each agent conditions on. When a proposal uses an interference measurement on a laboratory, retaining its original record elsewhere may change the prediction. A source's theorem statement and operational scenario should be read together.

Explore the experiment

Switch between the record and outside bases. In the outside basis, step through the three signed contributions to ok,ok. Square their sum, not their individual magnitudes, and compare with the probability table.

A worked example

What erroneous probability results from adding the three squared ok,ok contributions instead of amplitudes?

Each squared contribution is 1/12, so the erroneous sum is 1/4. The correct amplitude sum includes cancellation and gives 1/12. Treating alternatives as an incoherent mixture changes the preparation.

Try it yourself

Problem 22.1

Check the normalization of the outside probability table.

Hint

Use a denominator of twelve.

Worked solution

1/12+1/12+1/12+9/12=1.

Problem 22.2

Does a nonzero ok,ok probability itself make quantum theory inconsistent?

Hint

Separate the prediction from the additional inference chain.

Worked solution

No. It is a consistent Born-rule prediction. The tension arises when it is combined with cross-agent certainty claims under an additional assumption package.

Problem 22.3

Why are local-friendliness inequalities not interchangeable with the Frauchiger–Renner argument?

Hint

Compare their premises and experimental tasks.

Worked solution

They constrain different assumption packages and use different operational scenarios. A violation or inference in one is not automatically a demonstration of the other.

What to carry forward

Calculate the specified experiment first. Then identify the extra assumptions needed to transfer claims between observers and incompatible contexts.
