II · Paths, information, and time · Chapter 10

Quantum Erasure and Conditional Fringes

Where are the fringes hiding when path information is available?

In this chapter
  1. The question and the apparatus
  2. Build a prediction
  3. What the evidence can establish
  4. Interactive experiment
  5. Problems and solutions

10.1 The question and the apparatus

Correlate the two paths with orthogonal marker states, for example horizontal and vertical polarization of a partner system. Looking only at the signal gives no interference. Instead of reading the marker in the path-identifying basis, measure it in a superposition basis and sort signal detections by the marker outcome.

The sorted subsets can exhibit complementary interference patterns. The full dataset does not. This distinction between joint data and a selected subset is the essential experimental idea. Discarding a file containing path labels is not the same physical operation as measuring a coherent marker in a new basis.

The visual shows three distributions together: all signal events, events paired with marker +, and events paired with marker −. Each conditional curve is separately normalized. The weighted sum, not the unweighted visual height of a selected subset, predicts what the signal observer sees alone.

10.2 Build a prediction

Take the ideal state (|L⟩|H⟩+eiφ|R⟩|V⟩)/√2. In the marker basis |±⟩=(|H⟩±|V⟩)/√2, the conditional signal states are (|L⟩±eiφ|R⟩)/√2. The sign changes the interference term, shifting maxima of one subset to minima of the other.

After recombination, \(P(0\mid +)=(1+\cos\phi)/2\) and \(P(0\mid -)=(1-\cos\phi)/2\). Each marker sign occurs with probability 1/2. Therefore P(0)=ΣmP(m)P(0|m)=1/2 for every φ. At a screen, the same cancellation occurs between complementary fringes within an envelope.

The ordering of measurements on separate subsystems does not change these ideal joint probabilities, because the local measurement operators commute. Sorting a previously collected dataset later can reveal a correlation without changing any earlier event. The sorting requires the marker records to be communicated.

10.3 What the evidence can establish

An eraser experiment supports predictions about correlations and conditioning. It does not enable faster-than-light communication: a signal observer without the marker labels sees the same marginal distribution. Nor does a delayed marker measurement alter the past screen image. The later operation determines which conditional description is useful.

For real coincidence experiments, the time window and pairing rule matter. Accidental pairs dilute visibility; selecting windows after seeing the desired pattern can bias a result. Report both sorted subsets, their rates, and the unsorted data. Showing only one attractive fringe leaves out the cancellation that explains the experiment.

Our display assumes an ideal maximally entangled marker and equal subset weights. Imperfect entanglement, background, and unequal efficiencies need a larger model. The linked experiment is a physical implementation; the browser curves are an independently specified teaching model, not its data.

Explore the experiment

Change the phase and compare the two conditional curves. Check that their equal-weight average stays flat. Explain what information a distant signal observer would need to reproduce either subset.

A worked example

At φ=0, what are P(0,+), P(0,−), and P(0)?

P(0,+)=P(+)P(0|+)=1/2. P(0,−)=0. Their sum is P(0)=1/2. A certain conditional outcome does not mean a certain unconditional outcome.

Try it yourself

Problem 10.1

Why do opposite fringes not constitute a signal at the unsorted detector?

Hint

Average with the marker probabilities.

Worked solution

The cosine terms cancel in the equal-weight sum. The local marginal is independent of the eraser outcome.

Problem 10.2

Is deleting a classical path log a quantum eraser?

Hint

Compare a classical record with a controllable coherent marker.

Worked solution

No. Deletion does not undo the correlations with uncontrolled degrees of freedom or implement a superposition-basis measurement.

Problem 10.3

What must be reported alongside a selected coincidence fringe?

Hint

Consider normalization and selection.

Worked solution

The opposite subset, subset frequencies, unsorted counts, pairing rule, and uncertainty are needed to assess the correlation and rule out selection artifacts.

What to carry forward

Conditional fringes can coexist with an unmodulated marginal. Always write the conditioning event next to the probability.
