This lesson includes explanations, worked examples, and problems with solutions. Advanced results whose proofs are outside the chapter are identified explicitly.

7.1 Calibration Experiments

A fringe oscillates while its envelope decays. Its period estimates a detuning; its envelope estimates a sequence-dependent coherence time. Extracting those quantities requires a model and a calibration strategy.

7.1.1 Spectroscopy and Rabi scans

Spectroscopy scans drive frequency and measures the response to locate a transition. A Rabi experiment fixes the drive frequency and scans pulse duration or strength. The two scans answer different questions: where a transition lies and how strongly it is driven. Power broadening, extra levels, and state-preparation error can alter the apparent resonance.

7.1.2 Ramsey and echo

In a Ramsey sequence, two π/2 rotations surround a wait. With a suitable readout phase, \(P_0(t)=[1+V e^{-t/T_2^*}\cos(\Delta t+\phi_0)]/2\). Visibility V, offset phase φ0, decay, and detuning must not be conflated. A 2 μs fringe period corresponds to |Δ|/2π=0.5 MHz. The sign of detuning requires a phase-sensitive convention, not just the period.

Problem 7.1. What does a Ramsey period fail to reveal by itself?

Worked solution

It gives the magnitude of a detuning in the chosen model, but not its sign, the preparation error, or a full gate fidelity.

7.2 Process Characterization

7.2.1 State and process tomography

For a qubit state, measurements along X,Y,Z determine its Bloch components through their ±1 means, yielding ρ=(I+xX+yY+zZ)/2. Process tomography uses several known input states and output measurements to infer a channel. It requires assumptions about preparation and measurement, so an inaccurate reference can be mistaken for a bad gate.

7.2.2 Randomized benchmarking

Randomized benchmarking applies random gate sequences and an ideal inverse, then fits survival versus sequence length. Under the standard single-qubit depolarizing model the decay has form Aα^m+B, and average error per Clifford is (1−α)/2. This is a model-dependent interpretation, not a universal identity for arbitrary drift, leakage, or gate-dependent coherent noise. Error per Clifford also differs from error per native gate.

Problem 7.2. For α=0.998, what is the single-qubit depolarizing-model error per Clifford?

Worked solution

It is (1−0.998)/2=0.001. The qualification about the model and the counted gate type is essential.

7.3 Measurement Limits

7.3.1 Preparation and measurement errors

Preparation and measurement errors can appear in fitted offsets, but separating them from gate errors requires appropriate assumptions or additional experiments. A high survival probability after one favorable sequence proves little about all inputs. Test phase-sensitive states, leakage, simultaneous gates, and repeated readout when those behaviors matter to the workload.

7.3.2 Drift and validation

Split fitting and validation data, record time stamps, and repeat calibrations when drift changes the system. A numerical optimizer may fit parameters precisely even when its model is wrong. Report uncertainty, model residuals, and the actual pulse context. Characterization should support an implementation claim, not merely produce a collection of attractive numbers.

Problem 7.3. Why repeat a benchmark with simultaneous neighboring operations?

Worked solution

Crosstalk and shared-control effects may not appear when gates are tested in isolation. The intended workload may involve parallel activity.

We now apply this common language to concrete platforms, beginning with a nonlinear electrical oscillator.

Sources and further study: Knill et al., Randomized benchmarking of quantum gates.
