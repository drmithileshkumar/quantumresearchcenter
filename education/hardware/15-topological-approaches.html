What would it mean for quantum information to be protected by nonlocal physical structure?

Reading guide · preparation and approach

Starting point: Chapters 1–14; physical concepts and approximations are introduced as needed.

Anchor example: A pair of separated modes with a joint parity degree of freedom.

This lesson includes explanations, worked examples, and problems with solutions. Advanced results whose proofs are outside the chapter are identified explicitly.

15.1 Nonlocal Encoding

Suppose two logical states look identical to every sufficiently local probe. A local disturbance would then have difficulty learning which logical state was present.

15.1.1 Degeneracy and local perturbations

Topological proposals seek a low-energy space whose logical alternatives cannot be distinguished by local operations, up to finite-size corrections. A gap separates that space from unwanted excitations. This is a conditional property of a physical model, not a consequence of naming a qubit topological. The encoding, allowed disturbances, temperature, and system size all enter the protection claim.

15.1.2 Parity and encoded information

In Majorana-based proposals, spatially separated modes jointly encode fermionic occupation. A fixed total-parity constraint means that a single pair does not supply a freely controllable qubit; four ideal modes can encode one qubit within a fixed-parity sector. Logical information resides in joint parity relationships. The microscopic derivation of these modes is beyond this course; Kitaev’s wire model supplies a precise example.

Problem 15.1. Why are four ideal Majorana modes used for one fixed-parity qubit?

Worked solution

Two fermionic occupations give four basis states. Fixing their total parity leaves two alternatives. A single pair with fixed parity leaves only one, insufficient for a qubit.

15.2 Topological Operations

15.2.1 Exchange and braiding

For suitable non-Abelian excitations, exchanging their positions transforms the degenerate state space. Different exchange orders can give different transformations. Protection requires that motion preserve the gap and avoid unwanted excitations, while finite splitting must not accumulate uncontrolled phases. A trajectory drawing by itself therefore does not establish a robust operation: the physical Hamiltonian throughout the exchange matters.

15.2.2 Readout and universality

Fusion or joint-parity measurements can read encoded information and support measurement-based operations. Initialization also requires control over the relevant parity or fusion outcome. Braiding Majorana modes alone does not provide a universal gate set; additional operations or resources are needed. Their errors must be counted rather than assuming that topological protection extends automatically to a complete computation.

Problem 15.2. Does a protected braid imply a universal processor?

Worked solution

No. The generated gate set may be restricted. State preparation, readout, and any additional non-braiding gates require independent demonstrations and error budgets.

15.3 Physical Requirements

15.3.1 Gaps, poisoning, and finite size

If an unwanted excitation costs energy \(\Delta\), a simple equilibrium estimate contains a factor \(e^{-\Delta/(k_BT)}\). A large ratio suppresses thermal occupation but says nothing by itself about nonequilibrium quasiparticles. Parity-changing events, often called poisoning, can destroy the encoding. Finite separation can also split the nominally degenerate states, producing unwanted relative phases during storage and gates.

15.3.2 Evidence and limitations

Evidence must establish the claimed encoding and operations, distinguish alternative explanations of signals, and measure errors in the relevant regime. A spectral feature alone is not a demonstration of protected computation. Separate a theoretical model, evidence for its ingredients, and a working logical operation. This chapter presents design principles and their conditions; it does not assert that a complete protected universal processor has been realized.

Problem 15.3. Compare Boltzmann factors for \(\Delta/k_BT=5\) and 10.

Worked solution

They are about 0.00674 and 0.0000454. Their ratio is about 148. This calculation estimates thermal suppression only, not a measured parity lifetime.

Whatever the encoding, controls, cooling, and classical processing must support it. The next chapter counts those physical requirements.
