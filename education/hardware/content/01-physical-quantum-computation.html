1.1 Physical Requirements

A circuit says to rotate a qubit, couple it to another, and measure. A laboratory must supply a physical state, a timed interaction, and a detector. This course follows that chain and asks what must be true for the measured bit to mean what the algorithm predicts.

Predict before calculating. Two devices produce 00 and 11 equally often. Must both have prepared a Bell pair?

Compare your reasoning

No. A classical mixture has the same Z-basis populations. A phase-sensitive correlation measurement must test their coherence.

1.1.1 Encoding and initialization

Take the circuit that prepares a Bell pair: initialize two qubits in \(\ket{00}\), apply a Hadamard to the first, then a controlled-NOT. Its ideal output is \((\ket{00}+\ket{11})/\sqrt2\). A physical implementation must say what those two basis states are, how the initial state is prepared, and which field changes each amplitude. A wire in a circuit diagram does not specify any of these.

Write the prepared state as \(\ket\psi=a\ket0+b\ket1+c\ket\ell\), where \(\ket\ell\) is an unwanted device level. The normalization condition is \(|a|^2+|b|^2+|c|^2=1\). The probability of remaining in the intended qubit is \(1-|c|^2\). Even perfect control of the relative phase of \(a,b\) cannot remove population already outside that subspace. Initialization is the first physical operation in the computation, with its own success criterion.

1.1.2 Control and measurement

A control field supplies a Hamiltonian, the energy matrix that generates a change of state. During a short interval \(dt\), quantum evolution has the form \(\ket{\psi(t+dt)}\simeq[I-iH(t)dt/\hbar]\ket{\psi(t)}\). Here \(\hbar=h/(2\pi)\) converts energy times time into a dimensionless phase. Repeating short steps constructs the gate. For a constant Hamiltonian the result is \(U(t)=e^{-iHt/\hbar}\).

We will derive this description for small physical systems rather than treating a gate as an unexplained box. Measurement supplies a different connection: the quantum state changes the distribution of a recorded signal. A good population measurement alone cannot certify the Bell state. The mixture that gives 00 or 11 with equal probability has the same Z-basis outcomes. Additional measurements in rotated bases must test the relative coherence.

Problem 1.1. Does a long coherence time alone make a good processor?

Worked solution

No. Initialization, control, coupling, readout, and scalable operation must also work. A perfectly isolated but inaccessible state cannot execute a useful program.

1.2 Computational Resources

1.2.1 Physical and logical qubits

A physical qubit is the selected two-state degree of freedom of a device. A logical qubit distributes its information across physical resources according to an encoding. For example, a three-bit repetition encoding uses \(\ket{0_L}=\ket{000}\) and \(\ket{1_L}=\ket{111}\). If each physical bit flips independently with probability \(p\), majority decoding fails when two or three flip:

\[p_L=3p^2(1-p)+p^3=3p^2-2p^3.\]

The factor three counts which pair flips. At \(p=0.01\), this gives \(0.000298\), with perfect encoding and decoding assumed. This example protects against bit flips only; a phase error can still damage the encoded superposition. It illustrates the hardware trade: extra systems and operations buy protection against a specified error model. Counting logical qubits without naming that model hides what protection was actually obtained.

1.2.2 Time, connectivity, and error

Suppose an operation takes \(t_g\) and an excited state relaxes with lifetime \(T_1\). A constant probability of decay per unit time gives survival \(e^{-t_g/T_1}\), derived in Chapter 6. Its failure probability is \(1-e^{-t_g/T_1}\simeq t_g/T_1\) for a short gate. A hypothetical 50 ns gate with \(T_1=100\) μs has excited-state decay probability about \(5\times10^{-4}\). This is one contribution, not the gate’s complete error.

Connectivity also changes cost. If a pair must be brought together by routing operations, its useful interaction takes longer and uses more gates. For \(G\) independent failure opportunities of probability \(p\), the probability of at least one failure is \(1-(1-p)^G\). Real errors need not be independent or equally harmful; the expression is a model to examine, not a universal conversion from fidelity to circuit success.

Problem 1.2. What assumption permits the estimate t/T1?

Worked solution

An exponential relaxation model and a duration much shorter than T1. It estimates decay of excited-state population, not every contribution to gate infidelity.

1.3 Implementation Models

1.3.1 Gate and analog evolution

Gate and analog evolution use the same physical rule. A constant interaction \(H=\hbar\Omega X/2\) generates \(e^{-i\Omega tX/2}\). Calling this a rotation gate emphasizes a chosen angle and an interface to other gates. Letting a specified many-body Hamiltonian act for a chosen time emphasizes simulation of its dynamics.

For \(H=H_A+H_B\), short alternating pulses give \(e^{-iH_A\delta t/\hbar}e^{-iH_B\delta t/\hbar}\). Expanding both exponentials shows agreement with \(I-i(H_A+H_B)\delta t/\hbar\) to first order. Differences begin at second order and depend on whether the two terms commute. Shorter steps reduce this approximation error but require more switching. A physical comparison must include both the approximation and the errors introduced by the additional controls.

1.3.2 A device-level specification

For the Bell-pair task, record a concrete specification: basis states and register order; initial-state populations; drive frequencies, phases, and pulse areas; coupling and gate duration; then the measurement rule. Every number should carry units. We use angular frequencies such as \(\Omega\) in radians per second; a quoted value \(\Omega/(2\pi)=1\) MHz means \(\Omega=2\pi\times10^6\) s⁻¹.

The validation must match the claim. Measure populations and phase-sensitive correlations, repeat with nearby operations active, and account for rejected trials. A discrepancy between the predicted and measured distribution may come from preparation, evolution, or readout. The coming chapters separate these mechanisms by constructing their models individually. The useful final object is a prediction for an experiment, with the assumptions that make that prediction valid.

Problem 1.3. Why distinguish physical and logical qubit counts?

Worked solution

Encoding, syndrome measurement, routing, and auxiliary-state preparation consume physical resources. The mapping depends on the code and target logical error.

We begin by asking when a physical system with many states can be approximated by a qubit.

Worked model check. Three independent bits flip with probability 0.02. Find the majority-decoding failure probability, and name an error it does not protect against.

Worked solution

\(3(0.02)^2-2(0.02)^3=0.001184\). The toy repetition code protects bit values under the stated independent-flip model; it does not correct arbitrary phase errors.
