9.1 Atomic Qubits

Two ions can be far enough apart to address separately and still share a collective vibration. That vibration can carry the influence of one qubit to another.

9.1.1 Trapping and internal states

Electric fields confine charged atoms. The qubit resides in two selected internal states, such as hyperfine ground states or a ground and metastable state; it does not reside in the ion’s position. The trapping potential also has quantized motional levels separated by \(\hbar\nu\). Internal-state coherence and motional heating are therefore different properties of the same device.

9.1.2 Cooling and preparation

Optical pumping transfers population into a chosen internal state using selection rules and spontaneous emission. Laser cooling removes motional energy. Ground-state cooling becomes especially important for gates whose approximation assumes small motion. The preparation experiment should measure both the internal population and the motional distribution; a dark ion is not, by itself, proof that both are correct.

Problem 9.1. Why is an internal ground state insufficient evidence of motional cooling?

Worked solution

Internal and motional states are distinct degrees of freedom. Optical pumping can set the first while leaving a thermal mixture in the second. Sideband asymmetry or another motion-sensitive measurement is required.

9.2 Coherent Gates

9.2.1 Carrier and sideband transitions

A carrier transition changes the internal state without changing the motional quantum number. Sidebands change both: a red sideband couples \(\ket{0,n}\) to \(\ket{1,n-1}\). In the small-motion, resolved-sideband regime, its coupling is proportional to \(\eta\Omega\sqrt n\), where \(\eta\) measures the recoil relative to the oscillator length. The red sideband cannot lower \(n=0\), giving an experimental cooling diagnostic.

9.2.2 Motion-mediated entanglement

A state-dependent force can move a shared vibrational mode around a closed loop in its position–momentum plane. Different spin states accumulate different phases. When the loop closes, motion disentangles from the spins while a two-qubit phase remains. This explains why returning the bus to its starting state matters: an open loop leaves which-state information in motion and reduces spin coherence.

Problem 9.2. Compare red-sideband couplings for \(n=1\) and \(n=4\).

Worked solution

The amplitudes scale as \(\sqrt n\), so the latter is twice the former under the stated approximation. The corresponding population oscillations occur twice as fast.

9.3 Measurement and Architecture

9.3.1 Fluorescence detection

Illuminate a cycling transition coupled to one qubit state. Repeated fluorescence makes that state bright and the other dark. Photon-count overlap, unwanted pumping, and off-resonant scattering limit assignment fidelity. Measurement can disturb neighbouring ions, and cooling and reset take time. A circuit timing model must include those operations rather than counting laser gate pulses alone.

9.3.2 Transport and optical connections

Long chains acquire many closely spaced motional modes; addressing and mode control become harder. Segmented traps transport ions between regions, while optical links can connect separate modules probabilistically. Heating, laser phase noise, intensity drift, transport excitation, and photon collection each enter a different budget. Neither moving ions nor linking modules is free: compare total schedules with coherence and link waiting times.

Problem 9.3. What remains if a gate leaves distinguishable bus states?

Worked solution

Tracing out the bus multiplies the spin coherence by the overlap of those states. Perfectly distinguishable bus states erase that coherence, even if the spin populations look correct.

Neutral atoms remove the electric charge that supports trapping and Coulomb coupling. Optical forces and highly excited electronic states replace those mechanisms.
