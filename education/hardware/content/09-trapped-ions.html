9.1 Atomic Qubits

Two ions can be far enough apart to address separately and still share a collective vibration. That vibration can carry the influence of one qubit to another.

Predict before calculating. If two ion spins have the intended populations after a gate, can their shared motion still hide an error?

Compare your reasoning

Yes. A final motional state that depends on the spin branch can retain distinguishing information and reduce spin coherence.

9.1.1 Trapping and internal states

An ion has both internal electronic states and a position. Electric fields provide confinement; Coulomb forces couple the motions of different ions. Near a stable minimum, expand the potential as \(V(x)\simeq V(0)+m\nu^2x^2/2\). The constant shifts all energies equally, while the quadratic term makes motion an oscillator of frequency ν.

Quantizing it gives \(x=x_0(a+a^\dagger)\), with \(x_0=\sqrt{\hbar/(2m\nu)}\), and energies \(\hbar\nu(n+1/2)\). Internal levels supply the qubit; shared normal modes supply a mediator. These are separate degrees of freedom. For illustration, \(m=40\) atomic mass units and \(\nu/(2\pi)=1\) MHz give \(x_0\) about 11 nm. The harmonic approximation concerns motion near equilibrium and does not describe the entire trapping field.

9.1.2 Cooling and preparation

Optical pumping transfers population toward a selected internal state through driven excitation and spontaneous decay. Motional cooling is a different task. Removing one motional quantum lowers energy by \(\hbar\nu\); a red-sideband excitation followed by suitable decay can repeat this removal until the lowest motion state is favored.

For a thermal oscillator, neighboring populations have ratio \(r=e^{-\hbar\nu/(k_BT)}\). Normalization gives \(P_n=(1-r)r^n\) and mean occupation \(\bar n=r/(1-r)\). Therefore the ground-state probability is \(P_0=1/(1+\bar n)\). At \(\bar n=0.1\), it is about 0.909. Low temperature and known internal state must both be established for gates whose performance depends on motion. Other gate schemes tolerate some thermal motion but still require their own regime conditions.

Problem 9.1. Why is an internal ground state insufficient evidence of motional cooling?

Worked solution

Internal and motional states are distinct degrees of freedom. Optical pumping can set the first while leaving a thermal mixture in the second. Sideband asymmetry or another motion-sensitive measurement is required.

9.2 Coherent Gates

9.2.1 Carrier and sideband transitions

A laser contributes a spatial phase \(e^{ikx}\). Substituting the oscillator position gives \(e^{i\eta(a+a^\dagger)}\), with dimensionless \(\eta=kx_0\) for the relevant wave-vector projection. If \(\eta\sqrt{2n+1}\ll1\), expand it as \(1+i\eta(a+a^\dagger)\). This is the Lamb–Dicke approximation.

The constant term drives the internal carrier without changing n. The a term lowers motion and the \(a^\dagger\) term raises it. Since \(a\ket n=\sqrt n\ket{n-1}\), their Rabi strengths scale as \(\eta\Omega\sqrt n\) and \(\eta\Omega\sqrt{n+1}\). Selecting drive frequencies separated from the carrier by ν addresses these sidebands when the linewidths are sufficiently narrow. This derives why internal control can also manipulate the shared motion.

9.2.2 Motion-mediated entanglement

Two suitably detuned fields can produce a spin-dependent force on a shared mode. Choose the simplified model \(H/\hbar=gS(ae^{-i\delta t}+a^\dagger e^{i\delta t})\), with \(S=X_1+X_2\) and \(\delta>0\). Because the commutator of the oscillator forces is a scalar times \(S^2\), its exact propagator in this ideal model contains a displacement and phase

\[\alpha(t)=\frac g\delta(1-e^{i\delta t}),\qquad \Phi(t)=\frac{g^2}{\delta^2}(\delta t-\sin\delta t).\]

At \(T=2\pi/\delta\), the displacement vanishes and the remaining operation is \(e^{i\Phi(T)S^2}\). Since \(S^2=2I+2X_1X_2\), it includes an entangling XX rotation. Real experiments require mode selection, pulse shaping, and calibration beyond this single-mode force. Heating or a trajectory that fails to close leaves motion correlated with the qubits.

Problem 9.2. Compare red-sideband couplings for \(n=1\) and \(n=4\).

Worked solution

The amplitudes scale as \(\sqrt n\), so the latter is twice the former under the stated approximation. The corresponding population oscillations occur twice as fast.

9.3 Measurement and Architecture

9.3.1 Fluorescence detection

A state-selective cycling transition scatters many photons for one internal state and few for the other. If a bright ion yields detected photon rate R, the count in time t can be modeled as Poisson with mean \(\mu=Rt\). The chance of zero counts is \(e^{-\mu}\). A threshold of at least one count would miss a bright ion with that probability, even with no other imperfection.

A dark state also has background counts. Its false-bright probability is \(1-e^{-\mu_d}\). Increasing integration time reduces missed bright events but increases background exposure and possible state changes. These are model probabilities, not universal readout fidelities. The state-dependent scattering both produces the signal and can heat motion, so a subsequent gate may require recooling or another preparation step.

9.3.2 Transport and optical connections

Shared motion can connect many ions, but more ions also add modes and a denser frequency spectrum. A pulse designed for one mode can leave residual displacements on others. Transport-based designs instead move ions between storage, interaction, and measurement regions; motion, splitting, merging, and recooling then enter the schedule.

Optical links connect separate registers probabilistically. For per-attempt success s and attempt duration τ, independent retries take mean time \(\tau/s\), derived by summing the geometric distribution. During that wait a memory must retain coherence. Thus local gate duration and remote connection time are distinct architectural resources. Specify optical collection, reset, transport, mode heating, and simultaneous-operation errors before translating a favorable isolated-ion property into a processor claim.

Problem 9.3. What remains if a gate leaves distinguishable bus states?

Worked solution

Tracing out the bus multiplies the spin coherence by the overlap of those states. Perfectly distinguishable bus states erase that coherence, even if the spin populations look correct.

Neutral atoms remove the electric charge that supports trapping and Coulomb coupling. Optical forces and highly excited electronic states replace those mechanisms.

Worked model check. A thermal motional mode has mean occupation 0.05. Find the ground-state probability. Does it imply an equally pure internal qubit?

Worked solution

\(P_0=1/(1+0.05)\simeq0.95238\). Internal spin and motion are different degrees of freedom and require separate preparation checks.
