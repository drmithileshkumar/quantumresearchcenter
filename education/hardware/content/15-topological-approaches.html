15.1 Nonlocal Encoding

Suppose two logical states look identical to every sufficiently local probe. A local disturbance would then have difficulty learning which logical state was present.

Predict before calculating. Does an energy gap alone prove that two stored alternatives are insensitive to local disturbances?

Compare your reasoning

No. Local operators must also act nearly identically within the encoded space. Finite splitting, poisoning, and operations require separate tests.

15.1.1 Degeneracy and local perturbations

Let P project onto an ideal two-dimensional protected space. If a local disturbance O satisfies \(POP=c_OP\), its action within the space is proportional to the identity: it neither distinguishes nor rotates the two logical alternatives at that order. This is the mathematical content of the desired local insensitivity.

An energy gap separates the space from excitations, but a gap alone does not imply this condition. A perturbation can couple through excited states; finite size can split the two encoded energies; and an extended disturbance may not be local. The encoding, allowed perturbations, and boundary conditions must therefore be explicit. We use this criterion as a design principle and do not derive a microscopic topological phase from it.

15.1.2 Parity and encoded information

Ideal Majorana operators obey \(\gamma_j^\dagger=\gamma_j\) and \(\{\gamma_j,\gamma_k\}=2\delta_{jk}\). Pair two into \(f=(\gamma_1+i\gamma_2)/2\). Multiplication gives \(i\gamma_1\gamma_2=2f^\dagger f-1\), relating joint parity to fermionic occupation.

One pair has occupations zero or one, but fixing total parity selects only one of them. Four modes provide two occupations, and an even-total-parity constraint leaves \(\ket{00}\) and \(\ket{11}\), a two-state encoded space. These are joint occupations, not independently controllable local qubits. The algebra supplies an ideal encoding. Establishing modes with these properties in a physical device, maintaining parity, and accessing the correct joint measurements are separate experimental requirements.

Problem 15.1. Why are four ideal Majorana modes used for one fixed-parity qubit?

Worked solution

Two fermionic occupations give four basis states. Fixing their total parity leaves two alternatives. A single pair with fixed parity leaves only one, insufficient for a qubit.

15.2 Topological Operations

15.2.1 Exchange and braiding

In the ideal exchange algebra, one orientation of a braid is \(U_{jk}=\exp(\pi\gamma_j\gamma_k/4)\). Because \((\gamma_j\gamma_k)^2=-I\), expanding the exponential gives \(U_{jk}=(I+\gamma_j\gamma_k)/\sqrt2\). Reversing the exchange changes the sign in the exponent.

Neighboring exchange generators need not commute: \((\gamma_1\gamma_2)(\gamma_2\gamma_3)=\gamma_1\gamma_3\), whereas reversing their order gives \(-\gamma_1\gamma_3\). This is why exchange order can act on information. Physical motion must follow the intended gapped evolution, be slow enough to avoid unwanted excitation, and not accumulate uncontrolled splitting phases. The trajectory drawing identifies an exchange sequence; it does not by itself establish protection or even the required quasiparticles.

15.2.2 Readout and universality

A joint-parity measurement distinguishes eigenvalues of an operator such as \(i\gamma_1\gamma_2\). Initialization can prepare a known sector, and a collection of suitable parity measurements can support encoded operations. Local detectors and couplers still have to perform these measurements without introducing dominant faults.

Majorana braids generate a restricted gate set rather than arbitrary quantum rotations. Universal computation requires additional resources, such as suitable non-Clifford operations or state preparation, whose accuracy must be analyzed separately. We state this gate-set result from the cited theory rather than deriving the full classification. A protected exchange, an accurately measured parity, and a universal protected processor are increasingly stronger claims.

Problem 15.2. Does a protected braid imply a universal processor?

Worked solution

No. The generated gate set may be restricted. State preparation, readout, and any additional non-braiding gates require independent demonstrations and error budgets.

15.3 Physical Requirements

15.3.1 Gaps, poisoning, and finite size

If an unwanted excitation costs energy Δ, equilibrium weights contain \(e^{-\Delta/(k_BT)}\). Raising the gap-to-temperature ratio from five to ten reduces this factor from 0.00674 to 0.0000454. It says nothing by itself about nonequilibrium quasiparticles that change parity.

For finite separation L, a model can have residual splitting \(\epsilon(L)\sim\epsilon_0e^{-L/\xi}\), possibly with oscillatory factors, where ξ is a localization length. During storage time t this gives unwanted phase \(\epsilon(L)t/\hbar\). A small splitting is useful only relative to the duration and target error. Gap preservation, poisoning rate, finite-size splitting, and the additional operations needed for universality belong in the same error budget.

15.3.2 Evidence and limitations

A model should make predictions that distinguish it from competing explanations. A spectral feature can support a hypothesis about low-energy states, but does not alone establish nonlocal encoding or its operations. Test parity behavior, dependence on geometry and temperature, and reproducibility of the proposed control sequence.

The specific protection claim should be phrased as a measured relation: for example, how an encoded phase or operation error changes when the modes are separated further under matched conditions. Account for alternative sources of the same signal and for readout and postselection. This chapter develops an ideal algebra and conditional design principles. It does not claim that all microscopic ingredients, protected braids, or a complete universal implementation have been established by one observed feature.

Problem 15.3. Compare Boltzmann factors for \(\Delta/k_BT=5\) and 10.

Worked solution

They are about 0.00674 and 0.0000454. Their ratio is about 148. This calculation estimates thermal suppression only, not a measured parity lifetime.
