10.1 Atom Arrays

An atom excited to a large electronic orbit can shift the excitation energy of its neighbour. We can use that shift to make a pulse conditional.

Predict before calculating. If increasing laser power makes a gate faster, must it improve a blockade gate?

Compare your reasoning

No. Faster operation can reduce Rydberg decay while making unwanted double excitation harder to suppress.

10.1.1 Optical traps and loading

A far-detuned optical field induces an electric dipole. In a simplified two-level atom with detuning \(\Delta=\omega_0-\omega_L\) and coupling Ω, the rotating-frame matrix has levels 0 and \(\hbar\Delta\) coupled by \(\hbar\Omega/2\). For \(|\Omega/\Delta|\ll1\), the ground-connected eigenvalue shifts by \(-\hbar\Omega^2/(4\Delta)\). For positive Δ, stronger intensity lowers this energy and can confine an atom near a beam maximum.

Actual trap depths require the atom’s multilevel polarizability. Near the minimum, use the harmonic approximation of Chapter 9 to describe motion. Loading is another issue: if ten prescribed sites load independently with probability 0.6, full occupancy is \(0.6^{10}\simeq0.00605\). Imaging and rearrangement can select occupied sites rather than requiring every target to load at once.

10.1.2 Internal-state encoding

Choose two long-lived internal states as zero and one. Optical pumping supplies preparation; microwave or two-photon Raman drives supply rotations. In a Raman process, fields couple each ground state to a common excited state. At large detuning Δ, the intermediate amplitude follows the ground amplitudes approximately as \(c_e\simeq-(\Omega_1c_0+\Omega_2c_1)/(2\Delta)\).

Substituting this into the two ground-state equations produces an off-diagonal coupling of magnitude \(\hbar|\Omega_1\Omega_2|/(4|\Delta|)\), corresponding to effective Rabi strength \(|\Omega_1\Omega_2|/(2|\Delta|)\). It also produces light shifts that must be compensated. This elimination assumes weak intermediate occupation and sufficiently slow envelopes; spontaneous scattering, motional effects, and laser phase fluctuations remain physical errors.

Problem 10.1. If sites load independently with probability 0.6, what is the probability that ten prescribed sites are occupied?

Worked solution

It is 0.6¹⁰ ≈ 0.00605. This toy calculation explains why rearrangement can matter even when individual loading looks reasonably successful.

10.2 Rydberg Gates

10.2.1 Excitation and blockade

Let a resonant laser couple each atom’s state one to a Rydberg state r, and let two Rydberg excitations have interaction energy V. In the pair basis, add \(V\ket{rr}\bra{rr}\) to the sum of the two single-atom drive Hamiltonians. Define \(B=V/\hbar\). Once one atom occupies r, exciting the second is detuned by B.

The isolated unwanted transition has maximal transfer \(\Omega^2/(\Omega^2+B^2)\), by the detuned calculation of Chapter 3. Strong blockade means \(|B|\gg\Omega\), not that \(\ket{rr}\) has disappeared. In a van der Waals regime, \(V=C_6/R^6\), with \(C_6\) an energy times length⁶. Defining the blockade radius by \(|V|=\hbar\Omega\) gives \(R_b=(|C_6|/(\hbar\Omega))^{1/6}\). The interaction law is a regime-dependent atomic-physics input.

10.2.2 Conditional phase accumulation

Follow all four inputs through an ideal π–2π–π blockade sequence, using the same optical phase for the two control π pulses. Inputs with control zero see no control excitation. A target one makes a full resonant 2π rotation and gains −1. Inputs with control one gain −1 from the two control π pulses; blockade prevents the target from cycling when it is also one.

The ideal diagonal phases are therefore \((1,-1,-1,-1)\) in order 00,01,10,11. Applying Z to each qubit converts this to \((1,1,1,-1)\), a CZ gate. Finite blockade gives additional phase and leakage, and decay during Rydberg occupation breaks the ideal unitary. Stating the pulse phases and local corrections is essential: the word “blockade” specifies an interaction mechanism, not a complete gate.

Problem 10.2. For \(B=20\Omega\), bound transfer in the simple blockade model.

Worked solution

The maximum is \(1/401\approx0.00249\). It is not the total error of a Rydberg gate: decay, pulse errors, and other levels remain.

10.3 Readout and Scaling

10.3.1 Detection and atom loss

Imaging can report occupancy, internal state, or a combination, depending on the sequence. If one internal state is removed before imaging, an empty image may mean the intended removed state or an atom lost earlier. Without another measurement those events are ambiguous.

Use an explicit third outcome, loss, when the apparatus can identify it. An encoded qubit with probabilities \(p_0,p_1,p_{\rm loss}\) must retain all three in the unconditional report. A heralded erasure can be more useful to a decoder than an unknown error, but it does not restore the lost state by itself. Scattering during fluorescence can heat or eject neighboring atoms, so readout reuse and measurement isolation are separate performance questions.

10.3.2 Rearrangement and parallel control

A blockade gate balances interaction and lifetime. In a simple illustrative error model, imperfect blockade contributes \(c_1(\Omega/B)^2\), while Rydberg decay during a pulse contributes \(c_2/(\Omega\tau_r)\), where \(\tau_r\) is the relevant lifetime. Increasing Ω improves one contribution and worsens the other.

Minimizing their sum gives \(\Omega^3=c_2B^2/(2c_1\tau_r)\), within the assumptions of this simplified model. It is not a universal optimum for shaped, multilevel gates. Across an array, distances change B and simultaneous gates can interact. Include loading yield, rearrangement duration, light-shift variation, atom loss, and the allowed parallel gate sets in a system-level schedule.

Problem 10.3. Why should detected atom loss not be recorded as measurement zero?

Worked solution

That would merge an erasure with a valid state outcome and bias the data. Record occupancy or a separate loss flag and define how the computation handles it.

In a semiconductor, electrostatic confinement plays the role of the trap, while electron spin supplies the two states.

Worked model check. Double the atom separation in a C6/R6 interaction regime. If the original blockade shift was 64 times Ω, what is it afterward?

Worked solution

It falls by \(2^6=64\), leaving \(|B|=\Omega\). The isolated-transition maximum transfer rises from about \(1/4097\) to one half, so the original strong-blockade assumption fails.
