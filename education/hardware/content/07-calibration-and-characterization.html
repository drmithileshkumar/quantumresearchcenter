7.1 Calibration Experiments

A fringe oscillates while its envelope decays. Its period estimates a detuning; its envelope estimates a sequence-dependent coherence time. Extracting those quantities requires a model and a calibration strategy.

Predict before calculating. Can fitting more shots to the same model eliminate an incorrect calibration model?

Compare your reasoning

No. More shots reduce sampling fluctuations. Independent sequences and varied controls are needed to test whether the model predicts the device.

7.1.1 Spectroscopy and Rabi scans

Spectroscopy varies the drive frequency and records a response at a chosen duration. Rabi calibration instead fixes frequency near resonance and varies duration. In the detuned two-level model, the height and oscillation frequency both depend on \(\Delta\), so a large signal at one duration need not locate the exact resonance.

Use a weak frequency scan to identify a candidate transition, then time scans at several nearby frequencies to test the detuned formula. At resonance the model predicts a full oscillation height; reduced contrast can also come from preparation, readout, or relaxation. Repeating at different drive powers exposes broadening and multilevel effects. Calibration is an inference problem: say which parameters were fitted and which were independently measured.

7.1.2 Ramsey and echo

Prepare \(\ket+\) with a calibrated π/2 pulse. During the wait, the two basis amplitudes acquire a relative phase \(\Delta t\) in the drive frame. A second π/2 pulse converts that phase into a population. With the analysis pulse phase chosen for constructive return at zero delay,

\[P_0(t)=\tfrac12[1+V e^{-t/T_2^*}\cos(\Delta t+\phi_0)].\]

The ideal result follows by multiplying the two rotation matrices and the diagonal waiting evolution. V and \(\phi_0\) represent visibility and reference-phase offsets; the exponential envelope is a fitted noise model. A 2 μs fringe period gives \(|\Delta|/(2\pi)=0.5\) MHz. Measuring both cosine and sine analysis phases can resolve the sign convention that a cosine-only trace cannot.

Problem 7.1. What does a Ramsey period fail to reveal by itself?

Worked solution

It gives the magnitude of a detuning in the chosen model, but not its sign, the preparation error, or a full gate fidelity.

7.2 Process Characterization

7.2.1 State and process tomography

A qubit density matrix has trace one and is Hermitian, so it can be written \(\rho=(I+xX+yY+zZ)/2\), with real x,y,z. A Z measurement gives \(z=p_0-p_1\). Rotate the analysis basis to measure X and Y in the same way. These three expectations reconstruct all entries:

\[\rho=\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1+z&x-iy\\x+iy&1-z\end{pmatrix}.\]

Physical states require \(x^2+y^2+z^2\leq1\). Finite sampling can give an estimate outside this ball, motivating constrained fits. To characterize a process, probe enough independent input states to determine its action on the operator basis. Incorrect preparation and measurement references can be absorbed into the reconstructed process; reconstruction is not automatic certification of the gate.

7.2.2 Randomized benchmarking

Randomized benchmarking uses varied gate sequences followed by an ideal sequence inverse. Under a stationary effective depolarizing model, \(\mathcal D(\rho)=\alpha\rho+(1-\alpha)I/2\), each step shrinks the Bloch vector by α. After m steps, its length is multiplied by \(\alpha^m\), giving survival \(A\alpha^m+B\), with preparation and readout entering A and B.

For a pure input, one depolarizing step has fidelity \(\operatorname{Tr}[\rho\mathcal D(\rho)]=(1+\alpha)/2\), so the model’s infidelity is \((1-\alpha)/2\). The reduction of real randomized sequences to this model has assumptions and a separate theory. Leakage, drift, and strong gate dependence can change the interpretation. State whether the fitted unit is a Clifford, a native gate, or another sequence element.

Problem 7.2. For α=0.998, what is the single-qubit depolarizing-model error per Clifford?

Worked solution

It is (1−0.998)/2=0.001. The qualification about the model and the counted gate type is essential.

7.3 Measurement Limits

7.3.1 Preparation and measurement errors

Suppose a tomography experiment reports \(p_1=0.1\) after an intended identity. That number might describe a bad preparation, a bad gate, a detector offset, or several of them. One observation cannot identify three causes.

Useful experiments change their sensitivity: vary the sequence length to distinguish a fixed contrast loss from accumulating evolution error; change the input basis to expose phase errors; resolve extra levels to expose leakage. A benchmarking decay may separate some fixed errors into its offsets under its model, but it does not independently reconstruct every preparation and readout operation. Treat calibration uncertainty as part of the result, especially when it is comparable to the error being claimed.

7.3.2 Drift and validation

For N independent binary measurements of success probability p, the count has variance \(Np(1-p)\), so the fraction has variance \(p(1-p)/N\). Repeating a fit with more shots reduces sampling noise, but does not remove model error or slow drift.

A simple drift test interleaves two sequences rather than measuring all shots of one and then all shots of the other. Split data into time blocks, compare fitted parameters, and inspect residuals rather than only a best-fit number. Validate on pulse sequences that were not used in the fit, including simultaneous neighboring operations when the intended workload uses them. Calibration should produce a prediction that survives a changed experimental context.

Problem 7.3. Why repeat a benchmark with simultaneous neighboring operations?

Worked solution

Crosstalk and shared-control effects may not appear when gates are tested in isolation. The intended workload may involve parallel activity.

Worked model check. An effective single-qubit depolarizing fit gives alpha=0.996. Find its error per fitted sequence element. Does this establish an error per native pulse?

Worked solution

\((1-0.996)/2=0.002\). The interpretation applies to the fitted element, such as a Clifford. Converting to native pulses requires their decomposition and an additional noise model.
