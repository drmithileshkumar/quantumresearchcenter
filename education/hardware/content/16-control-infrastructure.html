16.1 Operating Conditions

A gate is a timed physical signal, and a measurement is a signal that must be interpreted. The classical equipment is part of the quantum computer.

Predict before calculating. A decoder has five-round latency. Must the measurement system pause for five rounds after every round?

Compare your reasoning

Not if processing is pipelined with sufficient throughput. An operation that needs a particular result must nevertheless wait for it.

16.1.1 Cryogenics and vacuum

A refrigerator controls a temperature stage, not automatically every device degree of freedom. For a resonator of angular frequency ω in thermal equilibrium, summing Boltzmann weights of its equally spaced levels gives \(\bar n=[e^{\hbar\omega/(k_BT)}-1]^{-1}\). A small thermal occupation requires the energy scale to exceed \(k_BT\).

For trapped particles, background collisions instead depend on gas density n, cross section σ, and relative speed v. A dilute-gas model gives rate \(\Gamma=n\sigma v\) and probability of no collision in time t equal to \(e^{-\Gamma t}\). The cross section and speed distribution depend on the actual species and gas. Cooling and vacuum specifications therefore follow from the physical error process and workload duration, rather than one universal operating number.

16.1.2 Optical and microwave delivery

A line filters the signal as well as noise. For a linear time-invariant delivery path, write \(v_{\rm device}(t)=\int h(t-t')v_{\rm source}(t')dt'\), where h is the impulse response. A sharp edge at the source may arrive rounded or ringing at the device.

A simple low-pass path obeys \(\tau\dot v_{\rm device}+v_{\rm device}=v_{\rm source}\). For a voltage step V, solving gives \(V[1-e^{-t/\tau}]\). If a gate is comparable in duration to τ, calibrating only its programmed amplitude misses its actual pulse area. Predistortion can compensate a measured response within bandwidth and dynamic-range limits, but cannot recover arbitrary frequencies removed by the path or cancel unmeasured drift.

Problem 16.1. A hypothetical cold stage has 10 μW available; each line adds 20 nW. What is the line-only upper bound?

Worked solution

The quotient is 500 lines. Other heat loads reduce that bound; it is not a complete refrigerator design.

16.2 Classical Control

16.2.1 Waveforms and synchronization

Represent a microwave waveform by two slowly varying quadratures: \(v(t)=I(t)\cos\omega_ct-Q(t)\sin\omega_ct\). Constant quadratures can be written as amplitude A and phase φ with \(I=A\cos\phi\), \(Q=A\sin\phi\). This connects digital waveform values to the rotation axes of Chapter 3.

A timing offset δt changes carrier phase by \(\omega_c\delta t\). At 5 GHz, 1 ps corresponds to about 0.0314 radians, or 1.8 degrees. The actual relevant error depends on how clocks and mixers share phase references; a common timing shift need not act like independent jitter on every pulse. Characterize the complete signal path and synchronize readout triggers and feedback as well as the drive channels.

16.2.2 Feedback and decoding

Let a syndrome result pass through acquisition, discrimination, decoding, and feed-forward stages. Their dependent latencies add. If each round produces b bits every τ seconds, the incoming stream is b/τ bits per second. A pipeline can have latency longer than τ while still accepting one round every τ.

For example, a five-round processing delay with one completed output per round does not force every round to pause. It does delay any operation that depends on that result. If the mean processing throughput is below the incoming rate, the queue grows without bound; even adequate mean throughput may have troublesome long tails. Pauli-frame tracking can postpone some physical corrections, but later noncommuting adaptive decisions can impose a real deadline.

Problem 16.2. A syndrome round takes 1 μs and decoding latency is 5 μs. Must every round wait 5 μs?

Worked solution

Not necessarily. A pipelined decoder can accept one round per microsecond while returning results five rounds later. Operations that require those results still face the latency, and sustained throughput must keep up.

16.3 System Budgets

16.3.1 Heat, bandwidth, and wiring

For one cold stage, write \(P_{\rm total}=Nq_{\rm line}+P_{\rm active}+P_{\rm background}\). Each term refers to heat deposited at that stage, not at room temperature. The admissible count satisfies \(N\leq(P_{\rm cool}-P_{\rm active}-P_{\rm background})/q_{\rm line}\).

If the hypothetical budget is 10 μW and each line contributes 20 nW, neglecting all other loads gives 500 lines. With 4 μW already committed, the same arithmetic permits only 300. Multiplexing can reduce line count while increasing bandwidth, crosstalk, or local electronics dissipation. A processor budget should list its assumptions by temperature stage and operating mode; an isolated quotient is not a cryogenic design.

16.3.2 Calibration and stability

Useful availability is the fraction of elapsed time that produces the intended data. If a cycle contains calibration time \(t_c\), acquisition \(t_a\), and other overhead \(t_o\), its duty fraction is \(t_a/(t_c+t_a+t_o)\). Fifteen minutes of calibration per hour already limits the fraction to 0.75 before other costs.

Calibrating less frequently may increase that fraction while allowing larger errors. For a simple drift model \(\delta\omega(t)=at\) after calibration, an idle interval of duration τ at age t incurs approximately phase \(at\tau\) when drift is slow during the interval. A specified phase tolerance therefore imposes a recalibration age. Real drift can be stochastic and multivariate; use time-stamped validation circuits rather than assuming a fixed maintenance interval remains valid as the machine grows.

Problem 16.3. If calibration takes 15 minutes each hour, what fraction remains for data collection?

Worked solution

At most 45/60 = 75%, before other overhead. Useful execution estimates should include this duty cycle rather than reporting only pulse time.

Infrastructure limits which operations can happen together. Architecture translates these constraints into a schedule for an entire algorithm.

Worked model check. A decoder receives 200 bits per 2 μs round. What sustained input rate is needed? Can a 10 μs latency coexist with sufficient throughput?

Worked solution

The rate is \(200/(2\times10^{-6})=10^8\) bits/s. A five-round pipeline may have 10 μs latency while accepting one complete round every 2 μs; dependent operations must still wait.
