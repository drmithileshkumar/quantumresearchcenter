5.1 Initialization

Two preparations produce overlapping detector signals. Choosing a threshold gives a bit, but it also gives mistakes. Readout is therefore a calibrated statistical decision linked to a physical disturbance.

5.1.1 Thermal preparation

For an ideal two-level system in thermal equilibrium with energy gap ΔE, the excited probability is \(p_1=1/(1+e^{\Delta E/k_BT})\). This follows from the ratio p1/p0=exp(−ΔE/kBT) and normalization. Low temperature helps when the gap is large compared with thermal energy, but unwanted excitations and incomplete equilibration can invalidate the equilibrium prediction.

5.1.2 Active reset

Active reset measures the state and conditionally applies a correction, or uses a dissipative process designed to remove excitation. Its error includes measurement mistakes, latency, residual population, and leakage. Waiting a fixed multiple of a nominal T1 is another preparation strategy, but it has a time cost and may approach a nonzero thermal excited population rather than perfect zero.

Problem 5.1. At ΔE/kBT=5, estimate thermal excited population.

Worked solution

It is 1/(1+e⁵)≈0.00669. This is an equilibrium two-level prediction, not a universal device initialization error.

5.2 Measurement Signals

5.2.1 State-dependent response

Readout may detect fluorescence, a resonator phase shift, a charge change, or a photon. In each case state information is converted into distinguishable classical signals. Amplification and averaging can improve separation, while the measurement interaction itself can disturb the state. The probability of assigning a correct bit and the probability of preserving an eigenstate are different properties.

5.2.2 Assignment and backaction

Let errors be e0=P(report 1|prepared 0) and e1=P(report 0|prepared 1). The observed probability of one is \(q=e_0+(1-e_0-e_1)p\), where p is the actual excited population before readout. If e0=e1=0.02 and p=0.8, q=0.788. Inverting this relation requires reliable calibration and increases uncertainty; it does not turn noisy single-shot labels into known correct outcomes.

Problem 5.2. If e0=e1=1/2, can the assignment relation be inverted?

Worked solution

No. The output is always a fair bit independent of p, so the detector contains no information about the population.

5.3 Readout Design

5.3.1 Integration and discrimination

Longer integration collects more signal but also permits decay during measurement, drift, or state changes induced by the readout. Optimize the total assignment error for the intended input distribution and operation. Equal-prior accuracy can be a poor measure for rare-event experiments. A threshold chosen on calibration data should be tested on separate data.

5.3.2 Repeated measurement and reuse

A quantum nondemolition readout approximately preserves the measured observable so a repeated measurement gives the same eigenvalue, absent intervening errors. High assignment fidelity alone does not establish this property. Repeated readout and reset experiments are important when a qubit must be measured many times during error correction rather than only once at the end.

Problem 5.3. Does a destructive detector with perfect labels qualify as reusable nondemolition readout?

Worked solution

No. It may identify the original state exactly while consuming the carrier. Reuse and preservation are separate requirements.
