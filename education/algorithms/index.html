Quantum Algorithms

From computational techniques to complete algorithms

About this course

Develop algorithms by specifying access, encoding useful properties, engineering interference, constructing readout, and accounting for resources. Chapters build from coherent computation and phase encoding to walks, operator transformations, evolution, and adaptive measurement.

Prerequisites: The Quantum Computing course or equivalent knowledge. Additional mathematics is developed through the problems that require it.

11 of 11 chapters contain draft lessons with worked examples and solved problems. These are teaching manuscripts in development; advanced results are stated with their assumptions and sources where a full proof lies beyond the course.

Chapters

  1. Quantum Algorithmic Thinking

    How do we turn a computational problem into a quantum procedure whose answer can actually be read?

  2. Coherent Computation

    How can a classical calculation become a reusable coherent subroutine?

  3. Phase Encoding

    How can a function change relative phases without leaving a record of its input?

  4. Fourier Methods

    How does a repeated pattern become a measurable frequency?

  5. Amplitude Manipulation

    How can a useful but unlikely outcome be made observable without knowing the answer?

  6. Quantum Walks

    How can graph structure organize useful interference?

  7. Operator Embedding

    How can a unitary circuit represent a transformation that is not unitary?

  8. Polynomial Transformations

    How can a short circuit transform an entire spectrum without reading its eigenvalues?

  9. Evolution Engineering

    How can simple available evolutions reproduce a desired dynamics?

  10. Variational Methods

    What can be learned by repeatedly adjusting and measuring a small quantum circuit?

  11. Adaptive Measurement

    When should the result of a measurement change the rest of the computation?
