Pump-Free Microwave-Optical Bell Pair Generation for Teleportation-Based Quantum Transduction

Fangxin Li et al.

arXiv:2609.16364v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 14 September 2026

Question
How can superconducting processors connect to optical networks while avoiding complications from optical pumping?
Result
Spin-mediated sources are predicted to herald microwave–optical Bell pairs at roughly 1–28 kHz, with fidelities of 0.86–0.99 depending on the platform. The pairs are intended for teleportation-based transduction.

Methods Separate resource generation from state transfer; Use an intermediate spin as an interface

Information Causality Characterizes the Set of Quantum Correlations in the Simplest Bell Scenario

Mariami Gachechiladze; Nikolai Miklin

arXiv:2609.10508v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 9 September 2026

Question
Can an information principle recover the quantum boundary in the simplest Bell experiment?
Result
Generalized information causality implies the Tsirelson–Landau–Masanes criterion on the four renormalized correlators, so it recovers the quantum boundary of that correlator projection. A separate example shows that it also excludes some correlations that macroscopic locality allows.

Methods Keep the full measurement record; Expand around zero capacity

Quantum computing · Theory and numerical analysis

Quantum error correction at ultra-low overhead

Zhide Lu; Weikang Li; Dong-Ling Deng

arXiv:2608.02773v2 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 3 August 2026

Question
Can quantum error-correcting codes combine high encoding rates, tolerable noise, and feasible neutral-atom operations?
Result
Cornucopia codes reach encoding rates just above 1/2 at distances 6 to 18 and pseudo-thresholds above 0.4% under circuit-level noise. Their 12-layer syndrome cycle is carried out by rigid moves of whole atom blocks.

Methods Design the code and its circuit together; Certify distance and count resources consistently

Quantum information · Theory

A solution to 2-copy distillability of Werner states

Jinshi Fu; Li Gao; Sang-Jun Park

arXiv:2607.21367v3 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 23 July 2026

Question
Can two copies of a Werner state yield distillable entanglement when one copy cannot?
Result
For every local dimension \(d\), a Werner state \(\rho_\alpha\) is two-copy distillable exactly when it is one-copy distillable (\(\alpha<-\tfrac12\)). States with \(-\tfrac12\le\alpha<-\tfrac1d\) are therefore NPT but not two-copy distillable.

Methods Turn distillation into an operator test; Use symmetry to isolate a boundary case

Quantum computing · Experiment

Quantum error correction with the toric code

Atom Computing and Collaborators

arXiv:2606.04079v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 2 June 2026

Question
Can neutral-atom logical memories sustain repeated error correction while replacing lost atoms?
Result
Twisted toric-code memories on 32 and 64 atoms (\([[16,1,(4,3)]]\) and \([[32,1,(6,4)]]\)) run for up to 90 and 70 syndrome cycles with atom reloading. Over four to eight cycles, the larger code has the lower logical error.

Methods Rotate data and ancilla roles; Make replenishment part of the computation
