Conservation in Every Quantum Measurement Outcome?

E. Okon; D. Sudarsky; M. Wiedemann

arXiv:2610.29804v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 21 October 2026

Question
Does including the system that prepared a superposition restore a conservation law in every individual measurement outcome, as Collins and Popescu have proposed?
Result
The authors argue that it does not, in the sense claimed. The need for a physical preparer is postulated rather than derived from the relativity of angle. The preparer never holds a definite value, so what the mechanism restores is a shifted probability distribution. A preparation that conserves one observable can disturb a noncommuting one. For energy, the preparation interaction breaks the additivity the bookkeeping relies on.

Methods Test a conservation mechanism against the whole symmetry group; Overlap factors set how broad a hidden partner must be

An entanglement band structure for general multiqubit states

Chon-Fai Kam

arXiv:2610.30040v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 21 October 2026

Question
Majorana pictured a symmetric multiqubit state as points on the sphere. Can a comparable geometric picture be given for general states, whose entanglement classes form continuous families?
Result
Projecting one qubit onto a direction \(\mathbf n\) leaves a conditional state of the others. Its entanglement invariants are the eigenvalues, called bands, of a non-Hermitian matrix \(L(u)\). For five qubits there are four bands, which meet at 24 exceptional points where the conditional state changes entanglement family. Together they form one Riemann surface of genus nine, which fixes the five-qubit entanglement class up to finitely many choices.

Methods Read SLOCC invariants from a holomorphic matrix pencil; Count exceptional points and the genus from the discriminant

Quantum computing · Theory and numerical analysis

Injection is all you need: parallel universal logic in high-rate concatenated self-dual codes

Joseph Sullivan; David Amaro; Michael A. Perlin

arXiv:2610.30087v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 21 October 2026

Question
High-rate codes store many logical qubits per block but make it hard to act on a chosen subset. How can they run parallel, addressable, universal logic?
Result
In concatenated self-dual codes with transversal global \(H\) and \(S\), any logical Clifford is injected by consuming at most 9 CSS resource states. \(T\) gates on any subset need one \(\ket H^{\otimes K}\) magic state and one CSS state, so a circuit of \(T\)-depth \(D_T\) needs at most \(12D_T\) resource states. A Hadamard parity check repeated at each concatenation level gives magic states whose undetected error is \(O(p^{2^L})\). In simulations at \(p=5\times10^{-4}\), this concatenated cultivation needed about ten times less spacetime than surface-code cultivation for the two distance-4 codes.

Methods Reduce every Clifford layer to CSS resource states; Hadamard-parity flags, and why one parity stalls at second order

Finite Classification of Transversal Diagonal Gates Beyond Stabilizer Codes

Xi He; Chao Zhang; Shilin Huang; Bei Zeng

arXiv:2610.29955v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 21 October 2026

Question
Code-preserving diagonal phases of general (nonadditive) qubit codes can vary continuously. Can the transversal diagonal gates of such codes still be classified exhaustively?
Result
Yes, at fixed code parameters. For a code of distance at least two, every projective logical action of a transversal diagonal gate has a physical representative of finite order \(q\), with \(m\mid q\) and \(q\) bounded in terms of \(n\) and \(K\) alone; \(q\le9\) for six qubits at distance three. For exact ((6,2,3)) codes, 66 candidate symmetry classes reduce to 15 realizable ones, with diagonal logical groups \(C_2\), \(C_3\) or \(C_5\). The order-five code is unique up to local unitaries and qubit permutations. It is also the shortest distance-3 qubit code whose transversal logical group cannot be conjugated into the Clifford group.

Methods Strip continuous phases that act only as logical scalars; Subset-sum sectors and moment matching as a staged filter

Quantum f-divergences from retrodiction: a unifying classical variational principle

Francesco Buscemi

arXiv:2610.29696v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 21 October 2026

Question
A classical \(f\)-divergence has several inequivalent quantum extensions. Can they all be obtained from one operational optimization?
Result
Yes. Compare a forward prepare-and-measure process with its reverse, obtained by Petz retrodiction relative to a reference prior \(\gamma\), subject to prescribed averages \(\rho\) and \(\sigma\). The minimum classical \(f\)-divergence between their complete records is attained by one spectral implementation, simultaneously for every operator-convex \(f\). A flat prior gives the standard (Petz) divergence, Umegaki for relative entropy. A prior equal to \(\rho\) or \(\sigma\) gives the maximal divergence, Belavkin–Staszewski for relative entropy, and power priors \(\gamma\propto\sigma^t\) give the geodesic family between them.

Methods Two operator-Jensen steps remove the implementation freedom; The retrodiction prior selects the quantum divergence

Question

Result
A lifting theorem produces new valid inequalities from old ones by peeling off one party’s largest Schmidt component and applying Horn’s inequalities to the other two. Starting from Klyachko’s first-row bound, at most two lifting steps reproduce every facet of the marginal polytope for local dimension up to 4, and 455 of the 466 facet classes for dimension 5.

Correlated decoding loses the surface code’s effective distance below threshold

César Hernando de la Fuente; Joonas Majaniemi; Evert van Nieuwenburg; Felix Frohnert

arXiv:2610.26785v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 19 October 2026

Question
Does two-pass correlated decoding of the surface code keep its advantage far below threshold?
Result
Not with the standard reweighting rule. Under circuit-level noise some size-\((d-1)/2\) fault configurations cause logical failures, so the logical error rate scales like \(p^{(d-1)/2}\) instead of \(p^{(d+1)/2}\). At \(d=3\) and \(p=10^{-5}\), correlated matching fails about 4.4 times as often as plain matching. Scaling the conditional edge probabilities by an error-rate-dependent factor \(\alpha^*(p)\) restores the distance scaling and a steady advantage.

Randomness Certification from Statistically Hiding Commitments

Ilya Merkulov; Rotem Arnon

arXiv:2610.25518v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 18 October 2026

Question
What cryptographic assumption lets a classical verifier certify randomness from a single untrusted quantum device, secure against quantum side information?
Result
A statistically hiding bit commitment that is computationally binding against quantum senders suffices; stronger notions such as collapse-binding are not needed. Consequently, post-quantum one-way permutations suffice. Trapdoor claw-free functions give an efficient verifier, and the quantum random-oracle model gives certification with no further assumption but an inefficient verifier.
