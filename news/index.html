Foundations of quantum mechanics · Theory

Dimension-free marginal inequalities for pure three-party states

Simon Morelli

arXiv:2610.28162v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 20 October 2026

Question
Which spectra can the three one-party marginals of a pure three-party state have, with inequalities that hold in every local dimension?
Result
A lifting theorem produces new valid inequalities from old ones by peeling off one party’s largest Schmidt component and applying Horn’s inequalities to the other two. Starting from Klyachko’s first-row bound, at most two lifting steps reproduce every facet of the marginal polytope for local dimension up to 4, and 455 of the 466 facet classes for dimension 5.

Methods Peel off the largest Schmidt component; Combine Horn triples with Abel summation

Trapped-ion-based quantum repeater node across a 17 km metropolitan fiber

K. Fabitha; T. Runggaldier; A. Winkler; F. Bernecker; J. Bate; M. Canteri; J. Helgert; T. E. Northup; V. Krutyanskiy; B. P. Lanyon

arXiv:2610.27854v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 20 October 2026

Question
Can a trapped-ion repeater node distribute entanglement over deployed city fiber rather than a laboratory spool?
Result
A two-ion node in Innsbruck entangled a photon sent through 17 km of rented municipal fiber with a photon detected locally. The Bell-state fidelity was \(81.6^{+1.9}_{-1.4}\%\) (with feed-forward simulated), at 0.69 successes per second. Storing the first ion qubit raised the success probability 13.6-fold over sending both photons without memory.

Methods Store the qubit for the fast link, not the slow one; Correct a drifting fiber by tomography and inversion

Polynomial-Time Classical Simulation of BosonSampling under Any Constant-Fraction Photon Loss

Byeongseon Go; Hyunseok Jeong

arXiv:2610.27065v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 19 October 2026

Question
Does lossy BosonSampling remain hard to simulate classically when a fixed fraction of the photons survives?
Result
Not under the conditions studied. For every fixed survival fraction \(n/N\in(0,1)\), Haar-random interferometers and \(M=\Omega(n^2N)\) modes, a classical algorithm samples within total variation distance \(\varepsilon\) of the collision-collapsed output distribution, for a \(1-\delta\) fraction of interferometers, in time \(\mathrm{poly}(M,N,\varepsilon^{-1},\delta^{-1})\). The same holds for every fixed transmission probability \(\eta\in(0,1)\).

Methods Decompose an average over random survivors; Sum over subsets with color coding

Quantum computing · Numerical study and analysis

Correlated decoding loses the surface code’s effective distance below threshold

César Hernando de la Fuente; Joonas Majaniemi; Evert van Nieuwenburg; Felix Frohnert

arXiv:2610.26785v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 19 October 2026

Question
Does two-pass correlated decoding of the surface code keep its advantage far below threshold?
Result
Not with the standard reweighting rule. Under circuit-level noise some size-\((d-1)/2\) fault configurations cause logical failures, so the logical error rate scales like \(p^{(d-1)/2}\) instead of \(p^{(d+1)/2}\). At \(d=3\) and \(p=10^{-5}\), correlated matching fails about 4.4 times as often as plain matching. Scaling the conditional edge probabilities by an error-rate-dependent factor \(\alpha^*(p)\) restores the distance scaling and a steady advantage.

Methods Count the independent faults behind a correction; Regularize the update with one error-rate-dependent parameter

Randomness Certification from Statistically Hiding Commitments

Ilya Merkulov; Rotem Arnon

arXiv:2610.25518v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 18 October 2026

Question
What cryptographic assumption lets a classical verifier certify randomness from a single untrusted quantum device, secure against quantum side information?
Result
A statistically hiding bit commitment that is computationally binding against quantum senders suffices; stronger notions such as collapse-binding are not needed. Consequently, post-quantum one-way permutations suffice. Trapdoor claw-free functions give an efficient verifier, and the quantum random-oracle model gives certification with no further assumption but an inefficient verifier.

Methods Hide a BB84 basis inside two commitment openings; Turn a predictor into two openings

A solution to 2-copy distillability of Werner states

Jinshi Fu; Li Gao; Sang-Jun Park

arXiv:2607.21367v3 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 23 July 2026

Question
Can two copies of a Werner state yield distillable entanglement when one copy cannot?
Result
For every local dimension \(d\), a Werner state \(\rho_\alpha\) is two-copy distillable exactly when it is one-copy distillable (\(\alpha<-\tfrac12\)). States with \(-\tfrac12\le\alpha<-\tfrac1d\) are therefore NPT but not two-copy distillable.

Methods Turn distillation into an operator test; Use symmetry to isolate a boundary case
