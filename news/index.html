Photon localization: Local measurements, localized states, and causality

Jan Gulla; Johannes Skaar

arXiv:2610.30473v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 21 October 2026

Question
What does it mean for a photon to be localized in quantum field theory, where there is no position operator, the vacuum is entangled across regions, and no-go theorems (Hegerfeldt, Malament, Reeh–Schlieder) seem to forbid localized particles altogether?
Result
The review argues that localization should be defined through local measurement: a state is strictly localized to a spacetime region \(\mathcal O\) if no observable outside \(\mathcal O\) can tell it from the vacuum. With this definition, single photons (and any state with a bounded photon number) cannot be strictly localized, while coherent states, squeezed states and states arbitrarily close to single photons can. On-demand sources must produce strictly localized states.

Methods Prove non-localizability from a nonvanishing vacuum matrix element; Build localized states from the polar part of a smeared field

Discrete Time Liquids

Zuo Wang; Paul M. Schindler; Marin Bukov; Marios H. Michael

arXiv:2610.30266v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 21 October 2026

Question
Time crystals break the discrete time-translation symmetry of a perfectly periodic drive. Can temporal order also emerge from noise, when the only drive is the thermal fluctuation of a cavity mode with finite coherence time?
Result
Yes. A two-dimensional classical spin lattice coupled to a thermal cavity mode locks to half the cavity frequency within a window of bath temperatures. When the cavity coherence time is short, single trajectories switch stochastically between the two period-doubled configurations. These switches, called time-solitons, destroy long-range temporal coherence while preserving local period doubling: a discrete time liquid. A one-parameter toy model with independent sign flips reproduces the full distribution of single-trajectory correlation times.

Methods Treat a narrow-band thermal mode as a random-amplitude, random-phase drive; Separate defects from decay with three nested correlation times

Normal-Form Characterization of Adaptivity and Entanglement in Pauli-Based Computation

Filipa C. R. Peres; Selman Ipek; Daniel Lovsted; Joshua Nevin; Anne Broadbent

arXiv:2610.30315v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 21 October 2026

Question
Pauli-based computation (PBC) runs a Clifford+\(T\) circuit as a sequence of commuting Pauli measurements on magic states, chosen adaptively from earlier outcomes. The adaptive tree of measurements is exponentially large. Is there a compact description of how branches differ, comparable to flow in one-way computing?
Result
Yes. Every branch of the PBC tree is obtained from the all-zero reference branch by conjugating each observable with phase gates \(S\) on the magic qubits whose earlier outcomes were 1. This normal form groups measurements into adaptive layers, gives a duality with a computation-specific one-way computation, detects unnecessary magic qubits in polynomial rather than exponential time, and allows ahead-of-time compilation. The resulting circuits use on average about 50% fewer CNOTs and 40% less quantum memory than state-of-the-art compilers on random circuits.

Methods Move outcome-dependent corrections onto the input state; Detect removable resources by checking one branch against a symmetry

Transversal syndrome extraction: A unifying construction for measuring the error syndromes of any stabilizer code

Miles D. Gorman

arXiv:2610.30279v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 21 October 2026

Question
Fault-tolerant syndrome extraction for a general stabilizer code is either deep (Shor’s method measures one stabilizer at a time with cat states) or wide (Steane’s method measures everything at once with a \(2n\)-qubit encoded ancilla). Configurable middle grounds were known only for CSS codes. Is there one for every stabilizer code?
Result
Yes. Partitioning the stabilizer generators into sets measured together, and building a matching ancilla stabilizer state from the generalized Steane ancilla, gives a family of transversal gadgets for any stabilizer code. Shor and Steane extraction appear as the two limits, so depth can be traded for width. For example, the full syndrome of an \([[8,2,3]]\) code can be measured with four layers of data–ancilla gates using at most 12 ancilla qubits. Simulated logical error rates fall between those of Shor and Steane extraction.

Methods Design ancillas so that propagated stabilizers carry the syndrome and nothing else; Interpolate between two known gadgets by deleting rows and qubits

Pure states are optimal for total energy-constrained quantum channel discrimination

Christos N. Gagatsos

arXiv:2610.30295v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 21 October 2026

Question
When discriminating two bosonic channels with a probe of fixed mean photon number and no ancilla, is a pure probe always optimal? Convexity suggests yes, but the pure components of a mixed probe generally have different photon numbers, so the best component may break the energy budget.
Result
Yes. Every state of mean photon number \(\bar n\) is a mixture of pure states each with mean photon number exactly \(\bar n\), including for infinite rank. Combined with Lieb concavity, this shows that for any two channels some equal-energy pure probe does at least as well as any mixed probe. This holds for the quantum Chernoff exponent, for every Chernoff quantity \(Q_s\), and for the single-shot error probability.

Distributed dual-comb spectroscopy

Haowei Shi; Kartikeya Arora; Zheshen Zhang; Quntao Zhuang

arXiv:2610.30462v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 21 October 2026

Question
Quantum-enhanced dual-comb spectroscopy uses squeezed or entangled frequency combs to beat shot noise over a broad band, and distributed quantum sensing uses entanglement across sites to estimate global quantities. Can one combine both, estimating weighted averages of absorption across many samples and many frequencies, with a single quantum light source?
Result
Yes. Each sample sits in a Mach–Zehnder interferometer with a bright classical comb in one port and a share of a single two-mode-squeezed comb, split \(K\) ways by a passive beam splitter, in the dark port. With splitter weights matched to the target weights, the variance of every weighted-average estimator falls by the squeezing gain \(G\). Splitters that do not depend on frequency force a trade-off between gases with different targets: for orthogonal targets the guaranteed advantage is \(2G/(G+1)\), at most 3 dB.

Question

Result

Mixed State Deep Thermalization under Generic Quantum Errors

Xie-Hang Yu; Pavel Kos

arXiv:2610.31580v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 22 October 2026

Question
After chaotic dynamics, the states of a subsystem conditioned on measuring the rest follow a universal (Haar) distribution. What distribution emerges when local errors make those conditional states mixed?
Result
A universal one fixed by the error alone. The late-time ensemble of conditional states is the channel-induced ensemble, \(\tilde\rho=XMX^\dagger\) with \(X\) a complex Gaussian (Ginibre) matrix and Born weight \(\operatorname{Tr}XMX^\dagger\). Here \(M\) is the normalized Choi state of the error, and only its spectrum matters. This is derived for dual-unitary circuits, global Haar dynamics and local Haar circuits at large local dimension, and checked numerically in qubit circuits and kicked Ising dynamics.

Matthew Can; Mark M. Wilde

arXiv:2610.31501v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 22 October 2026

Question
How much entanglement can two laboratories obtain from one use of a noisy channel or one copy of a shared noisy state, and can the answer be expressed as a binary hypothesis-testing cost?
Result
An \((m,\varepsilon)\) entanglement generation or one-way distillation protocol exists whenever \(e_{d,m}(1-e_{d,m}/dm)\le\varepsilon\). Here \(e_{d,m}\) is the optimal error for testing \(\rho_{AB}\) against an explicit alternative \(B_{d,m}\propto dI_A\otimes\rho_B-\rho_{AB}\). Question
Majorana pictured a symmetric multiqubit state as points on the sphere. Can a comparable geometric picture be given for general states, whose entanglement classes form continuous families?
Result
Projecting one qubit onto a direction \(\mathbf n\) leaves a conditional state of the others. Its entanglement invariants are the eigenvalues, called bands, of a non-Hermitian matrix \(L(u)\). Question
Code-preserving diagonal phases of general (nonadditive) qubit codes can vary continuously. Can the transversal diagonal gates of such codes still be classified exhaustively?
Result
Yes, at fixed code parameters. For a code of distance at least two, every projective logical action of a transversal diagonal gate has a physical representative of finite order \(q\), with \(m\mid q\) and \(q\) bounded in terms of \(n\) and \(K\) alone; \(q\le9\) for six qubits at distance three. Francesco Buscemi

Question
A classical \(f\)-divergence has several inequivalent quantum extensions. Can they all be obtained from one operational optimization?
Result
Yes. Compare a forward prepare-and-measure process with its reverse, obtained by Petz retrodiction relative to a reference prior \(\gamma\), subject to prescribed averages \(\rho\) and \(\sigma\).
