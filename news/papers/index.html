Bargmann-invariant witness of the graviton commutator

Hiroki Matsui

arXiv:2610.35253v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 26 October 2026

Question
Experiments proposed to show that gravity is quantum look for gravitationally induced entanglement or decoherence, but Newtonian entangling phases can be mimicked by assigning a classical field to each branch, and decoherence can be mimicked by classical noise. Is there an interference observable that no branchwise-definite classical gravitational field can reproduce?
Result
Yes. For matter coupled linearly to linearized gravity, each branch coherence factorizes exactly into a conservative phase, the characteristic functional of the graviton state, and a phase from the field commutator. For two interferometers, the phase \(\mathfrak B\) of a four-cycle Bargmann invariant cancels everything except the commutator: it is independent of the graviton state and of all Newtonian interactions, and it vanishes for every branchwise-definite classical model. It is bounded by which-path decoherence, \(\mathfrak B^2\le16\,\Gamma_X\Gamma_Y\).

Methods A cycle of coherences that cancels everything classical; Bounding a commutator signal by decoherence

Self-Correcting 3D Surface Code with Growing Local Dimension

Jinkang Guo

arXiv:2610.35099v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 26 October 2026

Question
A self-correcting quantum memory keeps information for a time growing exponentially in a power of its size under local thermal dynamics, with no active correction. The 4D toric code achieves this, but in three dimensions a no-go theorem for fixed local dimension and the string-like logical operator of the 3D surface code stand in the way. Does allowing the local dimension \(q\) to grow with system size help?
Result
Yes. Changing only the Pauli powers of the 3D surface code along one axis, so that its string-like logical operator carries powers \(1,2,4,\dots\) modulo \(q\), gives a translation-invariant code in which, with odd \(q\ge cL^3\) and below a critical temperature, both error sectors have memory time at least \(e^{\Omega(N^{1/3})}\) under single-qudit Metropolis dynamics. Numerics suggest \(\log t_{\rm mem}\sim\min\{q,N^{1/3}\}\), so much smaller \(q\) may suffice.

Methods Weighted chain complexes: change powers, keep supports; Energy against entropy, with a Lee-weight Boltzmann sum

Distillable entanglement is continuous

Lukas Schmitt; David Sutter

arXiv:2610.35722v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 26 October 2026

Question
Distillable entanglement, the best rate at which copies of a state can be turned into Bell pairs by local operations and classical communication, is defined operationally and has no single-letter formula. It was known to be lower semicontinuous and continuous on the interior of distillable states, but not continuous in general. Is it continuous everywhere, with an explicit bound?
Result
Yes. For states within trace distance \(\varepsilon<1/16\), \[|E_D(\rho)-E_D(\sigma)|\le4\sqrt\varepsilon\log d+4h(\sqrt\varepsilon),\] with \(d\) the smaller local dimension. The same bound holds for entanglement cost. The proof builds an explicit LOCC protocol that converts copies of \(\sigma\) into copies of \(\rho\) using a number of ebits per copy that vanishes as the states approach each other.

Methods Continuity by a cheap conversion protocol; Locating rare outcomes with Grover search instead of measuring every copy

Quantum-enhanced metrology with classical shadows

Luca Ion; Manuel Gessner

arXiv:2610.35632v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 26 October 2026

Question
Reaching the best precision in quantum metrology usually requires measuring a specific, often complicated observable tailored to the probe and encoding. Can one instead measure every qubit in random Pauli bases, as in classical shadows, and still estimate a parameter well, and how much entanglement advantage survives?
Result
Yes, with a quantified cost. Combining all Pauli moments up to weight \(K\) from one randomized data set by generalized least squares gives variance \(1/(n\mathcal I)\), where the shadow information \(\mathcal I\) has a closed form. Separable probes reach at most \(\mathcal I=N/3\), so exceeding it witnesses entanglement, and no probe exceeds \(2N^2/(N+2)<2N\). Entanglement therefore gives a constant-factor gain, at most 6, not Heisenberg scaling: about 15% for twisted probes of 12 qubits.

Exact Identification of Quantum Correlations in the Minimal Bell Scenario

Liang-Liang Sun; Jie Wang; Sixia Yu

arXiv:2610.34364v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 25 October 2026

Question
In the simplest Bell experiment, two parties with two binary measurements each, the extreme points of the quantum set are known, but deciding whether a given list of eight statistics is quantum still requires searching over mixtures of many of them or climbing a semidefinite hierarchy that converges only in the limit. Is there an exact, finite test?
Result
Yes. A behaviour is quantum if and only if it is a mixture of a single behaviour produced by projective measurements on a pure two-qubit state and a local (classical) behaviour. The key step is a proof that every Bell functional with a strict quantum advantage has exactly one quantum maximizer. Membership then reduces to a system of trigonometric equations in five angles, and every quantum behaviour can be realized in dimension \(6\times6\).
