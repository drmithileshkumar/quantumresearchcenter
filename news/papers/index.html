Inevitability of the Petz transpose map

Jacopo Surace; Chenxu Li; Francesco Buscemi

arXiv:2610.40158v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 29 October 2026

Question
Given a prior and a stochastic model, Bayes’ rule fixes the reverse conditional probabilities. A quantum state and a channel do not define a joint state as unambiguously, so several candidates for a quantum Bayes’ rule exist. Do consistency requirements on reversing a channel, with no appeal to a joint state or a figure of merit, single out one rule?
Result
For channels between finite-dimensional full matrix algebras, exactly one retrodiction assignment is involutive, respects sequential and parallel composition, and inverts unitaries: the Petz map \(\mathcal R_{\pi\to\sigma}[\Phi](X)=\sqrt\pi\,\Phi^\dagger(\sigma^{-1/2}X\sigma^{-1/2})\sqrt\pi\). Dropping involutivity leaves a family of doubly rotated Petz maps, and adding continuity in the prior reduces it to the one-parameter rotated Petz maps.

What Can Be Computed with Indefinite Causal Structure?

Omri Shmueli

arXiv:2610.40573v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 29 October 2026

Question
A quantum circuit, even in superposition, still has a causal order between its gates. Process matrices keep ordinary quantum mechanics locally and drop the global order. Earlier work placed polynomial-time process matrix computation between BQP and PP and left open whether it exceeds BQP. Does it, and does it still do so when the process is required to be unitary at every level?
Result
Process matrix computation solves every problem in NP in polynomial time, using purifiable processes whose generated channels are unitary but whose generating circuits are not. Requiring pure processes, the paper gives explicit unitary generators that solve unique-sink orientation (USO) with one use of the generated channel and probability 1. If USO has no quantum polynomial-time algorithm, pure indefinite causal order goes beyond BQP within the formalism.

Question
Linear combination of Hamiltonian simulation (LCHS) writes \(\mathrm e^{-tA}\) with \(A=X+\mathrm iY\) and \(X\succeq0\) as an integral of unitary evolutions, weighted by a kernel. Question
Experiments with levitated nanoparticles aim to show that a weak force, and eventually gravity, entangles the motion of two particles. Producing the entangled state is only half the task: it has to be told apart from separable states using a finite number of noisy measurements, taken one run at a time. How many measurements, and how much laboratory time, does certification need, and which protocol reaches the weakest forces?
Result

Qubit-environment entanglement in pure dephasing dynamics for mixed initial states

Małgorzata Strzałka; Nico Peters; Laura Ares; Jan Sperling; Katarzyna Roszak

arXiv:2610.36936v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 27 October 2026

Question
When a qubit only dephases, the joint qubit–environment state for a pure initial qubit is separable exactly when the environment ends up in the same state whichever pointer state the qubit is in, \(\hat R_{00}(t)=\hat R_{11}(t)\). This test made it possible to tell decoherence by entanglement from decoherence by classical correlations, and even to detect the difference on the qubit alone. Real qubits are already partly mixed. Does the test survive, and does entanglement then evolve as it does for pure qubits?
Result
If \(\hat R_{00}(t)=\hat R_{11}(t)\), the joint state stays separable, and zero-discord, for any initial qubit coherence. Otherwise, the family of minors that gave the pure-state criterion becomes only a witness: a negative minor proves entanglement, but nonnegative minors prove nothing. A Schur complement reduces the exact partial-transpose test to a matrix \(\hat D\) of the environment’s dimension, whose diagonal entries are those minors. Mixed qubits show behaviour impossible for pure ones: a separable initial period followed by sudden birth of entanglement, death and rebirth, and no entanglement at all during an entangling evolution when the qubit is strongly mixed. In a model of an NV-centre spin with up to five nuclear spins, the minors miss entanglement, sometimes for the whole evolution.

Quantum State Preparation for Complex-Valued Amplitudes

Fred Sun

arXiv:2610.36680v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 27 October 2026

Question
Quantum algorithms often compute numbers into binary registers with reversible arithmetic, but then need those numbers as amplitudes. For a complex \(z=x+iy\), the usual route encodes \(x\) and \(y\) separately and adds them with a linear combination of unitaries, which fixes the normalisation by the ranges of \(x\) and \(y\). How can \(z/\alpha\) be loaded coherently, for inputs in superposition, with a normalisation set by \(|z|\) itself?
Result
The procedure computes the magnitude \(r'\) and argument \(\phi'\) of \(z\) reversibly with CORDIC, which uses only shifts and additions. It then encodes \(r'/\alpha\) with an exact binary-to-amplitude routine, applies one phase gate per bit of \(\phi'\) to obtain \(e^{i\phi'}\), and uncomputes the arithmetic. Question
Every key distribution protocol has to limit what escapes the users’ laboratories. In a prepare-and-measure protocol this cannot be strict, since the source must send out a signal that depends on Alice’s private input. The minimal assumption is therefore a bound on how much the emitted systems, intended signal and side channels together, reveal about the input. Can that single number replace a model of the source’s states, dimension and energy, with nothing assumed about Bob’s measurement device?
Result
Yes, against collective attacks. The leakage bound states that the optimal number of correctly guessed inputs, out of \(n\) sent once each, is at most \(G\), with \(G=2\) meaning one bit. To estimate the average of a bounded \(D\times D\) matrix-valued function in operator norm, \(O(\varepsilon^{-2}\log(2D/\delta))\) independent samples suffice, but each costs \(m\) fresh random bits. arXiv:2610.37347v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 27 October 2026

Question
A quantum channel can destroy the incompatibility of measurements but never create it. Which channels preserve the incompatibility of every finite family of measurements? Chitambar and Zhang conjectured that, when the input is larger than the output, such a channel must carry a noiseless copy of its output space, and Uola and coauthors asked whether every dimension has a measurement family that is incompatible on the full space but compatible on every proper subspace.

Both questions are settled.
