Reset-Enhanced Dissipative Quantum Metrology

Silan Li; Qingjun Tong; Ruicheng Bao

arXiv:2610.31249v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 22 October 2026

Question
Dissipation in open quantum sensors usually washes out parameter information. Can the simplest dissipative control, a channel that resets the probe to a fixed state at rate \(r\), increase the quantum Fisher information in steady-state and transient sensing without changing the Hamiltonian or adding a coherent drive?
Result
Sometimes, and the paper says exactly when. An exact identity for \(\partial_rF\) gives a first-order test for local enhancement. For a thermal qubit reset to its ground state, steady-state thermometry improves if and only if \(0<\beta\omega<\ln(2+\sqrt5)\) and \(r\) is below a threshold, with optimum \(r_{\rm opt}=\gamma(2+\sqrt5-e^{\beta\omega})/(e^{\beta\omega}-1)\). For diagonal dynamics, reset cannot change the leading short-time Fisher information of a static parameter, but it raises elapsed-time information immediately, roughly as \(r^2\). For frequency estimation with reset to \(|+\rangle\), the gain at \(t=1/\gamma\) is about 7 times, 91% of it from the coherence (eigenvector) part.

The paper in detail

The probe evolves under a Lindbladian \(\mathcal L_\theta\) with biorthogonal modes \(\mathcal L_\theta R_j=\lambda_jR_j\) and a unique steady state. Reset adds \(r(\rho_\delta\operatorname{Tr}\rho-\rho)\). The new steady state is exactly \(\rho^{\rm ss}_{\theta,r}=\rho^{\rm ss}_\theta-\sum_{j\ge2}\tfrac{rd_j}{\lambda_j-r}R_j\) with \(d_j=\operatorname{Tr}(L_j^\dagger\rho_\delta)\). Reset therefore mixes non-stationary modes into the steady probe, weighting slow modes most. For any smooth full-rank family, \(\partial_rF=2\operatorname{Tr}[(\partial_\theta\partial_r\varrho)\mathbb L]-\operatorname{Tr}[(\partial_r\varrho)\mathbb L^2]\), with \(\mathbb L\) the symmetric logarithmic derivative, for steady and transient states alike.

When the dynamics stays diagonal in a fixed basis, populations obey \(\mathbf p(t)=\mathbf p_0+t[W_\theta\mathbf p_0+r(\mathbf p_\delta-\mathbf p_0)]+O(t^2)\). For a static parameter, the leading Fisher information \(t^2\sum_i[(\partial_\theta W)\mathbf p_0]_i^2/(\mathbf p_0)_i\) contains no \(r\), so any gain needs time to build up. For elapsed-time estimation, the instantaneous Fisher information is \(\sum_i[W\mathbf p_0+r(\mathbf p_\delta-\mathbf p_0)]_i^2/(\mathbf p_0)_i\), which depends on \(r\) at \(t=0\).

For a thermal qubit with bosonic rates and ground-state reset, \(p^{\rm ss}=\gamma/[\gamma(e^{\beta\omega}+1)+r(e^{\beta\omega}-1)]\), which gives a closed-form steady Fisher information and the exact advantage window. Outside the high-temperature window, reset only pins the probe near a temperature-blind state. With a temporary reset pulse, transient thermometry reaches a high-information window earlier, and for suitable \(r\) can exceed the equilibrium Fisher information for a while. Strong reset suppresses temperature susceptibility. Time-estimation Fisher information jumps by more than two orders of magnitude just after reset switches on, then decays as the state nears its new fixed point.

For frequency estimation, an amplitude-damped qubit is reset to \(|+\rangle\). The populations do not depend on \(\omega\), so all information sits in the coherence \(c_{\omega,r}(t)\), and \(F_\omega=4|\partial_\omega c|^2+[\partial_\omega|c|^2]^2/(p(1-p)-|c|^2)\). Without reset \(F^{(0)}_\omega=4|c_0|^2t^2e^{-\gamma t}\) decays as coherence is lost. Reset replenishes the coherent modes that carry the phase. At \(t=1\) the optimum \(r\approx1.73\gamma\) gives a gain of 6.96, dominated by the eigenvector term \(s^2|\partial_\omega\hat{\mathbf s}|^2\), so energy-basis readout cannot access it.

Transferable methods

  1. Response of the QFI to a control parameter through the SLD

    Differentiating the Fisher information with respect to any control and using the self-adjointness of the Jordan product removes the derivative of the SLD. Only derivatives of the state and the SLD at the operating point are needed.

    With \(\mathcal J_\rho(X)=\tfrac12\{\rho,X\}\) and \(\partial_\theta\varrho=\mathcal J_\varrho(\mathbb L)\), \(F=\operatorname{Tr}[(\partial_\theta\varrho)\mathbb L]\) gives \(\partial_rF=\operatorname{Tr}[(\partial_r\partial_\theta\varrho)\mathbb L]+\operatorname{Tr}[\mathbb L\,\mathcal J_\varrho(\partial_r\mathbb L)]\). Differentiating the SLD equation gives \(\mathcal J_\varrho(\partial_r\mathbb L)=\partial_r\partial_\theta\varrho-\tfrac12\{\partial_r\varrho,\mathbb L\}\), and trace cyclicity yields \(\partial_rF=2\operatorname{Tr}[(\partial_\theta\partial_r\varrho)\mathbb L]-\operatorname{Tr}[(\partial_r\varrho)\mathbb L^2]\).

    At \(r=0\) in the steady state, \(\partial_r\rho^{\rm ss}=\rho_1=-\sum_{j\ge2}d_jR_j/\lambda_j\), so the first-order test uses only the unreset modes and the target overlaps.

    Editorial check: for the thermal qubit, the closed form \(F^{\rm ss}(\beta;s)\) matches a finite-difference Fisher information of \(p^{\rm ss}\) to \(10^{-10}\). Scanning \(s=r/\gamma\) gives optima 3.988, 0.883, 0.059 and 0 at \(\beta\omega=0.5\), 1.0, 1.4 and 1.5, matching \(r_{\rm opt}\), with no gain beyond \(\ln(2+\sqrt5)=1.4436\). The gain at \(\beta\omega=0.5\) is 2.48.

    How to reuse it

    For any control knob in a sensing protocol (a dissipation rate, detuning or measurement strength), this identity gives the gradient of the QFI without solving for \(\partial_r\mathbb L\), which suits gradient-based protocol design. It holds on fixed-rank families; at rank changes use one-sided limits or the minimal SLD.

  2. Short-time expansion separating static parameters from time

    Expanding to first order in \(t\) shows which Fisher information can feel a modified generator at once. Information about a static parameter must accumulate through \(\partial_\theta W\), while a clock’s information is the squared drift itself, which reset changes at \(t=0\).

    With \(\mathbf p_0\) and \(\mathbf p_\delta\) independent of \(\theta\), \(\partial_\theta\mathbf p(t)=t(\partial_\theta W)\mathbf p_0+O(t^2)\), which has no \(r\) at leading order, so \(F_\theta=t^2\sum_i[(\partial_\theta W)\mathbf p_0]_i^2/(\mathbf p_0)_i+O(t^3)\). By contrast \(\partial_t\mathbf p(0)=W\mathbf p_0+r(\mathbf p_\delta-\mathbf p_0)\), so \(F_t(0)=\sum_i[W\mathbf p_0+r(\mathbf p_\delta-\mathbf p_0)]_i^2/(\mathbf p_0)_i\), quadratic in \(r\) once the reset drift dominates.

    Coherent counterpart: when populations are blind to \(\omega\), the information is \(F_\omega=4|\partial_\omega c|^2+[\partial_\omega|c|^2]^2/(p(1-p)-|c|^2)\). Without reset \(c=c_0e^{(i\omega-\gamma/2)t}\), so \(\partial_\omega c=itc\) and \(F^{(0)}_\omega=4|c_0|^2t^2e^{-\gamma t}\).

    Editorial check: evaluating the paper’s formula for its \(\rho_0\) gives \(F^{(0)}_\omega=0.00828\) at \(t=1\). Scanning \(r\in[0,5]\) finds the optimum at \(r=1.732\) with gain 6.96, matching Fig. 2(d).

    How to reuse it

    Before designing a control to boost a sensor, expand to first order in time. If the parameter enters only through the generator, nothing that changes the initial drift helps at leading order. For clock-type tasks, engineering the drift helps immediately. The argument assumes diagonal dynamics in a fixed basis and full-support initial populations.

Context & limitations

Why it matters. Reset is easy to implement by optical pumping or measurement and feedback. The paper gives exact windows rather than a blanket claim, and a diagnostic computable from the unreset Liouvillian modes.

Limitations. Only qubit examples are worked out. The identity is local in \(r\), so finite-\(r\) advantage needs the integrated response. Reaching the frequency bound needs phase-sensitive measurements. The time, energy and imperfection costs of reset are not included. Large gain ratios occur where the baseline Fisher information is small, so absolute values matter.
