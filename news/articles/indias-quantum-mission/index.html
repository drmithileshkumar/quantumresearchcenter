India’s National Quantum Mission, explained

Computing is one part of a programme spanning communication, sensing, and the materials behind quantum devices.

By Quantum Research Center26 October 20261 min read

Background reading · An introduction to the ideas behind the news.

A programme across four fields

India launched its National Quantum Mission in April 2023, with ₹6,003.65 crore sanctioned for 2023–24 to 2030–31. Its scope includes quantum computing, communication, sensing and metrology, and materials and devices.

Where the work is organized

The government’s science and technology portal describes thematic hubs for computing at IISc Bengaluru, communication at IIT Madras with C-DOT, sensing and metrology at IIT Bombay, and materials and devices at IIT Delhi. The hub-and-spoke structure connects institutions and industry around these areas.

How to read future updates

A funding commitment, a new facility, a laboratory result, and a deployed product mark different stages of progress. Ask what was announced, what has been demonstrated, and what evidence supports the claimed benefit.

This is background on the programme, not an assessment that its technical targets have been met. Future coverage can follow individual projects and compare their results with their stated goals.
