New York maps its quantum sector behind a $360 million commitment

A state-commissioned study ranks New York second in the US for quantum activity and estimates the economic return on its hubs. Which parts are funded, which are planned, and which are projections.

By Quantum Research Center28 October 20262 min read

What was announced

On 23 October 2026 New York Governor Kathy Hochul released a statewide study of the quantum technology sector, commissioned by the state’s science and technology division NYSTAR and prepared by The Quantum Insider. It accompanies a state commitment of $360 million: $300 million for a Quantum Research and Innovation Hub at SUNY Stony Brook and $60 million, in the fiscal 2027 budget, for regional hubs.

Sources: [1]

What the study found

The study counts 134 operating companies and organizations, 219 research groups and 734 patent families across ten regions of the state, and ranks New York second to California in quantum-related businesses, research groups and patents. It describes New York as one of two states with activity across the whole chain from research and manufacturing to applications, with strengths in semiconductors, optics and photonics, and potential users in finance, healthcare and manufacturing. Its main recommendation is better coordination between researchers, manufacturers and customers.

Sources: [1]

Funded, planned and projected

The Stony Brook hub was first announced in September 2025 as a 150,000-square-foot facility targeted for 2029. Up to four regional commercialization hubs, each with a startup incubator and up to $15 million, were proposed in the 2026 State of the State; the October release says the state is preparing to establish them but names no locations.

The study’s economic estimates, $2.3 billion to $5 billion in activity and 7,200 to 10,100 job-years through 2036, are projections for the full $360 million, not results. The release also lists federal and private investments in the state that are separate from the state money, including a $1 billion CHIPS and Science Act award for a quantum foundry in Albany matched by $1 billion from IBM, and $375 million in federal funding for GlobalFoundries’ quantum manufacturing.

Sources: [1], [2], [3]

How to read it

State programmes like this one, and national ones such as India’s mission, are best judged by what gets built and used: facilities completed, companies started and customers served. Counts of patents and research groups describe a starting position. The study itself was produced by an industry media and analysis firm under a state contract, so its rankings are a useful map rather than an independent audit.
