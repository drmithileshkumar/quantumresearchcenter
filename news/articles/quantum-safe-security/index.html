Quantum-safe security starts with ordinary computers

Why preparing for quantum computers means changing the mathematics behind digital security.

By Quantum Research Center26 October 20261 min read

Background reading · An introduction to the ideas behind the news.

The change already has a starting point

On 13 August 2024, NIST announced approval of three post-quantum cryptography standards. One provides a way for communicating parties to establish a shared secret; two concern digital signatures, which help verify who signed data and whether it has changed.

What does “post-quantum” mean?

These methods run on conventional computers. Their mathematics is designed to withstand attacks from both conventional and future quantum computers. That is different from sending quantum states through a special communication link.

Why prepare ahead of time?

A sufficiently capable quantum computer could undermine widely used public-key cryptography. Information intercepted today may still be sensitive when a future attacker can decrypt it, and replacing cryptography across existing systems takes time.

What the milestone does—and does not—show

A published standard gives implementers a common specification. It does not mean every service has adopted it, or that an encryption-breaking quantum computer has been built. When reading a rollout announcement, look for which systems changed and what independent testing was performed.
