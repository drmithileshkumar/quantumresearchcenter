Foundations of quantum mechanics · Theory and numerical analysis

Local Dephasing Sustains Continuous Time Crystals in a Spin-1/2 Ensemble

Zhen-huan Yang; Dan-Bo Zhang

arXiv:2610.31230v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 22 October 2026

Question
Pure dephasing destroys single-spin coherence and is normally counted as loss. In a fully connected ferromagnetic spin-\(1/2\) ensemble with local decay, can dephasing instead be what keeps a collective oscillation going, a continuous time crystal under time-independent dynamics?
Result
Yes. An exact operator identity shows that dephasing feeds energy into the transverse interaction, which local decay removes with a delay of order \(1/\Gamma_1\). In homogeneous mean field there is no periodic orbit without dephasing, and on a periodic orbit the delayed feedback exactly pays for the transverse damping. Liouvillian spectra for \(N=80\)–\(300\) show oscillatory decay rates falling as \(N^{-0.32}\) and \(N^{-0.46}\), with frequencies approaching the mean-field values.
The paper in detailSetting, main result, and open questions

The model is \(H=\tfrac{h_z}{2}\sum_i\sigma_i^z-\tfrac{J}{N}\sum_{i<j}\sigma_i^x\sigma_j^x\), with each spin subject to decay \(\gamma\mathcal D[\sigma_i^-]\) and pure dephasing \(\gamma_\phi\mathcal D[\sigma_i^z]\), so \(\Gamma_1=\gamma\) and \(\Gamma_2=\gamma/2+2\gamma_\phi\). In homogeneous mean field the Bloch vector obeys nonlinear Bloch equations. At \(J=h_z\approx105\,\Gamma_1\) and from the same initial state, the magnetization relaxes when \(\gamma_\phi=0\) and oscillates indefinitely when \(\gamma_\phi=1.5\,\Gamma_1\). Two symmetry-related fixed points \(F_\pm\) exist when \(2h_zJ>h_z^2+\Gamma_2^2\), and linearization shows they are always stable. The limit cycle therefore does not come from a Hopf bifurcation but coexists with them, and the outcome depends on the basin of the initial state.

The mechanism comes from the shifted energy \(Q_N=2H/N+h_zI\). Because it commutes with \(H\), only the dissipators change it, and \(\mathcal L^\dagger Q_N=-\Gamma_1Q_N+4J\gamma_\phi U_N\), where \(U_N\) is the transverse pair correlation. Ferromagnetically aligned transverse spins lower the interaction energy, and dephasing destroys that alignment, so it raises the energy. In mean field \(\dot Q=-\Gamma_1Q+4J\gamma_\phi m_x^2\). Eliminating \(m_y\) and \(m_z\) leaves a damped nonlinear oscillator for \(m_x\) with an extra force \(-2JQm_x\). Without dephasing \(Q\) decays to zero, and an energy argument over one period forces \(\dot m_x\equiv0\), so no periodic orbit exists.

Charging alone does not explain motion, because charging and loss can also balance at a fixed point. What matters is that \(Q\) follows \(m_x^2\) through a memory kernel \(e^{-\Gamma_1s}\), so each harmonic lags by \(\arctan(\nu/\Gamma_1)\). The lag makes the orbit enclose an area in the \((m_x^2,Q)\) plane, and the feedback work around this loop equals \(\langle\dot Q^2\rangle/(4\gamma_\phi)>0\). It exactly balances the damping \(2\Gamma_2\oint\dot m_x^2\,dt\). An instantaneous response would retrace a line and do no work.

Beyond mean field, the Liouvillian is diagonalized in Dicke blocks for \(N=80\)–\(300\). The odd and even oscillatory branches become longer lived (\(\kappa\propto N^{-0.323}\), \(N^{-0.458}\)), their frequencies approach \(\Omega\) and \(2\Omega\), and the per-spin oscillatory weights in stationary correlations stay of comparable size. Projecting the identity onto an eigenmode gives \(q_k/u_k=4J\gamma_\phi/(\Gamma_1+\lambda_k)\), the quantum version of the mean-field lag. Semiclassical trajectories at \(N=10^5\) show oscillatory episodes interrupted by stays near \(F_\pm\), about 13% of the time, followed by reactivation. Simulated photon counting at efficiency 0.3 resolves the \(2\Omega\) peak from \(N=10^5\) upward.

Transferable methods2 techniques, with derivations
  1. Exact energy balance from an observable that commutes with the Hamiltonian

    An observable that commutes with \(H\) changes only through the dissipators, so its Heisenberg equation is exact at any \(N\) and often closes on a few operators. Here it separates energy loss from a dephasing-driven source and constrains every Liouvillian mode.

    With \(Q_N=h_z(M_z+I)-JU_N\) and \([H,Q_N]=0\), \(\mathcal L^\dagger Q_N=\mathcal L^\dagger_{\rm diss}Q_N\). Local decay gives \(\mathcal L^\dagger_{\rm diss}(M_z+I)=-\Gamma_1(M_z+I)\), and each product \(\sigma_i^x\sigma_j^x\) of distinct spins decays at \(2\Gamma_2\), so \(\mathcal L^\dagger_{\rm diss}U_N=-2\Gamma_2U_N\). Hence \(\mathcal L^\dagger Q_N=-\Gamma_1Q_N+(2\Gamma_2-\Gamma_1)JU_N\), and \(2\Gamma_2-\Gamma_1=4\gamma_\phi\).

    For a right eigenmode \(\mathcal LR_k=\lambda_kR_k\) with \(\operatorname{Tr}R_k=0\), projecting the identity gives \(\lambda_kq_k=-\Gamma_1q_k+4J\gamma_\phi u_k\), so \(q_k/u_k=4J\gamma_\phi/(\Gamma_1+\lambda_k)\) with \(q_k=\operatorname{Tr}(Q_NR_k)\) and \(u_k=\operatorname{Tr}(U_NR_k)\). As \(\kappa_k\to0\) this becomes the harmonic response \(4J\gamma_\phi/(\Gamma_1+i\nu)\).

    Editorial check: integrating the mean-field equations at the Fig. 1 parameters, \(\dot Q=-\Gamma_1Q+4J\gamma_\phi m_x^2\) holds to \(10^{-13}\) along the trajectory. With \(\gamma_\phi=0\) the state settles at \(m_z=-0.5\); with \(\gamma_\phi=1.5\), \(m_z\) keeps oscillating between \(-0.63\) and \(-0.38\) at late times.

    How to reuse it

    In an open many-body model, look for a conserved quantity shifted by a constant so that the dissipators map it into a small operator set. The resulting identity gives exact finite-\(N\) relations between eigenmode projections and between correlation functions, which also check numerics. It needs dissipators that do not generate a growing operator hierarchy.

  2. Cycle work of a lagged feedback variable

    To decide whether a damped oscillator coupled to a relaxing variable can sustain a periodic orbit, multiply its equation by the velocity and integrate over a period. Conservative terms drop out, so damping must be paid by feedback work, and that work is nonzero only if the feedback lags.

    Eliminating \(m_y=-(\dot m_x+\Gamma_2m_x)/h_z\) and \(m_z=(Q+Jm_x^2)/h_z-1\) gives \[\ddot m_x+2\Gamma_2\dot m_x=-(h_z^2+\Gamma_2^2-2h_zJ)m_x-2J^2m_x^3-2JQm_x.\] Multiplying by \(\dot m_x\) and integrating over a period gives \(2\Gamma_2\oint\dot m_x^2\,dt=-J\oint Q\,d(m_x^2)\). If \(\gamma_\phi=0\), \(Q\to0\), the right side vanishes and \(m_x\) is constant.

    With \(u=m_x^2\) and \(\dot Q=-\Gamma_1Q+4J\gamma_\phi u\), integration by parts gives \[-J\langle Q\dot u\rangle=J\langle\dot Qu\rangle=\frac{\langle\dot Q(\dot Q+\Gamma_1Q)\rangle}{4\gamma_\phi}=\frac{\langle\dot Q^2\rangle}{4\gamma_\phi},\] since \(\langle Q\dot Q\rangle=0\) over a period. The work is strictly positive unless \(Q\) is constant. If \(Q\) were proportional to \(u\) at every instant, the loop would have zero area.

    Editorial check: on the \(\gamma_\phi=1.5\) orbit (period \(T\approx0.29\,T_1\)), averaging over 33 whole periods gives \(2\Gamma_2\langle\dot m_x^2\rangle\), \(-J\langle Q\dot u\rangle\) and \(\langle\dot Q^2\rangle/(4\gamma_\phi)\) all equal to 420.99. The charging–loss balance \(\Gamma_1\langle Q\rangle=4J\gamma_\phi\langle m_x^2\rangle=39.93\) holds as well.

    How to reuse it

    For any oscillator coupled to a slowly relaxing variable (temperature, population, cavity field), this energy method gives a necessary condition for limit cycles: a nonzero source and a finite relaxation time. The \(\langle\dot Q^2\rangle\) form fixes the sign without solving for the orbit. It does not prove that the cycle exists or say which initial states reach it.

Source: Section III.1 (Eqs. 7–8); Section III.2–III.3 (Eqs. 9–18); Section IV.2 (Eqs. 24–27). Based on the linked paper version. Derivations marked as editorial, and suggested uses in other problems, are editorial guidance rather than results of the paper.

Context & limitationsWhy it matters and what it does not show

Why it matters. It turns a ubiquitous decoherence channel into a control parameter for autonomous temporal order. The identity gives a testable amplitude–phase relation between energy and magnetization in every oscillatory mode, which can be checked beyond all-to-all models. The oscillating state also takes part in metastable switching between two stationary states.

Limitations. The necessity proof holds in homogeneous mean field. At finite \(N\) the evidence is power-law fits over \(N\le300\), not a proof that decay rates vanish. The limit cycle coexists with two stable fixed points, so whether oscillations appear depends on the initial state. Fluctuations and photon-counting readout are treated with a semiclassical diffusion model rather than exact quantum trajectories, and no experiment is reported.

Entanglement certification of attosecond light fields with photoelectron interferometry

Philipp Stammer

arXiv:2610.31187v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 22 October 2026

Question
High-harmonic generation is predicted to entangle the infrared driving field with extreme-ultraviolet harmonics. Standard optical certification needs local oscillators or photon-correlation detectors that barely exist at XUV frequencies. Can photoelectron interferometry (RABBIT), already routine in attosecond science, certify that entanglement on its own?
Result
Not from one sideband: every set of single-sideband moments has a fully separable explanation. Adding the two neighbouring sidebands supplies moments \(U\) and \(V\), and \(|Q|^2>UV\) then certifies IR–XUV entanglement through the partial transpose. A further bound \(|Q|>H(U,V)\) certifies genuine three-mode entanglement and is tight when \(U=0\). On model states, a displaced two-mode squeezed state is certified only in a window of displacement and squeezing, and a non-Gaussian GME family only for squeezing \(r<0.043\).
The paper in detailSetting, main result, and open questions

In RABBIT, harmonics \(\omega_1\) and \(\omega_2=\omega_1+2\omega_0\) ionize an atom while one infrared photon is absorbed or emitted. Both paths reach the same photoelectron sideband and interfere as a function of the IR–XUV delay \(\tau\). Quantum-optically the sideband transition operator is \(T_0=g_1a_1a_0e^{i\omega_0\tau}+g_2a_2a_0^\dagger e^{-i\omega_0\tau}\), so the yield is \(g_1^2D_1+g_2^2D_2+2g_1g_2\operatorname{Re}[Qe^{2i\omega_0\tau}]\), with \(D_1=\langle N_1N_0\rangle\), \(D_2=\langle N_2(N_0+1)\rangle\) and \(Q=\langle a_2^\dagger a_1a_0^2\rangle\). The fringe therefore measures a three-mode coherence.

A fringe alone certifies nothing. Writing \(Q=\langle A^\dagger B\rangle\) with \(A=a_2a_0^\dagger\) and \(B=a_1a_0\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(|Q|^2\le D_1D_2\) for every state. Moreover, every allowed triple \((Q,D_1,D_2)\) is reproduced by a fully separable state: squeezed IR vacuum, a coherent upper harmonic and a phase-averaged lower harmonic. No criterion built only from one sideband can work.

The neighbouring sidebands pair \(a_2\) with IR absorption and \(a_1\) with IR emission, so they measure \(U=\langle N_2N_0\rangle\) and \(V=\langle N_1(N_0+1)\rangle\), with the vacuum term moved to the other harmonic. These are exactly the entries of a moment matrix of the partially transposed state, so every state with positive partial transpose obeys \(|Q|^2\le UV\), and violation certifies entanglement between the IR and the harmonics. In an unshaped spectrum further harmonics add terms to the outer sidebands. Their cycle-averaged constant parts still bound \(U\) and \(V\) from above, so a weaker test survives, and masking those harmonics recovers the tight one.

Genuine tripartite entanglement must exclude mixtures of states separable across different cuts. Splitting \(Q\) by Fock sector and bounding each cut gives, for all biseparable states, \[|Q|\le H(U,V)=\sqrt{(2-\sqrt3)V}+(1+\sqrt2)\sqrt{U(V+1)}+\sqrt{2UV}.\] At \(U=0\) the bound \(|Q|^2\le(2-\sqrt3)V\) is attained by an explicit biseparable state, so it is optimal. For a displaced two-mode squeezed IR–harmonic pair with a coherent second harmonic, certification requires pair occupation \(n<(4\sqrt2-2)/7\approx0.52\) and displacement inside a window, while the exact Gaussian test detects entanglement everywhere. Certifying XUV–XUV entanglement through \(|\langle a_2^\dagger a_1\rangle|^2>\langle N_2N_1\rangle\) would need electron coincidences.

Transferable methods2 techniques, with derivations
  1. Partial-transpose moment matrices from the measured orderings

    Positivity of a partially transposed state implies positivity of any moment matrix built on it. Choosing operators whose transposed moments are exactly the measured quantities gives a witness with no tomography. Here the choice swaps \(a_0\) and \(a_0^\dagger\), turning the orderings the experiment measures into the ones the test needs.

    Let \(\sigma=\rho^{T_0}\), transposed on the IR mode in the number basis. If \(\rho\) has positive partial transpose, \(\sigma\ge0\) and \(G_{ij}=\operatorname{Tr}[\sigma f_i^\dagger f_j]\succeq0\). With \(f_1=a_2a_0\) and \(f_2=a_1a_0^\dagger\), using \(N_0^T=N_0\) and \([(a_0^\dagger)^2]^T=a_0^2\), \(G=\begin{pmatrix}U&Q\\Q^*&V\end{pmatrix}\), so \(\det G=UV-|Q|^2\ge0\).

    Editorial remark: the measured central-band moments differ from \(U,V\) only by vacuum terms, \(D_2-U=\langle N_2\rangle\) and \(V-D_1=\langle N_1\rangle\). The same coherence is bounded by \(D_1D_2\) for every state but by \(UV\) only for PPT states, and only the second bound can be violated.

    Editorial check: a 40-level Fock computation for the displaced two-mode squeezed state at \(x=|\alpha|^2=0.3\), \(z=0.25\) reproduces \(|Q|^2=4|\beta|^2xn(n+1)\), \(U=|\beta|^2(x+n)\) and \(V=n(x+2n+2)\). Then \(|Q|^2>UV\) reduces to \(x^2-(n+2)x+2n(n+1)<0\), whose roots are the paper’s \(x_\pm\). They are real only when \(7n^2+4n-4<0\), that is \(n<(4\sqrt2-2)/7\approx0.522\).

    How to reuse it

    When a detection scheme yields only certain operator orderings (normally ordered intensities for absorption, extra vacuum terms for emission), look for operator pairs whose partial transpose maps the measured moments into a moment matrix. This works for any signal built from interfering transition paths. The witness is only sufficient, and its strength depends on the chosen \(f_i\).

  2. Biseparable bounds by isolating one Fock sector

    To bound a coherence over all biseparable states, split the operator’s action into Fock sectors. The lowest sector, where each cut must share the coherence, is bounded cut by cut using positivity and Cauchy–Schwarz. Everything else is bounded for all states by measured cross-moments.

    With \(t\) IR photons, \[a_2^\dagger a_1a_0^2|t,s,r\rangle=\sqrt{st(t-1)(r+1)}\,|t-2,s-1,r+1\rangle.\] Write \(Q=q_L+R_A+R_B\), where \(q_L=\sqrt2\langle a_1\otimes|0,1\rangle\langle2,0|_{02}\rangle\) collects \(t=2,r=0\). For every state, \(|R_A|\le\sqrt{2dU}\) and \(|R_B|\le\sqrt{2U(n+1)}\), with \(n=\langle N_1\rangle\) and \(d=\langle N_1N_0\rangle\).

    For the cut \((1|02)\), let \(z\) and \(w\) be the populations of \(|2,0\rangle\) and \(|0,1\rangle\) in \(\rho_{02}\). Then \(|q_L|^2\le2nzw\le2nz(1-z)\) and \(V=n(1+\langle N_0\rangle)\ge n(1+2z)\), so \(|q_L|^2/V\le2z(1-z)/(1+2z)\le2-\sqrt3\). The other two cuts give \(|q_L|^2\le U(n+1)\), and weighted Cauchy–Schwarz combines mixtures of cuts.

    Editorial check: maximizing \(2z(1-z)/(1+2z)\) numerically gives 0.267949 at \(z=0.366025\), matching \(2-\sqrt3\) and \(z_*=(\sqrt3-1)/2\). For the GME family of Eq. (17) at \(p=0.75\), bisection on \(|Q|-H(U,V)\) gives \(r_c=0.042945\), matching the paper’s 0.04294.

    How to reuse it

    When a single measured coherence must certify multipartite entanglement, find the smallest Fock sector that contributes to it, optimize over that sector exactly, and push the rest into remainders controlled by measurable moments. Expect tightness only in special limits such as \(U=0\).

Derivations marked as editorial, and suggested uses in other problems, are editorial guidance rather than results of the paper.

Context & limitationsWhy it matters and what it does not show

Why it matters. These are the first proven separability bounds for attosecond quantum light that use only electron spectra. They turn an established pulse-characterization technique into an entanglement test across very different frequencies, without auxiliary optical measurements.

Limitations. The witnesses are sufficient, not necessary, and miss much entanglement: the Gaussian example is entangled for all parameters but certified only in a small region. The model keeps three modes at lowest perturbative order; noise, finite resolution and detector effects are not analysed, and the tight two-mode bound needs spectral masking of neighbouring harmonics. XUV–XUV entanglement would need electron coincidences beyond standard RABBIT. No experiment is reported.

The Impact of Markovian Errors on Random Circuits

Anthony M. Polloreno; Timothy Proctor; Robin Blume-Kohout

arXiv:2610.31500v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 22 October 2026

Question
Randomized benchmarking reports one error rate, but under gate-dependent noise it is unclear what that number measures. Average gate infidelity is gauge dependent, so it cannot be the answer. For small Markovian errors, how does each elementary error (Pauli-stochastic, over-rotation, axis tilt) enter the RB decay?
Result
For any gate set and sampling distribution forming a sequence-asymptotic unitary 2-design, Pauli-stochastic rates are the only errors that enter the decay linearly. Hamiltonian rates enter through a quadratic form that vanishes on gauge directions. For direct RB of \(\{X_{\pi/2},Y_{\pi/2}\}\) with probability \(p\) of \(X\), the form is explicit in two over-rotations and one relative axis tilt. Equal over-rotations \(\theta\) give \(r=3\theta^2/4\) at \(p=1/2\), three times each gate’s infidelity, and about 16.6 times at \(p=0.9\). For Clifford RB, a fixed form with weights \(1/9\) and \(1/18\) is filtered through the compilation.
The paper in detailSetting, main result, and open questions

The motivating example has native gates \(X_{\pi/2}\) and \(Y_{\pi/2}\) with over-rotations \(\theta_X,\theta_Y\) and a tilt \(\phi\) of the angle between their axes. Four error models are chosen with the same leading native-gate entanglement infidelity. Over 2000 random compilations of the 24 single-qubit Cliffords, the Clifford-RB rate per native gate varies widely and differs between models. In direct RB, which samples native gates with probability \(p\) for \(X\), the weighted-infidelity heuristic predicts flat curves, but the actual rates depend strongly on \(p\) and differ between models.

Each noisy gate is written as \(\widetilde{\mathcal G}_k=e^{L_k}\mathcal G_k\), with \(L_k\) expanded in Hamiltonian, stochastic, correlation and active generators. A small gauge change \(e^K\) shifts \(L_k\) by \(K-\mathcal G_kK\mathcal G_k^{-1}\), and first-order gauge-invariant (FOGI) coordinates are the combinations unchanged by every such shift. The RB decay is the eigenvalue \(\gamma\) of \(\mathscr L=\sum_k\Omega_k\mathcal G_k\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal G}_k\) continuing the ideal depolarizing mode. A spectral gap of the ideal moment operator, which defines a sequence-asymptotic 2-design, keeps it isolated for small errors, and \(r=\tfrac{d^2-1}{d^2}(1-\gamma)\).

Theorem 1 states \[\gamma=1-\tfrac{d^2}{d^2-1}\sum_{k,P}\Omega_ks_P^{(k)}-Q_H(\mathbf h;\Omega)+O(\delta^2,\delta\theta,\theta^3).\] The linear term follows from degenerate perturbation theory on the two-dimensional ideal eigenspace: only the superoperator trace of the averaged generator matters, and only stochastic generators have nonzero trace. The quadratic term is gauge invariant because \(\gamma\) is exactly invariant under similarity transformations and has no linear Hamiltonian part. Therefore \(Q_H\) annihilates every linearized gauge direction and depends only on FOGI coordinates.

For \(\{X_{\pi/2},Y_{\pi/2}\}\), the \(6\times6\) Hamiltonian form has rank 3 with the gauge directions as kernel. Its reduced form in \(F_1\approx\theta_X/2\), \(F_2\approx\theta_Y/2\) and the relational \(F_3\approx\phi/2\) is \(\mathbf Q_{\rm DRB}(p)\). The tilt weight \(16pq/3\) vanishes when either gate disappears. The intrinsic weights diverge like \(1/q\) and \(1/p\) because consecutive copies of the same gate add their errors coherently. For Clifford RB, first-order compiled vectors \(\mathbf h_C\) give \(Q^{(2)}_{\rm CRB}=\tfrac1{18}\sum_C\|\mathbf h_C\|^2+\tfrac1{216}\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf w\), equivalent to weight \(1/9\) on three symmetric directions, \(1/18\) on the other 66 and zero on gauge. Compilation changes how native errors project onto these sectors.

Transferable methods2 techniques, with derivations
  1. First-order RB decay from a Choi-space projector

    The unperturbed eigenvalue-one space of the RB moment operator is spanned by the identity and the completely depolarizing map, both fixed by every ideal gate. First-order shifts reduce to projecting the averaged error generator onto this space, which depends only on two traces.

    With \(\widetilde{\mathcal G}_k=(1+\varepsilon g_k)\mathcal G_k\), every \(X\in\mathcal V_1=\operatorname{Span}\{\Delta,\mathrm{id}\}\) commutes with ideal unitary channels, so \(\widehat{\mathscr L}_1(X)=\bar g\circ X\) with \(\bar g=\sum_k\Omega_kg_k\). The Gram matrix of \(\{\Delta,\mathrm{id}\}\) in the Choi inner product is \(\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&d^2\end{pmatrix}\), which gives \(P_{\mathcal V_1}(\Phi)=\tfrac{d\operatorname{tr}\Phi(I)-\operatorname{Tr}\Phi}{d^2-1}\Delta+\tfrac{\operatorname{Tr}\Phi-\operatorname{tr}\Phi(I)/d}{d^2-1}\mathrm{id}\).

    Trace-preserving generators have \(\operatorname{tr}\bar g(I)=0\), so \(P_{\mathcal V_1}(\bar g\circ D)=\tfrac{\operatorname{Tr}\bar g}{d^2-1}D\) with \(D=\mathrm{id}-\Delta\), and \(\delta\gamma=\operatorname{Tr}\bar g/(d^2-1)\). Since \(\operatorname{Tr}H_P=\operatorname{Tr}C_{P,Q}=\operatorname{Tr}A_{P,Q}=0\) and \(\operatorname{Tr}S_P=\operatorname{tr}(P)^2-d^2=-d^2\), the result is \(r=\sum_k\Omega_k\sum_Ps_P^{(k)}+O(\delta^2,\delta\theta,\theta^2)\).

    Editorial remark: for stochastic noise this is the sampling-weighted sum of Pauli error rates, so the naive heuristic is right at first order. Its failure for coherent errors in Figs. 1–2 is entirely a second-order effect.

    How to reuse it

    Any protocol whose signal decays as powers of a twirled moment operator (RB variants, cycle benchmarking, mirror circuits) can be analysed this way: identify the ideal eigenvalue-one space, build its projector from Choi pairings, and read the first-order shift from traces. It needs a spectral gap of the ideal moment operator, and the constants worsen as that gap closes.

  2. Gauge invariance forces the coherent term onto FOGI coordinates

    Since \(\gamma\) is an exact gauge invariant with no linear Hamiltonian term, its quadratic Hamiltonian coefficient must vanish on every linearized gauge direction. The form then lives on the FOGI quotient, which for the example gate set is three-dimensional.

    Set \(\mathbf h=\varepsilon\mathbf a\) and gauge by \(e^{\varepsilon tK}\), so \(\mathbf h'=\varepsilon(\mathbf a+t\mathbf g_H(K))+O(\varepsilon^2)\). The \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) correction affects \(\gamma\) only at order \(\varepsilon^3\) because there is no linear term. Comparing \(\varepsilon^2\) coefficients of \(\gamma(\mathbf h')=\gamma(\mathbf h)\) gives \(Q_H(\mathbf a+t\mathbf g)=Q_H(\mathbf a)\) for all \(t\), so \(\mathbf a^{\mathsf T}\mathbf Q_H\mathbf g=0\) for all \(\mathbf a\) and \(\mathbf Q_H\mathbf g_H(K)=0\).

    At \(p=1/2\), \(\mathbf Q_{\rm DRB}=\tfrac13\begin{pmatrix}5&1&2\\1&5&2\\2&2&4\end{pmatrix}\), with eigenvalues \(8/3\) (equal \(F_i\)), \(4/3\) (differential over-rotation) and \(2/3\) (direction \((-1,-1,2)\)). For equal over-rotations \(\mathbf F=(\theta/2,\theta/2,0)\), \(\mathbf F^{\mathsf T}\mathbf Q\mathbf F=\tfrac{\theta^2}{4}\cdot\tfrac{5+5+2}{3}=\theta^2\), so \(r=\tfrac34\theta^2\).

    Editorial check: building the \(9\times9\) direct-RB \(\mathscr L\) matrix from the exact noisy rotations of Eqs. (3)–(4) with \(\theta=0.004\), the depolarizing eigenvalue matches Eqs. (80)–(82) to within 0.3% at \(p=0.3\), 0.5 and 0.9 for the equal, opposite and tilt models.

    How to reuse it

    For any gauge-invariant figure of merit whose linear coherent term vanishes, the leading coherent contribution must be a form on FOGI coordinates. Find the gauge kernel numerically, then report sensitivities to intrinsic and relational errors separately. A single scalar cannot fix error signs, so combine several sampling settings or deliberate error injection.

Context & limitationsWhy it matters and what it does not show

Why it matters. It explains why direct-RB rates depend on the sampling distribution and Clifford-RB rates on the compilation, and it turns an RB number into a function of physical, gauge-invariant error parameters. Running at several sampling probabilities gives quadratic constraints on coherent errors.

Limitations. The expansion is perturbative at fixed sampling. Its coefficients diverge as \(p\to0\) or \(1\), and it fails first there. Explicit forms are given only for one single-qubit gate set. Correlation and active generators are treated only at leading order, and multi-qubit gate sets are left open. Quadratic constraints cannot fix the signs of errors. Drift, leakage and non-Markovian noise are excluded.

Problem-aware sampling for bias and variance reduction in randomised Hamiltonian simulation

Brian Coyle; Jonathan H. Fetherolf; Antonio Márquez Romero; Stefano Scali; Giuseppe Buonaiuto; Josh Kirsopp

arXiv:2610.31515v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 22 October 2026

Question
qDRIFT approximates \(e^{-iHT}\) by random Pauli rotations sampled in proportion to \(|c_j|\), ignoring the initial state and the observable. Its estimators carry bias and variance that cost many steps and trajectories. When the goal is a single \(\langle O(T)\rangle\) from a known initial state, can the sampling distribution be tuned to the problem while keeping qDRIFT’s guarantees?
Result
Yes, with StateDRIFT. To fourth order in \(dt\), bias and variance depend on the distribution only through a few \(t=0\) quantities built from \(|\psi\rangle\), \(O\) and the Hamiltonian terms. This gives closed-form variance-optimal rules, an interpolating family that provably improves both bias and variance at one step for sign-definite inputs, and an optimized family for deeper circuits. For molecules of 6 to 18 qubits the dynamics resolves in 1.7–26 times fewer steps and 1.2–11 times fewer two-qubit gates; for BeH\(_2\) the gate count falls from 11,201 to 983. Mixing with 20% of the vanilla distribution bounds every reweighting by a factor of 5.
The paper in detailSetting, main result, and open questions

With \(H=\sum_jc_jP_j\), each qDRIFT step samples \(j\) with probability \(q_j\) and applies \(e^{-i\theta_jP_j}\), \(\theta_j=c_jdt/q_j\). With \(M\) trajectories the error is \(B_N^2+V_N/M\), and only the bias does not shrink with \(M\). One step is exact: \(\hat O_1(j)=\langle O\rangle+\sin^2\theta_j\,\gamma_j+\tfrac12\sin2\theta_j\,\beta_j\), with \(\gamma_j=\langle P_jOP_j\rangle-\langle O\rangle\) and \(\beta_j=-i\langle[O,P_j]\rangle\). In the toy \(H=3Z_0Z_1+X_0+X_1\), vanilla qDRIFT spends 60% of samples on \(Z_0Z_1\), which commutes with the magnetization and has \(\gamma=0\). The state-aware rule moves weight to the transverse terms and cuts the root-mean-square error by 2–7 times.

For \(N\) steps, \(B_N=N(b_{\gamma,2}-\tfrac12\varphi_{H,H})dt^2+O(dt^3)\), with \(b_{\gamma,2}=\sum_jc_j^2\gamma_j/q_j\) and \(\varphi_{H,H}=d^2\langle O\rangle/dt^2|_0\). The variance starts at \(Nv_{\beta^2,2}dt^2\). Cross-step terms carry higher powers of \(N\) but still fall as \(1/N\) at fixed \(T\). If the state, observable and terms are real in some basis, as for real-orbital chemistry with Hartree–Fock references, then \(\beta_j=0\) and odd orders vanish. When the \(\gamma_j\) have mixed signs, a distribution can make \(b_{\gamma,2}=\tfrac12\varphi_{H,H}\) and cancel the \(dt^2\) bias.

Densities of the form \(\sum_jc_j^2f_j/q_j\) are minimized by \(q_j\propto|c_j|\sqrt{f_j}\), a Neyman allocation. This gives \(q_j\propto|c_j|\sqrt{|\gamma_j|}\) when \(\beta_j=0\), \(q_j\propto|c_j||\beta_j|\) otherwise, and the family \(q_j\propto|c_j|\sqrt{|\gamma_j|+\alpha\beta_j^2}\) between them. Along this family the bias density grows and the variance density shrinks, so for sign-definite \(\gamma\) some \(\alpha\) beats vanilla in both. Deeper circuits use per-term weights on five densities, optimized for mean-squared error with neither bias nor variance more than 5% worse than vanilla. Earlier state-aware rules are special cases with \(O=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\), for which \(\gamma_j\le0\) and the bias cannot be cancelled.

For random product states under five lattice models of 6–18 qubits, most single-step instances improve both bias and variance. For quenches between molecular geometries, from H\(_3\) to CH\(_4\), the steps needed to resolve the change in a weighted occupation fall 1.7–26 times. A validity ratio \(R^{(4)}_{\rm val}>2\) separated wins from losses in every case tested. For deployment, a Hartree–Fock surrogate misjudges correlated LiH and does worse than vanilla, while an MPS with \(\chi=4\) suffices. Matchgate shadows need only the 3-RDM for every distribution-dependent input and the 5-RDM for one fixed term, with \(O(n^5\log n/\varepsilon^2)\) snapshots.

Transferable methods2 techniques, with derivations
  1. Neyman allocation for importance-sampled channels

    The leading bias and variance are sums \(\sum_jc_j^2f_j/q_j\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives the minimizer in closed form, and the same argument applies to any weighted sum of such densities, which yields a one-parameter family trading bias against variance.

    For \(f_j\ge0\) and \(\sum_jq_j=1\), \[\Bigl(\sum_j|c_j|\sqrt{f_j}\Bigr)^2=\Bigl(\sum_j\tfrac{|c_j|\sqrt{f_j}}{\sqrt{q_j}}\sqrt{q_j}\Bigr)^2\le\sum_j\tfrac{c_j^2f_j}{q_j}\sum_jq_j,\] with equality iff \(q_j\propto|c_j|\sqrt{f_j}\). With \(f_j=|\gamma_j|\) of one sign, the single-step variance \(v_\gamma=\operatorname{Var}_q[(c_j/q_j)^2\gamma_j]\) vanishes at the same \(q\), because the random variable becomes constant.

    The bias \((b_{\gamma,2}-\tfrac12\varphi_{H,H})dt^2\) is minimized at that \(q\) but cancels only if \(\tfrac12\varphi_{H,H}\) lies beyond \(b_\gamma^\star=\pm(\sum_j|c_j|\sqrt{|\gamma_j|})^2\). Mixed signs allow exact cancellation, which is how the staggered magnetization of the XY chain reaches \(|B_1|\approx10^{-4}\) of vanilla.

    Editorial check: for the toy model with \(|\psi_a\rangle\), \(O=Z_0+Z_1\) and \(T=0.2\), \(\gamma=(0,-1.732,-1)\). The 20%-mixed rule \(q=(0.12,0.49,0.39)\) lowers the exact single-step bias from \(-0.498\) to \(-0.339\) and the variance from 0.251 to 0.0072. The root-mean-square error at \(M=1\) falls from 0.707 to 0.349, within the 2–7 times range of Fig. 2.

    How to reuse it

    Any randomized compiler or importance-sampled estimator (randomized measurements, LCU sampling, Pauli-path sums) whose cost is \(\sum_jw_j^2f_j/q_j\) can be tuned with \(q_j\propto|w_j|\sqrt{f_j}\), provided \(f_j\) is cheap to estimate in advance. Mix with the baseline so that no probability falls below \(\tau\) times its baseline value, which bounds every reweighting by \(1/\tau\).

  2. Majorana degree counting fixes the RDM order

    To estimate the distribution inputs on a fermionic state, count how many Majorana operators the nested commutators can contain. This fixes which reduced density matrix is needed and therefore the matchgate-shadow cost.

    Majorana monomials \(\zeta_{\mathcal I}\) and \(\zeta_{\mathcal I'}\) with \(|\mathcal I'|\) even commute unless \(|\mathcal I\cap\mathcal I'|\) is odd, in which case \([\zeta_{\mathcal I},\zeta_{\mathcal I'}]=\pm2\zeta_{\mathcal S}\) with \(\mathcal S\) the symmetric difference and \(|\mathcal S|\le|\mathcal I|+|\mathcal I'|-2\). Hamiltonian terms have degree 2 or 4, so each commutator raises the degree by at most 2.

    A one-body \(O\) has degree 2. Conjugation by \(P_j\) flips the sign of pairs with exactly one index in \(\mathcal I_j\), so \(\gamma_j=-2i\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal I_j,\nu\notin\mathcal I_j}A_{\mu\nu}\langle\zeta_\mu\zeta_\nu\rangle\) needs only the 1-RDM. \(\beta_j\) needs the 2-RDM and \(\varphi_{j,b}\) the 3-RDM. The fixed term \(\langle[H,[H,[H,[H,O]]]]\rangle\) has degree up to 10 and needs the 5-RDM.

    Matchgate shadows estimate a degree-\(2k\) correlator with single-snapshot variance at most \(\binom{2n}{2k}/\binom nk\approx(2n)^k/(2k-1)!!\), polynomial in \(n\) at fixed \(k\), whereas local Pauli shadows scale as \(3^w\) in the Pauli weight.

    How to reuse it

    Before measuring nested-commutator quantities on fermionic states (Trotter error bounds, Krylov moments, perturbative corrections), count Majorana degrees to find the highest RDM order needed. One matchgate-shadow dataset then serves every such quantity, provided the Hamiltonian has at most quartic fermionic terms.

Context & limitationsWhy it matters and what it does not show

Why it matters. It is an ancilla-free change to randomized simulation that targets the quantity actually estimated. Its inputs can come from a bond-dimension-4 matrix-product state surrogate (for LiH) or from matchgate shadows with polynomially many snapshots, without full classical simulation.

Limitations. The expansion is fourth order. Its truncation bound scales as \((\lambda T)^3\) and is loose, so the method targets short times: water fails at \(T=1\), as the validity metric predicts. Most benchmarks use exact inputs on classically simulable sizes. A Hartree–Fock surrogate performs worse than vanilla qDRIFT for LiH. The distribution is fixed at \(t=0\), and gate savings depend on the cost model.

FVQKD: A Formal Verification of Quantum Key Distribution Security in Rocq

Ruixuan Deng; Zhengfeng Ji; Jiayu Zhang; Li Zhou

arXiv:2610.30645v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 22 October 2026

Question
QKD security rests on hand-checked proofs that chain smooth entropies, an entropic uncertainty relation, a sampling bound and the leftover hash lemma, drawn from several papers with differing conventions. Can the composable finite-key security of BB84 be machine-checked end to end, from a formal protocol program to the key-length theorem?
Result
Yes, in FVQKD, a Rocq development of about 151,000 lines, 102,000 of them new, with no admitted lemmas and only the kernel plus three standard axioms. It proves composable finite-key security of BB84 at the tight Tomamichel et al. bound, for every initial state and every adversary channel acting on a formally specified protocol program. Mechanization exposed a missing case in the published argument: after conditioning on the test passing, the smoothing parameter \(\varepsilon/\sqrt{p_{\rm pass}}\) can exceed 1, outside the domain of the uncertainty relation. A second proof branch using the abort probability repairs it, and the key-length formula is unchanged. A concrete instance with \(n=k=10^4\) certifies a 2000-bit key.
The paper in detailSetting, main result, and open questions

In BB84, sifting leaves \(n\) key rounds and \(k\) test rounds. The protocol aborts if the test error rate exceeds \(Q_{\rm tol}\), reconciles keys while leaking \(\mathrm{leak}_{\rm EC}\) bits, checks agreement with a hash, and compresses the key by two-universal hashing. Security means \(\varepsilon_c\)-correctness and \(\varepsilon_s\)-secrecy, \((1-p_{\rm abort})\min_\sigma\tfrac12\|\rho_{SE}-\tau_S\otimes\sigma_E\|_1\le\varepsilon_s\), which is composable. The target bound is \(\ell\le n(q-h(Q_{\rm tol}+\mu))-\mathrm{leak}_{\rm EC}-\log_2\tfrac{2}{\varepsilon_s^2\varepsilon_c}\), where \(\mu\sim1/\sqrt k\) is the only finite-size loss beyond logarithmic terms.

The development has five modules in dependency order, \(\texttt{utils}<\texttt{entropy}<\{\texttt{lhl},\texttt{eur}\}<\texttt{qkd}\), built on MathComp, MathComp-Analysis and CoqQ. The lower four contain no protocol-specific content. They include Uhlmann’s theorem, purified distance with monotonicity under trace-nonincreasing maps, smooth min- and max-entropies and their duality, the POVM-general smooth uncertainty relation, the quantum leftover hash lemma, Serfling’s bound, and what the authors describe as the first machine-checked SDP strong-duality theorem. The top layer composes them as sealed theorems.

A small command language with classical and quantum variables, sampling, measurement and an adversary channel action gives the protocol a formal semantics on classical–quantum states. Sifting, parameter estimation and verification become program branches ending in \(\perp\). Theorem 10 quantifies over every initial state and every transcript-dependent adversary. Six trust assumptions, such as no side channels, an authenticated classical channel and a characterized source, appear as explicit hypotheses or device obligations. For \(n=k=10^4\), \(Q_{\rm tol}=1\%\), \(\mathrm{leak}_{\rm EC}=1024\) and \(\varepsilon\) parameters \(2^{-34}\), the artifact proves the key-length expression is at least 2625 bits, certifying \(\ell=2000\) at \(\varepsilon_{\rm tot}=3\cdot2^{-34}\).

Two side conditions were missing from the published proof. Smooth entropies need \(\varepsilon<\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}\rho}\). Conditioning on the test passing rescales \(\varepsilon\) to \(\varepsilon/\sqrt{p_{\rm pass}}\), which an adversary forcing aborts can push above 1: \(p_{\rm pass}=10^{-12}\) and \(\varepsilon=10^{-5}\) give 10. The key is also conditioned on verification accepting, a second event the published proof does not treat. Beyond these gaps, the six source papers define smoothing balls by purified distance or by a generalized trace distance, and optimize over normalized or sub-normalized witnesses. The duality \(H^\varepsilon_{\max}(A|B)=-H^\varepsilon_{\min}(A|C)\) relies on purified distance, so FVQKD fixes one convention and bridges the others with coercion lemmas.

Transferable methods2 techniques, with derivations
  1. The finite-key bound as a chain of entropy inequalities

    The key-length theorem is assembled from three independent results. The uncertainty relation converts Bob’s correlation with Alice into Eve’s ignorance, the sampling bound controls the unobserved error rate, and the leftover hash lemma turns min-entropy into a nearly uniform key. Each is a separately verified module.

    The uncertainty relation states \(H^\varepsilon_{\min}(X|E)+H^\varepsilon_{\max}(Z|B)\ge nq\), with \(q=-\log_2\max_{x,z}\|\sqrt{M_x}\sqrt{N_z}\|_\infty^2\), and \(q=1\) for conjugate qubit bases. Data processing gives \(H^\varepsilon_{\max}(Z|B)\le H^\varepsilon_{\max}(Z|Z')\), and Serfling’s bound on sampling without replacement gives \(H^\varepsilon_{\max}(Z|Z')\le n\,h(Q_{\rm tol}+\mu)\). Hence \(H^\varepsilon_{\min}(X|E)\ge n(q-h(Q_{\rm tol}+\mu))\).

    The chain rule subtracts the \(\mathrm{leak}_{\rm EC}+\lceil\log_2(1/\varepsilon_c)\rceil\) disclosed bits. The leftover hash lemma bounds the distance from uniform by \(\varepsilon+\tfrac12\sqrt{2^{\ell-m}}\), and requiring \(\tfrac12\sqrt{2^{\ell-m}}\le\bar\varepsilon\) gives \(\ell\le m-2\log_2\tfrac1{2\bar\varepsilon}\), the form in Theorem 10.

    Editorial check: evaluating the Theorem 10 expression at the instance parameters (\(q=1\), \(\varepsilon=\bar\varepsilon=\varepsilon_c=2^{-34}\)) gives \(\mu=0.0687\), \(h(0.0787)=0.397\) and a right-hand side of about 4900 bits. The artifact proves the weaker lower bound 2625, which is enough to certify \(\ell=2000\).

    How to reuse it

    Protocols on the smooth-entropy route (decoy-state BB84, six-state, qudit or MDI variants) reuse the same three-part chain. What changes is the uncertainty relation’s overlap \(q\) and the parameter-estimation statistics. Writing a new proof in this form shows exactly which part must be re-proved.

  2. Split on the event probability when conditioning inflates a smoothing parameter

    Conditioning on an event of probability \(p\) renormalizes the state and inflates any purified-distance radius by \(1/\sqrt p\). If \(p\) is small the radius leaves the domain where smooth entropies are defined. The fix is a case split: either \(p\) is large enough to apply the theorem, or it is so small that the composable definition’s prefactor makes secrecy trivial.

    Smooth entropies require \(0\le\varepsilon<\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}\rho}\). For the normalized conditional state this means \(\varepsilon'=\varepsilon/\sqrt{p_{\rm pass}}<1\), that is \(p_{\rm pass}>\varepsilon^2\). The published proof checks only \(\varepsilon'\ge0\).

    Editorial reconstruction of the second branch: the trace distance is at most 1, so the secrecy quantity is at most \(1-p_{\rm abort}\le p_{\rm pass}\). If \(p_{\rm pass}\le\varepsilon^2\) and \(\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_s\le1\), it is at most \(\varepsilon^2\le\varepsilon_s\). FVQKD applies the same split to the later conditioning on verification acceptance.

    How to reuse it

    Whenever a proof conditions on a passing test and then applies an \(\varepsilon\)-dependent or smoothed bound (finite-key QKD, randomness certification, device-independent protocols), check the rescaled parameter’s domain. The usual repair is a case split on the event probability, using the \((1-p_{\rm abort})\) factor in the security definition.

Context & limitationsWhy it matters and what it does not show

Why it matters. This is the first end-to-end machine-checked QKD security proof. About two thirds of the code is protocol independent: purified distance, smooth-entropy duality, the uncertainty relation, the quantum leftover hash lemma, Serfling’s bound and SDP strong duality. Decoy-state and measurement-device-independent proofs on the same route can reuse it, which matters for certification.

Limitations. Only the information-theoretic argument is verified. Side channels and device imperfections are assumed away, and the executable endpoint is restricted to ideal projective \(X/Z\) devices with basis-independent detection. Weak-coherent sources, decoy states and the key consumed by authentication are not modelled. Device-independent and continuous-variable QKD need new libraries. The finding is a gap in a proof, not an attack.

Reset-Enhanced Dissipative Quantum Metrology

Silan Li; Qingjun Tong; Ruicheng Bao

arXiv:2610.31249v1 ↗PDF ↗Submitted 22 October 2026

Question
Dissipation in open quantum sensors usually washes out parameter information. Can the simplest dissipative control, a channel that resets the probe to a fixed state at rate \(r\), increase the quantum Fisher information in steady-state and transient sensing without changing the Hamiltonian or adding a coherent drive?
Result
Sometimes, and the paper says exactly when. An exact identity for \(\partial_rF\) gives a first-order test for local enhancement. For a thermal qubit reset to its ground state, steady-state thermometry improves if and only if \(0<\beta\omega<\ln(2+\sqrt5)\) and \(r\) is below a threshold, with optimum \(r_{\rm opt}=\gamma(2+\sqrt5-e^{\beta\omega})/(e^{\beta\omega}-1)\). For diagonal dynamics, reset cannot change the leading short-time Fisher information of a static parameter, but it raises elapsed-time information immediately, roughly as \(r^2\). For frequency estimation with reset to \(|+\rangle\), the gain at \(t=1/\gamma\) is about 7 times, 91% of it from the coherence (eigenvector) part.
The paper in detailSetting, main result, and open questions

The probe evolves under a Lindbladian \(\mathcal L_\theta\) with biorthogonal modes \(\mathcal L_\theta R_j=\lambda_jR_j\) and a unique steady state. Reset adds \(r(\rho_\delta\operatorname{Tr}\rho-\rho)\). The new steady state is exactly \(\rho^{\rm ss}_{\theta,r}=\rho^{\rm ss}_\theta-\sum_{j\ge2}\tfrac{rd_j}{\lambda_j-r}R_j\) with \(d_j=\operatorname{Tr}(L_j^\dagger\rho_\delta)\). Reset therefore mixes non-stationary modes into the steady probe, weighting slow modes most. For any smooth full-rank family, \(\partial_rF=2\operatorname{Tr}[(\partial_\theta\partial_r\varrho)\mathbb L]-\operatorname{Tr}[(\partial_r\varrho)\mathbb L^2]\), with \(\mathbb L\) the symmetric logarithmic derivative, for steady and transient states alike.

When the dynamics stays diagonal in a fixed basis, populations obey \(\mathbf p(t)=\mathbf p_0+t[W_\theta\mathbf p_0+r(\mathbf p_\delta-\mathbf p_0)]+O(t^2)\). For a static parameter, the leading Fisher information \(t^2\sum_i[(\partial_\theta W)\mathbf p_0]_i^2/(\mathbf p_0)_i\) contains no \(r\), so any gain needs time to build up. For elapsed-time estimation, the instantaneous Fisher information is \(\sum_i[W\mathbf p_0+r(\mathbf p_\delta-\mathbf p_0)]_i^2/(\mathbf p_0)_i\), which depends on \(r\) at \(t=0\).

For a thermal qubit with bosonic rates and ground-state reset, \(p^{\rm ss}=\gamma/[\gamma(e^{\beta\omega}+1)+r(e^{\beta\omega}-1)]\), which gives a closed-form steady Fisher information and the exact advantage window. Outside the high-temperature window, reset only pins the probe near a temperature-blind state. With a temporary reset pulse, transient thermometry reaches a high-information window earlier, and for suitable \(r\) can exceed the equilibrium Fisher information for a while. Strong reset suppresses temperature susceptibility. Time-estimation Fisher information jumps by more than two orders of magnitude just after reset switches on, then decays as the state nears its new fixed point.

For frequency estimation, an amplitude-damped qubit is reset to \(|+\rangle\). The populations do not depend on \(\omega\), so all information sits in the coherence \(c_{\omega,r}(t)\), and \(F_\omega=4|\partial_\omega c|^2+[\partial_\omega|c|^2]^2/(p(1-p)-|c|^2)\). Without reset \(F^{(0)}_\omega=4|c_0|^2t^2e^{-\gamma t}\) decays as coherence is lost. Reset replenishes the coherent modes that carry the phase. At \(t=1\) the optimum \(r\approx1.73\gamma\) gives a gain of 6.96, dominated by the eigenvector term \(s^2|\partial_\omega\hat{\mathbf s}|^2\), so energy-basis readout cannot access it.

Transferable methods2 techniques, with derivations
  1. Response of the QFI to a control parameter through the SLD

    Differentiating the Fisher information with respect to any control and using the self-adjointness of the Jordan product removes the derivative of the SLD. Only derivatives of the state and the SLD at the operating point are needed.

    With \(\mathcal J_\rho(X)=\tfrac12\{\rho,X\}\) and \(\partial_\theta\varrho=\mathcal J_\varrho(\mathbb L)\), \(F=\operatorname{Tr}[(\partial_\theta\varrho)\mathbb L]\) gives \(\partial_rF=\operatorname{Tr}[(\partial_r\partial_\theta\varrho)\mathbb L]+\operatorname{Tr}[\mathbb L\,\mathcal J_\varrho(\partial_r\mathbb L)]\). Differentiating the SLD equation gives \(\mathcal J_\varrho(\partial_r\mathbb L)=\partial_r\partial_\theta\varrho-\tfrac12\{\partial_r\varrho,\mathbb L\}\), and trace cyclicity yields \(\partial_rF=2\operatorname{Tr}[(\partial_\theta\partial_r\varrho)\mathbb L]-\operatorname{Tr}[(\partial_r\varrho)\mathbb L^2]\).

    At \(r=0\) in the steady state, \(\partial_r\rho^{\rm ss}=\rho_1=-\sum_{j\ge2}d_jR_j/\lambda_j\), so the first-order test uses only the unreset modes and the target overlaps.

    Editorial check: for the thermal qubit, the closed form \(F^{\rm ss}(\beta;s)\) matches a finite-difference Fisher information of \(p^{\rm ss}\) to \(10^{-10}\). Scanning \(s=r/\gamma\) gives optima 3.988, 0.883, 0.059 and 0 at \(\beta\omega=0.5\), 1.0, 1.4 and 1.5, matching \(r_{\rm opt}\), with no gain beyond \(\ln(2+\sqrt5)=1.4436\). The gain at \(\beta\omega=0.5\) is 2.48.

    How to reuse it

    For any control knob in a sensing protocol (a dissipation rate, detuning or measurement strength), this identity gives the gradient of the QFI without solving for \(\partial_r\mathbb L\), which suits gradient-based protocol design. It holds on fixed-rank families; at rank changes use one-sided limits or the minimal SLD.

  2. Short-time expansion separating static parameters from time

    Expanding to first order in \(t\) shows which Fisher information can feel a modified generator at once. Information about a static parameter must accumulate through \(\partial_\theta W\), while a clock’s information is the squared drift itself, which reset changes at \(t=0\).

    With \(\mathbf p_0\) and \(\mathbf p_\delta\) independent of \(\theta\), \(\partial_\theta\mathbf p(t)=t(\partial_\theta W)\mathbf p_0+O(t^2)\), which has no \(r\) at leading order, so \(F_\theta=t^2\sum_i[(\partial_\theta W)\mathbf p_0]_i^2/(\mathbf p_0)_i+O(t^3)\). By contrast \(\partial_t\mathbf p(0)=W\mathbf p_0+r(\mathbf p_\delta-\mathbf p_0)\), so \(F_t(0)=\sum_i[W\mathbf p_0+r(\mathbf p_\delta-\mathbf p_0)]_i^2/(\mathbf p_0)_i\), quadratic in \(r\) once the reset drift dominates.

    Coherent counterpart: when populations are blind to \(\omega\), the information is \(F_\omega=4|\partial_\omega c|^2+[\partial_\omega|c|^2]^2/(p(1-p)-|c|^2)\). Without reset \(c=c_0e^{(i\omega-\gamma/2)t}\), so \(\partial_\omega c=itc\) and \(F^{(0)}_\omega=4|c_0|^2t^2e^{-\gamma t}\).

    Editorial check: evaluating the paper’s formula for its \(\rho_0\) gives \(F^{(0)}_\omega=0.00828\) at \(t=1\). Scanning \(r\in[0,5]\) finds the optimum at \(r=1.732\) with gain 6.96, matching Fig. 2(d).

    How to reuse it

    Before designing a control to boost a sensor, expand to first order in time. If the parameter enters only through the generator, nothing that changes the initial drift helps at leading order. For clock-type tasks, engineering the drift helps immediately. The argument assumes diagonal dynamics in a fixed basis and full-support initial populations.

Context & limitationsWhy it matters and what it does not show

Why it matters. Reset is easy to implement by optical pumping or measurement and feedback. The paper gives exact windows rather than a blanket claim, and a diagnostic computable from the unreset Liouvillian modes.

Limitations. Only qubit examples are worked out. The identity is local in \(r\), so finite-\(r\) advantage needs the integrated response. Reaching the frequency bound needs phase-sensitive measurements. The time, energy and imperfection costs of reset are not included. Large gain ratios occur where the baseline Fisher information is small, so absolute values matter.
