Research category

Quantum Technology

How can fragile quantum effects become reliable processors, networks, and measuring instruments?

The field in perspective

An ideal protocol becomes a technology only when control, noise, measurement, and resource costs can be handled together. The important comparison is with a credible alternative under the same conditions.

These pages connect small mathematical models to measurable engineering requirements. Illustrative parameters explain a trade-off; they are not forecasts or rankings of current devices.

Fundamental questions

  1. Can quantum devices remain controllable as they grow? ↗

    A scalable device must preserve useful control and verifiable performance as calibration, connectivity, and correlated errors grow.

  2. Can quantum networks overcome distance and loss? ↗

    Repeaters promise a different rate–distance trade-off, but loss, memory, and scheduling must be accounted for together.

  3. When does entanglement improve a measurement? ↗

    The ideal Heisenberg scaling is a starting point; useful sensing advantage must survive noise and equal-resource comparisons.
