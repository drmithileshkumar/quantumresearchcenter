Research category

Quantum Computation

Which problems can quantum systems solve efficiently, and what resources make that possible?

The field in perspective

A computational advantage is a statement about a task, a family of inputs, an error tolerance, and a resource model. A large state space alone does not establish an advantage.

These questions connect computational complexity to physical simulation and error-corrected computation. They ask both what can be achieved in principle and what must be paid to achieve it.

Fundamental questions

  1. What can quantum computers compute efficiently? ↗

    Interference produces remarkable algorithms, but the boundary of efficient quantum computation is still incompletely understood.

  2. When does simulating quantum matter become useful? ↗

    Efficiently producing a quantum evolution is different from efficiently extracting a scientifically useful prediction.

  3. What does reliable quantum computation cost? ↗

    Error correction changes the scaling of failure, but practical overhead depends on codes, noise, gates, and architecture together.
