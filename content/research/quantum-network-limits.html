The question in perspective

A classical repeater can read and regenerate a signal. An unknown quantum state cannot be copied in that way. Quantum networks instead distribute entanglement, use heralded successful links, and connect them through swapping or error-corrected protocols. The central question is whether the delivered rate and fidelity improve once all waiting and hardware costs are counted.

Loss sets a benchmark

For a simple optical attenuation model, transmissivity over distance \(L\) is \(\eta=e^{-L/L_{\rm att}}\), where \(L_{\rm att}\) is an attenuation length. In an ideal bosonic pure-loss channel, the asymptotic repeaterless secret-key capacity with unlimited two-way classical assistance and no input-energy constraint is

\[K_0(\eta)=-\log_2(1-\eta)\quad\text{bits per optical mode}.\]

For small \(\eta\), \(K_0\approx\eta/\ln2\). The rate therefore falls exponentially with distance. This is a benchmark for that channel and resource model; it is not an unassisted qubit capacity or a rate per second.

Consider two independently attempted segments. In a toy model, each segment succeeds with probability \(p\) per round, and a memory stores the first success while the second segment continues. Let the waiting times \(T_1,T_2\) be independent geometric random variables, counted from one. Then

\[\mathbb E[\max(T_1,T_2)]=\mathbb E[T_1]+\mathbb E[T_2]-\mathbb E[\min(T_1,T_2)] =\frac2p-\frac1{2p-p^2}.\]

The minimum has success probability \(1-(1-p)^2\) per round. For \(p\ll1\), the expected completion time is approximately \(3/(2p)\) rounds. Requiring both links to succeed simultaneously instead gives mean \(1/p^2\). At \(p=0.1\), these are about 14.74 and 100 rounds.

Storage creates its own cost. If coherence decays as \(e^{-t/T_2}\), a stored link loses coherence while waiting. The completion-time improvement alone does not establish an improved usable-entanglement or secret-key rate.

What is established

The repeaterless capacity bound gives a precise rate–loss benchmark. The quantum repeater review develops architectures that use additional nodes and resources to change the trade-off. Exceeding the benchmark requires comparing the same channel uses and assistance model, with repeater resources reported separately.

Converting modes into a rate per second also requires clock rate, multiplexing, coupling and detection efficiencies, classical signalling time, and protocol success probabilities. A heralded event is not automatically a high-fidelity delivered pair.

What remains unresolved

Which combination of memory lifetime, optical interfaces, swapping fidelity, and multiplexing yields a scalable advantage for a target network task? Memory cutoffs, asynchronous scheduling, and error correction interact. Optimizing each component in isolation can choose a poor end-to-end protocol.

A concrete next investigation

Reproduce: sample the two geometric waiting times and recover the exact completion-time mean. Keep attempted modes and elapsed rounds as separate counters.

Check: include decoherence during \(|T_1-T_2|\) waiting rounds and report the delivered fidelity distribution, not only the mean delivery rate.

Extend: optimize a memory cutoff or multiplexing schedule under a fixed hardware budget. A useful result improves a specified end-to-end metric and states when it exceeds the appropriate direct-link benchmark.
